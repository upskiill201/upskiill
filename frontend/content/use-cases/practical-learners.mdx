## What makes a learner "practical"?

You would rather build something small today than read about something big. You remember what you did far better than what you watched. And you judge a course by one question: *can I use this now?*

## How is Teyro built for that?

| What you want | How Teyro does it |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Do, don't watch** | Every lesson has an [Apply step](/features/immediate-practice) with instant feedback |
| **Small wins, often** | Lessons take a few minutes; a daily goal of about 5 to 20 |
| **Know what's next** | A [learning path](/features/guided-roadmap) picks the next lesson for you |
| **Keep going** | [Streaks](/features/streaks-and-freeze), leagues and quests |

## What does a week look like?

Five to fifteen minutes a day on your phone, one idea per lesson, each one used before you move on. At the weekend, take what you learned and build something with it — the lessons give you the pieces, a project puts them together.

## What Teyro won't do

It won't hand you a certificate (not yet), and it isn't a bootcamp. It gives you the daily practice that most people skip — the part that actually builds the skill. For the bigger picture, see [how to teach yourself to code](/blog/how-to-teach-yourself-to-code).
