The new phone thrilled you for a week. By month two it was just a phone.

## Direct answer

Hedonic adaptation is the way we get used to new things so the happiness they bring fades. A new car, outfit or gadget feels exciting, then becomes normal. Knowing this helps you spend on what lasts: experiences, relationships and things you use often.

## Why it matters for money

If every purchase fades, spending more does not keep you happier. You can end up on a treadmill, buying again to get the same buzz.

## How to slow it down

1. **Choose experiences or tools you use often.**
2. **Space out upgrades.**
3. **Practise gratitude** for what you already have.
4. **Share purchases** with others, such as a trip or a meal.
5. **Add variety,** rather than more of the same.

## A useful question

"How often will I use this, and will I still care in three months?"
