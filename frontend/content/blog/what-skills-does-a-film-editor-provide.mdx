## 1. Narrative Pacing: The Heartbeat of Storytelling

A great editor can make a slow conversation feel electrifying or make an action scene easy to follow:

* **Holding on the Reaction:** Often, the emotional weight of a scene is not on the person speaking, but on the silent reaction of the listener.
* **The Invisible Cut:** Cutting on action (e.g., as a character opens a door) hides the transition from the viewer's conscious perception.

---

## 2. Advanced Audio Design: 50% of the Movie Experience

Amateur video is defined by bad audio; professional cinema is defined by rich soundscapes:

1. **J-Cuts and L-Cuts:** Having the audio of the next shot begin 1 second before the visual cut (J-Cut) creates natural conversational flow.
2. **Room Tone & Ambient Foley:** Laying down background room tone prevents awkward digital silence between spoken lines.
3. **Dialogue EQ and Compression:** Ensuring voices remain crisp and intelligible over orchestral music.

---

## 3. Eye-Trace and the 180-Degree Rule

* **The 180-Degree Line:** Maintaining an imaginary spatial axis between two characters so they consistently look at each other across cuts.
* **Eye-Trace Matching:** Positioning the focal point of the incoming shot in the exact quadrant where the viewer's eyes were resting in the previous shot.
