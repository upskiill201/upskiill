Student money often arrives in lumps: a loan payment, an allowance on the first of the month, a summer job. It feels like a lot on day one and nothing by week six. Here is **how to budget as a student** so the money lasts the whole term.

## Direct answer: how to budget as a student

Add up all the money you will have for the term, subtract fixed costs (rent, fees, bills), and divide what remains by the number of weeks. That is your weekly spending money. Pay yourself that amount each week, keep a small buffer, and track what you spend so you can adjust.

## Step 1: Add up everything coming in

Include loans or grants, family support, wages from a job, and any scholarship. Count only what you are sure to receive.

## Step 2: Subtract the fixed costs

Rent, utilities, tuition or fees not already paid, phone, transport passes, insurance. These come off first.

## Step 3: Split the rest into weeks

Take what is left and divide by the number of weeks in the term.

**Example:** you receive $3,200 for a 16-week term.

- Fixed costs for the term (rent, phone, transport pass): $2,000
- Remaining: $3,200 − $2,000 = $1,200
- Keep a $160 buffer for surprises → $1,040
- Weekly spending money: $1,040 ÷ 16 = **$65 a week**

That $65 covers food, going out, small purchases and everything else. If you spend $80 in week one, you will see you must cut back later, instead of discovering it in week ten.

## Step 4: Use one pot for weekly money

Move each week's amount into a separate account, card or cash envelope. When it is gone, it is gone. This one habit prevents most end-of-term panics.

## Step 5: Cut the leaks that matter

Student budgets leak in the same places:

- Food delivery and takeaway. Cooking a few meals in bulk saves a lot over a term.
- Subscriptions you forgot, especially free trials that start billing.
- Small impulse purchases and bank fees.
- Group costs. Agree amounts up front.

## Step 6: Plan for the lumpy costs

Textbooks, a laptop repair, a trip home, exam resit fees. Estimate them for the term and subtract them before dividing into weeks.

## Step 7: Look ahead to the next term

Earn, borrow and save with the next term in mind. If you have money left, hold it in the buffer. It is easier to start a term with a cushion.

## If you are in your 20s and just starting out

The same plan works when you leave education: replace "term" with "month" and "loan" with "salary". ## Tools

A notes app, a basic [monthly budget template](/blog/monthly-budget-template), or a bank's built-in spending view is enough. You do not need a paid app to build the habit.

## The bottom line

Students do not need to be perfect with money. They need a weekly number and a way to stick to it. Work yours out with the formula above, and review it every Sunday.
