Becoming a manager changes your job overnight. You are no longer judged on your own work, but on how well your team does. Here are **new manager tips** for the first day, the first week and the first 90 days.

## Direct answer

In your first 90 days as a new manager: listen and learn before you change anything, meet each person one on one, set clear expectations and goals, start regular one-on-ones, deliver one or two small visible wins, and ask for feedback on how you are doing. Avoid the traps of doing the work yourself and changing everything at once.

## Before you start

- Ask your own manager what success looks like in the first 90 days.
- Find out how your team's work is judged.
- Write down what you want to learn about each person.

## Your first day

- **Introduce yourself briefly.** Who you are, what you value and that you want to listen.
- **Do not announce changes.** You do not know enough yet.
- **Ask questions.** "What is working well? What is frustrating?"
- **Remember names and details.** It signals that you care.

## Your first week

1. **Meet each person one on one**, even for 20 minutes. Ask about their work, what they want to learn and how they like feedback.
2. **Learn the main processes** and where they slow people down.
3. **Meet your key stakeholders:** people who rely on your team.
4. **Write down what you hear**, patterns matter more than single comments.

## Your first month

- Share a simple picture of the team's goals.
- Start **regular one-on-ones**, using the person's agenda first.
- Check roles are clear: who owns what.
- Fix one or two small, visible irritations, such as a broken process or an unclear form. Quick wins earn trust.

## Months two and three

- Set clear goals for the team and for each person.
- Practise **delegation:** hand over outcomes, not only tasks.
- Give specific feedback early and often. See [how to be a good leader](/blog/how-to-be-a-good-leader).
- Have the hard conversation you have been avoiding, privately and kindly.
- Ask your team: "What should I start, stop and continue?"

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | What to do instead |
| :--- | :--- |
| Doing the work yourself | Delegate outcomes and coach |
| Changing too much too fast | Learn first, change one thing at a time |
| Avoiding hard conversations | Address issues early and privately |
| Being everyone's friend | Be friendly and fair, clear on standards |
| Never asking for feedback | Ask once a month |

## If you manage former peers

Be open about the change, keep confidences, treat everyone the same and talk to each person early. Expect some awkwardness at first.

## Tools to use

- One-on-one notes (a simple shared document works)
- A weekly goal list for the team
- A "decisions made" log so people know what was decided and why

## The bottom line

New managers succeed by listening first, being clear, being consistent and asking for feedback.
