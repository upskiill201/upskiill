How long does it take to learn coding? The honest answer is "it depends on what you mean by learn." Writing your first working program takes an afternoon. Building a small app takes a few months. Getting hired as a developer takes most self-taught learners around a year. The single biggest variable is not talent. It is how many hours of hands-on practice you put in, and whether you keep practising. That is why [Teyro](/onboarding/0) focuses on the one thing that controls your timeline: a daily coding habit you do not break.

This guide gives realistic timelines for every goal, answers "is 1 month enough?", and shows how many hours actually make a difference.

---

## Direct Answer: How Long Does It Take to Learn Coding?

With 30 to 60 minutes of daily practice, it takes about **2 to 3 months to learn coding basics**, **around 6 months to build your own small apps**, and **9 to 18 months to become job-ready** as a self-taught developer. Full-time learners can compress this to roughly 3 to 9 months. Total hands-on hours matter more than calendar time.

| Goal | Hands-On Hours | 15 min/day | 1 hr/day | Full-time (6 hr/day) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Write your first program | 1–2 | Day 1 | Day 1 | Day 1 |
| Understand the basics | 40–60 | 5–8 months | 6–8 weeks | 1–2 weeks |
| Build small apps alone | 150–250 | 2+ years | 5–8 months | 1–2 months |
| First freelance income | 250–400 | not realistic | 8–13 months | 2–3 months |
| Junior developer job-ready | 600–1,000+ | not realistic | 18–30 months | 4–9 months |

Fifteen minutes a day is perfect for building the habit and learning fundamentals, but serious career goals need more hours once the habit is locked in. Many learners start at 15 minutes and naturally grow to an hour as coding becomes enjoyable.

## Is 1 Month Enough to Learn Coding?

One month is enough to learn **the basics**, not to become a developer.

In 30 days of consistent daily practice (around 30 to 60 minutes), you can realistically:

- Understand variables, data types, conditionals and loops
- Write functions and use lists and dictionaries
- Build tiny programs: a calculator, number-guessing game, unit converter
- Read simple code written by others and roughly understand it

In one month you will **not** be able to:

- Build a complete web app with users and a database
- Pass a developer job interview
- Work comfortably in a real team codebase

Month one is about building the foundation and the habit. People who treat it as "I should be employable by now" often quit in disappointment. People who treat it as "I now know I can do this" keep going.

## How Many Hours of Coding Is Enough?

### Per Day

| Daily Time | What It's Good For |
| :--- | :--- |
| **15 minutes** | Building the habit, learning concepts, staying consistent on busy days |
| **30–60 minutes** | Steady progress; the sweet spot for most part-time learners |
| **1–3 hours** | Fast progress; time for projects and debugging |
| **4+ hours** | Bootcamp pace; needs breaks, sleep and variety to avoid burnout |

Research on learning consistently favours **spaced practice**: shorter sessions spread across days beat one long cram session. Your brain consolidates new skills during sleep, so 7 sessions of 30 minutes will usually beat one 3.5-hour session. We explain the science in our guide to [spaced repetition](/blog/spaced-repetition-how-to-study-smarter).

### In Total

A useful mental model: **the first 20 hours** of deliberate practice take you from clueless to functional (you can write simple programs). **The next 200 hours** make you capable (you can build things). **The next 500+ hours** make you employable.

What counts as an hour? Only **hands-on** time: writing code, running it, fixing it. Watching a 10-hour video course counts for far less than you would hope.

## How Long to Learn Coding to Get a Job?

For self-taught learners practicing part-time, **9 to 18 months** is typical. The timeline depends on:

1. **Your target role.** Front-end web and junior QA roles tend to be more accessible than backend, data engineering or machine learning roles.
2. **Your portfolio.** Employers want to see 3 to 5 real projects, not certificates.
3. **Your local and remote job market.** Entry-level competition varies a lot by country and year.
4. **Your network.** Referrals and community involvement shorten job searches dramatically.

Can I learn coding on my own and get a job? Yes, many developers do. But plan for the job search itself to take 2 to 6 months after you are skilled enough. Read [self-taught skills that actually get you hired](/blog/self-taught-skills-that-actually-get-you-hired) for what to prioritise.

## How Long Does It Take to Learn Coding to Make Money?

You do not need a full-time job to earn from coding. Typical first income paths, roughly in order of accessibility:

| Income Path | Skills Needed | Typical Time to First Earning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Automating tasks at your current job | Python basics, spreadsheets | 2–4 months |
| Simple websites for local businesses | HTML, CSS, basic JavaScript | 4–8 months |
| Small freelance fixes and scripts | One language well, debugging | 6–12 months |
| Junior developer salary | Portfolio, Git, a framework | 9–18 months |

The fastest route to earning is often **not** changing jobs. It is using code to become dramatically more productive at the job you already have, which frequently leads to a raise or an internal move. More ideas in [skills to learn to make money on the side](/blog/skills-to-learn-to-make-money-on-the-side).

## Can I Learn to Code in 2 Years?

Two years is plenty. In fact, two years of consistent part-time practice (about an hour a day) adds up to roughly 700 hours, which is enough to become job-ready with room to spare. By the two-year mark, a dedicated learner can typically:

- Work confidently in one main language and read two or three others
- Build full apps with a framework, a database and user accounts
- Use Git, write tests and deploy projects online
- Show a portfolio of real projects, ideally with real users

The risk with a two-year goal is not that it is too short. It is that "two years" feels far away, which makes skipping today feel harmless. Big timelines are built from small daily reps.

## 5 Things That Make Learning Coding Faster

1. **Code every day.** Consistency beats intensity. A 100-day streak of 30 minutes beats five weekend marathons.
2. **Build projects early.** Start small projects around week 3. Projects expose gaps that courses hide.
3. **Stick to one language.** Language hopping resets your progress.
4. **Get feedback.** Share code in communities, ask for reviews, or pair with a friend.
5. **Use active recall.** Try to write code from memory before looking it up. Struggle is where learning happens.

## The Bottom Line

Learning the basics of coding takes a few months, building real things takes about six, and becoming job-ready takes most part-time learners 9 to 18 months. None of those timelines start until you do, and none of them finish if you stop. The fastest learners are not the smartest. They are the ones who never break the chain.
