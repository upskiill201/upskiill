Udemy and Skillshare dominate search results for "sell online courses," but they're not automatically the right fit for every creator — Udemy's marketplace pricing dynamics and revenue splits frustrate many experienced creators, and Skillshare's subscription model doesn't suit every content type. This guide covers the alternatives worth evaluating and what actually differs between them, including where [Teyro's](/onboarding/0) Creator Studio fits.

## Direct Answer: Best Alternatives to Skillshare and Udemy

The strongest alternatives to Skillshare and Udemy for selling courses are Teachable and Kajabi (self-branded platforms with full pricing control), Thinkific (similar self-hosted approach), and gamified platforms like Teyro, which combine course publishing with built-in learner engagement mechanics — streaks, XP, progress tracking — that plain video-hosting platforms don't provide.

## Why Creators Look Beyond Udemy and Skillshare

Udemy's marketplace model relies heavily on aggressive promotional discounting, which drives volume but often at the cost of the creator's actual take per sale — a $200 course frequently sells for $15-20 during a promotion, with revenue splits shifting further against the creator depending on how the sale originated. Skillshare's subscription model pays creators based on minutes watched across the platform's subscriber pool, which can produce unpredictable and sometimes low per-student economics for niche content.

Neither platform provides structural support for the completion problem covered in [why students don't finish my course](/blog/why-students-dont-finish-my-course) — both are fundamentally video-hosting marketplaces, leaving engagement and completion entirely up to the creator's content and the learner's personal follow-through.

## Self-Hosted Platforms: Teachable, Kajabi, Thinkific

These platforms give creators their own branded course site with full control over pricing, bundling, and marketing, typically for a monthly platform fee rather than a revenue cut. This suits established creators with an existing audience who can drive their own traffic, since these platforms don't provide marketplace discovery the way Udemy or Skillshare do. They also don't include gamification or engagement mechanics by default — completion remains a creator-side responsibility.
