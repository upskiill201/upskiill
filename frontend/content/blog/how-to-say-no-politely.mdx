Saying no is hard because we want to be liked. The skill is to be clear and kind at the same time.

## Direct answer

To say no politely, thank the person, say no clearly without a long excuse, and offer an alternative only if you really want to. Keep your tone warm and your answer short. If they push, repeat your answer calmly. A clear no early is kinder than a vague yes you later regret.

## The formula

1. **Thank:** "Thanks for thinking of me."
2. **No:** "I can't do that."
3. **Reason (optional, short):** "My plate's full this week."
4. **Alternative (optional):** "I could help next month."

## Phrases that work

### At work
- "I'd like to help, but I can't take this on without dropping something. Which should I put first?"
- "That doesn't fit my priorities this week. Can we talk about timing?"
- "I'm not the right person for this. Have you asked Sam?"

### Professional and customers
- "Thank you for the offer. We're not able to do that, but here's what we can do."
- "That's outside what we provide. I can suggest another option."

### In a text
- "Thanks so much for the invite! I can't make it, have a great time."
- "I appreciate you asking, but I'll pass this time."

### Friends and family
- "I love you, and I can't lend money right now."
- "I'm not up for it tonight. Let's do something next week."

## Holding your ground

- Keep your voice calm and steady.
- Repeat: "I understand, and the answer is still no."
- Don't apologise repeatedly.
- Don't invent excuses you'll have to keep up.

## When saying no is part of a negotiation

Saying no to an unreasonable request is a negotiation skill. Know your limits, your alternatives and what you can offer instead. See [negotiation tactics and strategies](/blog/negotiation-tactics-and-strategies).
