Almost every job post asks for "leadership", and almost every CV says "strong leader". Neither tells the reader anything. Here is how to show leadership skills on a resume with evidence, including when you have never had a manager title.

## Direct answer

Do not write "good leader". Write what you led, how many people, and what improved. Use the pattern: **action verb + what you did + result.** If you have no title, use projects, training, volunteering or covering for a manager.

## The formula

> **Verb + what you led or changed + measurable result**

- Weak: "Responsible for a team."
- Better: "Led a team of five to deliver the Q3 product launch two weeks early."

If you do not have a number, use a clear outcome: "so new staff could work alone within a week".

## Examples by situation

### Team lead or manager

- Coached four team members through quarterly goals, and two were promoted within the year.
- Cut weekly meeting time by 30 minutes by agreeing a clear agenda and owners.
- Delegated the monthly report to a junior colleague with a checklist, freeing six hours a month.

### No title, still leading

- Organised a cross-team project to fix a recurring delay and presented the plan to managers.
- Trained three new colleagues on the ordering system.
- Volunteered as shift lead during a staff shortage and kept service times steady.

### Student or early career

- Led a six-person group project, set weekly goals and presented the final result.
- Coordinated a club event for 80 attendees, managing a small budget.
- Mentored first-year students on study skills.

Replace the numbers with your own. Only claim what is true, because interviewers will ask.

## Action verbs

Led, coordinated, coached, mentored, delegated, organised, launched, improved, resolved, trained, guided, set up, negotiated.

## Where to put it

1. **Summary:** one line, such as "Team lead with five years of experience leading small teams."
2. **Experience bullets:** the main evidence.
3. **Skills:** specific abilities (coaching, planning, conflict resolution), not just "leadership".

## For a performance review

The same formula works: "Led the onboarding redesign, which cut ramp-up time by two weeks." Write results, not duties.
