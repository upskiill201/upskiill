Which is the best AI for a beginner? There are now dozens of AI assistants competing for your attention, and every week brings a new "ChatGPT killer" headline. The good news for beginners: the top AI assistants are all very capable, most have generous free tiers, and the skills you build using one transfer to the others. The tool matters less than learning to use it well, and that is a skill [Teyro](/onboarding/0) helps you build in 15-minute daily lessons.

Here is an honest, beginner-focused comparison of the major AI assistants, which ones are truly free, and how to get started.

---

## Direct Answer: The Best AI for Beginners

The best AI for most beginners is **ChatGPT, Claude or Google Gemini**, all of which offer capable free tiers. **ChatGPT** is the most versatile and widely used, **Claude** excels at writing and long documents, and **Gemini** fits naturally if you use Google apps. For research with cited sources, use **Perplexity**. For a fully free, unlimited option, run an **open-weight model** locally.

| AI Assistant | Made By | Best For Beginners Who... | Free Tier |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **ChatGPT** | OpenAI | Want an all-rounder with the largest community | Yes, with limits |
| **Claude** | Anthropic | Write a lot, or work with long documents and PDFs | Yes, with limits |
| **Gemini** | Google | Live in Gmail, Docs and Google Drive | Yes, with limits |
| **Microsoft Copilot** | Microsoft | Use Windows, Word, Excel and Outlook | Yes, with limits |
| **Perplexity** | Perplexity AI | Research topics and want sources for every answer | Yes, with limits |
| **Open-weight models** (Llama, Gemma, Mistral, Qwen, DeepSeek) | Various | Want fully free, private AI on their own computer | Fully free (needs a capable computer) |

AI products change quickly, so features and limits shift month to month. The categories above have stayed stable.

---

## ChatGPT: The Versatile All-Rounder

ChatGPT is the AI most people have heard of, and for good reason. It handles writing, brainstorming, explanations, coding help, image understanding and voice conversations in one place.

**Strengths for beginners:**

- Huge community, so tutorials and prompt examples are everywhere
- Handles almost any everyday task reasonably well
- Voice mode makes it feel natural to use

**Watch out for:** confident mistakes on facts and numbers. Always verify important information.

---

## Claude: Best for Writing and Long Documents

Claude, made by Anthropic, is often preferred for writing quality, careful reasoning and working with long documents. You can upload a long PDF or report and ask detailed questions about it.

**Strengths for beginners:**

- Natural, less generic writing style
- Strong at summarising and analysing long files
- Good at explaining concepts step by step

**Watch out for:** free-tier usage limits can be reached quickly during heavy use.

---

## Gemini: Best for Google Users

Google's Gemini integrates with Gmail, Docs, Drive and other Google services, which is useful if your work already lives there.

**Strengths for beginners:**

- Works inside tools you already use
- Strong multimodal features (images, documents, video)
- Connects well with Google Search

---

## Microsoft Copilot: Best for Microsoft 365 Users

Copilot is built into Windows, Edge and Microsoft 365 apps. If your job revolves around Word, Excel, PowerPoint and Outlook, it is the most convenient way to start using AI at work, though the deepest Office features usually require a paid plan.

---

## Perplexity: Best for Research

Perplexity is an AI answer engine that searches the web and shows its sources. For beginners researching a topic (like "what jobs will AI replace by 2030?"), seeing sources makes it easier to verify claims.

---

## Is ChatGPT Still the Best AI?

ChatGPT is still one of the best AI assistants and the most widely used, but it is **no longer the undisputed leader in every area**. The top AI labs release new models frequently, and rankings on benchmarks and head-to-head comparisons trade places regularly.

A practical way to think about it:

| Task | Strong Choices |
| :--- | :--- |
| General everyday help | ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini |
| Writing and editing | Claude, ChatGPT |
| Long PDFs and reports | Claude, Gemini |
| Research with sources | Perplexity |
| Google Workspace | Gemini |
| Microsoft 365 | Copilot |
| Coding help | Claude, ChatGPT |
| Privacy and zero cost | Local open-weight models |

## Which AI Is Better Than ChatGPT?

No single AI beats ChatGPT at everything. But for specific jobs, others are often better. The only reliable way to know is to test them yourself:

1. Pick a real task you do often (for example, "summarise this article into five bullet points for my manager").
2. Give **the exact same prompt** to ChatGPT and one alternative.
3. Compare accuracy, tone and usefulness.
4. Repeat with two more tasks.

In twenty minutes you will know which AI suits you better than any ranking can tell you.

## Which AI Is 100% Free?

This depends on what "free" means:

### Free Tiers (Free, but Limited)

ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot and Perplexity all offer free tiers. They are genuinely useful, but they limit how much you can use them, restrict access to the most powerful models or features, and offer paid upgrades, typically around $20 a month for individual plans.

### Truly 100% Free: Open-Weight Models

Open-weight models, such as **Meta's Llama, Google's Gemma, Mistral, Alibaba's Qwen and DeepSeek**, can be downloaded and run on your own computer for free, with no usage limits and no data leaving your device. Beginner-friendly apps like **Ollama** and **LM Studio** make this surprisingly easy.

The trade-offs: you need a reasonably powerful computer (more memory is better), smaller local models are usually less capable than the top cloud assistants, and setup takes a little effort. It is a great learning project once you are comfortable with the basics.

## Where Do I Begin With AI?

1. **Pick one assistant** from the table above. If unsure, start with ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini.
2. **Use it daily for 2 weeks** on real tasks: emails, summaries, explanations, planning.
3. **Learn prompt basics:** give context, a clear goal, the audience and the format you want.
4. **Always verify** facts, statistics and quotes. AI tools can invent convincing but false information.
5. **Try a second tool** with the same prompts to understand their differences.
6. **Take a free beginner course** to understand how AI works. See [can I learn AI for free?](/blog/can-i-learn-ai-for-free)

## A Beginner Prompt Template That Works in Any AI

```
Role:     You are a patient tutor.
Context:  I am a complete beginner learning about [topic].
Task:     Explain [concept] in simple terms.
Format:   Use 3 short paragraphs and one everyday analogy.
Check:    End with 2 quiz questions to test my understanding.
```

This works in ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot and local models, which is exactly why prompting skill matters more than which tool you pick. If you want AI to help you learn skills (not just answer questions), see [ChatGPT vs an app for learning a skill](/blog/chatgpt-vs-app-for-learning-a-skill).
