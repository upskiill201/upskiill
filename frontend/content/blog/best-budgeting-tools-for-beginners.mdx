There are hundreds of budgeting apps, and picking one can feel like a way to avoid starting. The truth: **the best budgeting tool is the one you open every week.** Here is how to choose among the main types.

## Direct answer: which tool for a beginner

Start with the simplest tool you will actually use: a spreadsheet or a notes page. If you prefer automation, try your bank's built-in spending view or a budgeting app, and check its current price before you commit. Switch only if you stop using it.

## The main types

| Type | Good for | Watch out for |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Paper or notes page | Total beginners, tight budgets, no setup | You must update it yourself |
| Spreadsheet (Excel or Google Sheets) | Control, free, sharing with a partner | Needs a little setup |
| Bank app spending view | Automatic categories, no extra account | Limited planning features |
| Budgeting app | Importing transactions, reminders | Subscriptions, privacy, bank connections |
| Envelope or sub-account system | Stopping overspending in a category | Needs several accounts or cash |

## Spreadsheet or notes page

Free, flexible and yours. Copy our [monthly budget template](/blog/monthly-budget-template) into Excel or Google Sheets, or write the same rows in a notes app. Because you enter each number, you pay attention to it, which is useful for learning.

## Your bank's own tools

Many banks show spending by category and let you set limits. It costs nothing, needs no extra login, and is a good first step for tracking.

## Budgeting apps

Apps can import transactions, categorise them and remind you. Before you choose, check:

- **Price and trial:** what does it cost after the free period, and how do you cancel?
- **Bank connections:** does it work with your bank in your country?
- **Privacy:** what data does it keep and share?
- **Method:** does it fit how you want to budget, such as the [50/30/20 rule](/blog/50-30-20-rule-budget) or zero-based?

Prices and features change often, so check them on the app's own page.

## Envelope-style tools

If you overspend in one category, such as eating out, split your money into pots: cash envelopes, or separate sub-accounts for each category. When it is empty, you stop.

## How to choose in three questions

1. **Do I want to enter numbers or have them imported?** Entering teaches you; importing saves time.
2. **Do I budget alone or with a partner?** Shared sheets help couples ([how to budget as a couple or family](/blog/how-to-budget-as-a-couple-or-family)).
3. **Will I pay?** If you are on a small income, free tools are enough ([how to budget on a low income](/blog/how-to-budget-on-a-low-income)).

## Why the tool matters less than the habit

A tool records your money. It does not decide where it goes. The habits are tracking, putting needs first, giving every unit a job and reviewing weekly. Learn those first, and any tool works. Our guide to [how to budget for beginners](/blog/how-to-budget-for-beginners) covers the steps.
