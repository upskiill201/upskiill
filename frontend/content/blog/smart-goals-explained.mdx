A goal you cannot check is a wish.

## Direct answer

A SMART goal is Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant and Time-bound. It states exactly what will be done, how success is measured, why it matters and by when. Turn "get better at sales" into "increase my monthly closed deals from 5 to 8 by the end of the quarter".

## The five parts

| Letter | Question |
| :--- | :--- |
| Specific | what exactly will I do? |
| Measurable | how will I know it worked? |
| Achievable | is it realistic with my resources? |
| Relevant | does it matter to my role or aims? |
| Time-bound | by when? |

## Examples

- **Work:** "Reduce average response time to customer emails from 24 to 12 hours within three months."
- **Learning:** "Complete 40 lessons of a SQL course by 31 March."
- **Career:** "Have coffee with five people in the data team by the end of June."

## Template

"I will [action] so that [reason], measured by [metric], by [date]."

## Common mistakes

- Vague words like "improve" with no number.
- Too many goals at once.
- No review date.
