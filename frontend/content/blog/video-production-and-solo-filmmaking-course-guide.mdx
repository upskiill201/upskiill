## How to Make a Short Film Completely by Yourself (The Solo Blueprint)

Creating a short film without a large crew forces you to embrace simplicity and discipline:

```
[The Solo Filmmaking Framework]
                       │
  ┌────────────────────┼────────────────────┐
  │                    │                    │
  ▼                    ▼                    ▼
[1. The Single Location] [2. Natural Light]  [3. Multi-Layer Audio]
  • Master bedroom,      • Large window bounce • Wireless mic on actor
   coffee shop, car       • 1 LED hair light   • Rich Foley & room tone
  │                    │                    │
  ▼                    ▼                    ▼
[4. Tripod & Slider]   [5. Shot List Plan]  [6. Post-Color Mood]
  • Locked-off framing   • Wide, Medium, Tight• Cinematic 2.39:1 crop
  • Parallax moves       • Insert cutaways    • Moody teal/warm grade
```

### 1. The 1-Location, 2-Actor Script Rule
Avoid scripts requiring multiple complex locations, car chases, or large crowds. The most powerful indie films revolve around intimate interpersonal conflicts, psychological tension, or single-room mysteries.

### 2. The 3-Shot Framing Rule (Wide, Medium, Close-Up)
For every dialogue beat, record three complementary angles:
- **Master Wide Shot:** Establishes spatial context and character body language.
- **Medium Over-the-Shoulder (OTS):** Connects the two speaking characters.
- **Tight Close-Up:** Emphasizes micro-expressions, tears, and emotional intensity.

## The Ultimate Color Grading Pipeline (Correction vs Grading)

Color is the subconscious language of cinema. Follow this sequential color workflow in DaVinci Resolve or Premiere Pro:

```
[Raw Log / Flat Footage]
           │
           ▼
[Step 1: Technical Normalization] ───> CST (Color Space Transform) to Rec.709
           │
           ▼
[Step 2: Primary Exposure & Balance] -> Fix black lift, mid gamma & highlight gain
           │
           ▼
[Step 3: Shot-to-Shot Matching]    ──> Ensure skin tones match across all camera angles
           │
           ▼
[Step 4: Creative Color Grading]   ──> Apply cinematic palette (e.g., Teal & Orange)
           │
           ▼
[Step 5: Film Grain & Textures]    ──> Layer 35mm film grain and subtle halation
```

### 1. Primary Exposure Balancing
Look at your **Waveform Monitor**:
- Set deepest shadow blacks to `0 - 10 IRE` (avoid crushing detail into pitch black).
- Set specular highlights (skies, lightbulbs) just below `100 IRE` to prevent clipping.

### 2. Skin Tone Vector Alignment
Ensure human skin highlights sit precisely on the **Skin Tone Line** (approximately 75 degrees on the vectorscope), regardless of race or ethnicity, for natural and flattering skin rendering.
