Yes, you can legally build, publish, and sell software created using AI tools. Major model providers (OpenAI, Anthropic, Cursor) explicitly assign all commercial ownership rights to the user. While raw, untouched AI code cannot be copyrighted, human developers own the composite application architecture, branding, database schemas, and proprietary business logic.

## Who Owns an App Created by AI? (Copyright vs Commercial Rights)

To understand software ownership, you must distinguish between **commercial rights** and **statutory copyright**:

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│               COMMERCIAL RIGHTS VS STATUTORY COPYRIGHT                 │
├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│  1. COMMERCIAL RIGHTS (Contract Law):                                  │
│     The terms of service between you and OpenAI/Anthropic. They grant   │
│     you 100% rights to sell, license, and monetize the code output.    │
│                                                                        │
│  2. COPYRIGHT PROTECTION (Intellectual Property Law):                  │
│     The government's legal monopoly protecting your work from copycats.│
│     The US Copyright Office requires HUMAN AUTHORSHIP to grant a       │
│     registered copyright.                                              │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### The US Copyright Office Ruling on AI Code
In landmark guidance issued by the US Copyright Office, the government ruled that **pure, unedited AI output cannot be registered for copyright** because copyright law protects only the fruits of human creative labor.

However, this does **not** mean your app is unprotected:
* **The Compilation Defense:** If a human selects, arranges, connects, and modifies AI-generated modules into a coherent system (such as pairing a Next.js frontend with a Supabase database and custom Stripe webhooks), the **composite work** is fully eligible for copyright protection.
* **Proprietary Assets:** Your unique brand name, logo, custom user interface design, original database schema, and domain name are 100% your exclusive intellectual property.

### The Real Risk: Open-Source License Contamination (GPL Viral Code)
The only legitimate copyright danger for software developers is **license contamination**. If an AI model reproduces a complex 200-line algorithmic function verbatim from a **GPL (General Public License)** repository, your commercial application could technically be bound by open-source copyleft terms.

#### How to Protect Yourself:
* Enable **"Block suggestions matching public code"** in GitHub Copilot and Cursor settings.
* Run open-source dependency scanners (like Snyk or FOSSA) on your codebase before commercial deployment.
* Write original architecture prompts and combine multiple small modules rather than asking AI to copy existing proprietary applications.

## App Store Guidelines: Publishing AI Apps on Apple & Google

Can you publish an app built with AI on the iOS App Store and Google Play Store? **Yes.** Hundreds of thousands of apps built with AI tools are live in both stores today.

However, Apple and Google enforce strict guidelines for apps that *utilize* generative AI features:

1. **User Content Reporting & Moderation (Apple Guideline 1.2):** If your app generates text or images for end-users, you must include a built-in "Report" button, content filtering guardrails, and terms of service prohibiting hate speech or harmful output.
2. **Preventing Generic "Spam" Apps (Apple Guideline 4.3):** Apple rejects apps that are low-effort clones of existing products. If you build a basic ChatGPT wrapper that just displays a chat box, Apple will reject it as a "repetitive utility." Your app must provide unique value, custom UI, or specific domain functionality.
3. **Google Play AI Disclosures:** Google requires developers to complete a declaration form confirming whether their app features user-facing generative AI tools.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Is it legal to build and sell an app made with AI?
Yes, it is 100% legal to build and sell software created with AI assistance. Major AI providers (including OpenAI, Anthropic, and GitHub Copilot) grant users full commercial ownership of all generated code outputs in their terms of service.

### Who legally owns code and applications generated by AI?
Under current US Copyright Office rulings, raw AI-generated content cannot be copyrighted because copyright requires human authorship. However, the human prompt engineer owns the overarching application, original architecture, UI arrangement, and customized codebase.

### Can I publish an app made with AI on the Apple App Store or Google Play?
Yes. Both Apple and Google permit AI-generated applications as long as they comply with standard developer guidelines regarding user privacy, content moderation, stability, and anti-spam rules.

### Do I need an LLC to sell a software app?
While you can legally sell software as a sole proprietor, forming an LLC (Limited Liability Company) is strongly recommended. An LLC protects your personal assets from commercial debt, software liability, and copyright infringement claims.
