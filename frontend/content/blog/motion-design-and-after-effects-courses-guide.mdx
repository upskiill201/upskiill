## The 4 Core Principles of Motion Design (Beyond Basic Keyframing)

Amateur motion design looks linear, robotic, and stiff. Professional motion design feels organic, weighted, and punchy. Master these four foundational principles:

```
[The 4 Core Pillars of Professional Motion Design]
                       │
  ┌────────────────────┼────────────────────┐
  │                    │                    │
  ▼                    ▼                    ▼
[1. The Speed Graph]   [2. Anticipation & Overshoot] [3. Stagger & Delay]
  • Easy Ease (F9)       • Pulling back before jump   • Cascading layer offsets
  • Bezier curves (80%)  • Bouncing past target (105%)• Overlapping follow-through
  │                    │                    │
  ▼                    ▼                    ▼
[4. Kinetic Typography] ───────────────────> Rhythmic scale, tracking & text reveals
```

### 1. The Value Graph vs Speed Graph
Never leave keyframes on linear interpolation. Select your keyframes, press `F9` (**Easy Ease**), and open the **Speed Graph Editor**. Pull the influence handle to `70% - 85%` so elements accelerate rapidly and decelerate smoothly into their final rest position.

### 2. Overshoot and Elastic Bounce
When an object scales into frame, animate it scaling from $0\%$ to $108\%$, then bouncing back to $98\%$, before settling at $100\%$. This simulates real-world physical inertia and gives animations personality.

### 3. Staggering Layers (The Waterfall Effect)
When animating multiple list items, icons, or text lines, offset each layer by 2 to 4 frames on the timeline rather than letting them appear simultaneously.
