Many small sellers pick a price by feel, and end up busy but broke. A simple method fixes that.

## Direct answer

To price a product, work out the full cost of each unit (materials, time, packaging, fees, delivery and a share of overheads), decide the profit margin you need, check what similar products and your customers will pay, then set a price and test it. Price to cover costs and reflect the value you deliver, not just to match competitors.

## Step 1: Find your real cost

| Cost | Examples |
| :--- | :--- |
| Direct | Materials, packaging, delivery |
| Your time | Hours × a fair hourly rate |
| Fees | Payment, platform, marketplace |
| Overheads | Rent, tools, marketing, spread across units |

## Step 2: Choose a margin

Margin is the share of the price you keep as profit.

- Price = cost ÷ (1 − margin)
- Cost 6 and a 50% margin: 6 ÷ 0.5 = **12**

## Step 3: Check the market

- What do similar products cost?
- What is the next-best alternative for the customer?
- What is it worth to them? A saved hour or a nicer gift can justify a higher price.

## Step 4: Set options

Offering good, better and best often lifts sales of the middle option.

## Step 5: Test and adjust

- Try a price for a few weeks.
- Watch sales and feedback.
- Raise prices gradually and tell customers why when needed.

## Mistakes

- Forgetting your time
- Competing only on being cheapest
- Ignoring fees
- Never revisiting prices
