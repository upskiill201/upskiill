## University UX/UI Programs in Canada

Several Canadian universities offer continuing-education certificates specifically in UX/UI design:

- **University of Toronto (SCS)** — UX Design certificate, strong on research methods and credibility for career-changers.
- **BCIT (British Columbia Institute of Technology)** — Part-time UX design certificate, practical and industry-connected.
- **York University (SCE)** — UX design and human-computer interaction courses.
- **Humber College** — UX design post-graduate certificate, includes a capstone portfolio project.

These programs cost **$2,000–$6,000 CAD** and typically run 4–12 months part-time. They carry more institutional credibility than a bootcamp or online certificate, which matters if you're targeting larger corporate or government design roles.

---

## UX/UI Bootcamps in Canada

Immersive bootcamps compress design training into 10–24 weeks, often with job-placement support:

- **BrainStation** (Toronto, Vancouver, remote) — UX/UI design bootcamp with strong industry partnerships.
- **Lighthouse Labs** — Known primarily for dev bootcamps but also offers UX/UI tracks.
- **General Assembly** — UX design immersive, available in Canada via remote cohorts.

Bootcamps cost **$10,000–$16,000 CAD** and are the fastest route to a portfolio and job-ready skills, but represent a significant financial commitment before you know whether design work suits you day-to-day.

> **Before committing $12,000+ to a bootcamp**, test the fundamentals — wireframing, user flows, basic Figma — through free or low-cost resources first. See [is paying for online courses worth it?](/blog/is-paying-for-online-courses-worth-it) for how to evaluate this decision.

## Online UX/UI Courses (Cheapest and Most Flexible)

- **Google UX Design Certificate** (via Coursera) — widely recognized, ~$49/month, 3–6 months part-time.
- **Udemy UX/UI courses** — one-time purchase, $10–$20 on sale, variable quality (filter for 4.5+ stars, 10,000+ ratings).
- **Interaction Design Foundation** — subscription-based, strong on design theory and research.

These are the most affordable options but require self-discipline — completion rates for self-paced video courses across the industry sit around **10–15%**, meaning most people who buy these courses never finish them.
