Discretionary spending is the part of your budget where you have a choice.

## Direct answer

Discretionary spending is money you spend on wants rather than needs, such as dining out, entertainment, hobbies, travel and new gadgets. Non-discretionary spending covers essentials like housing, utilities and basic food. Setting a limit on discretionary spending lets you enjoy it without guilt.

## Examples

| Discretionary | Non-discretionary |
| :--- | :--- |
| Takeaways and restaurants | Rent and utilities |
| Streaming services | Basic groceries |
| Hobbies | Transport to work |
| Holidays | Health costs |
| New clothes | Minimum debt payments |

## How to manage it

1. Total last month's wants.
2. Set a monthly limit that fits your goals.
3. Use a separate account or card for it.
4. Decide your top three treats and prioritise them.

See the [50/30/20 rule](/blog/50-30-20-rule-budget) for one way to size this.
