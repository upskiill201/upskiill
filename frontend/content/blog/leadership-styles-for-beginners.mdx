You may have heard that leaders are "democratic" or "servant" or "transformational". Here are the main **leadership styles** in plain language, when each works, and a short quiz to see where you lean.

## Direct answer

The main leadership styles are autocratic (the leader decides), democratic (the group shapes the decision), laissez-faire (hands-off), transformational (inspire change), servant (put the team first), coaching (develop each person) and situational (adapt to the person and task). No single style is best: good leaders switch depending on the situation.

## The styles

### Autocratic
The leader decides and tells. **Works when:** there is a crisis, the team is new or a quick decision is needed. **Risk:** low ownership and fewer ideas. Psychologist Kurt Lewin described this style in early research on leadership.

### Democratic
The leader invites input, then decides together or with strong consultation. **Works when:** people are skilled and quality matters. **Risk:** slow if overused.

### Laissez-faire
The leader gives freedom and steps back. **Works when:** people are experienced and self-driven. **Risk:** drift and confusion if people need guidance.

### Transformational
The leader inspires through a vision and personal example. **Works when:** change or growth is needed. **Risk:** can neglect details.

### Servant
The leader focuses on removing obstacles and developing people. The idea is associated with Robert Greenleaf. **Works when:** trust and long-term growth matter. **Risk:** decisions can be slow if overdone.

### Coaching
The leader asks questions and builds skills one to one. **Works when:** you have time and people want to grow. **Risk:** not suitable in an emergency.

### Situational
The leader adapts the amount of direction and support to the person's skill and confidence for the task. The Hersey and Blanchard model is the best known.

Daniel Goleman also described six styles (coercive, authoritative, affiliative, democratic, pacesetting and coaching) and argued that leaders who use several get the best results.

## When to use which

| Situation | Style to lean on |
| :--- | :--- |
| New team member, unfamiliar task | More directive |
| Skilled, motivated person | Hands-off or democratic |
| Crisis or tight deadline | Directive |
| Growing a high performer | Coaching |
| Big change | Transformational |
| Low morale | Servant, listening first |

## A quick self-quiz

Answer honestly. Pick the closer one each time.

1. When a decision is urgent, do you **(A)** decide quickly or **(B)** gather views first?
2. When someone struggles, do you **(A)** show them how or **(B)** ask questions so they find it?
3. When plans change, do you **(A)** set the new direction or **(B)** explain why and invite ideas?
4. When things go well, do you **(A)** keep the team on the plan or **(B)** give more freedom?

Mostly A: you lean directive. Mostly B: you lean participative. The goal is not to be one or the other, but to notice your habit and use the other when it fits.

## How to widen your style

- Ask your team: "What would help you most from me right now?"
- Try the opposite style once a week in a low-risk situation.
- Match style to the person, not to your habit.
