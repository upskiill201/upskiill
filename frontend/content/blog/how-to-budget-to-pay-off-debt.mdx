Debt feels heavy partly because it is unclear. A budget turns it into a plan with dates. This guide shows **how to budget to pay off debt**, with a worked example. It is general information, not personal financial advice.

## Direct answer: how to budget to pay off debt

List every debt with its balance, interest rate and minimum payment. Budget for all the minimums first, then find extra money by tracking spending and trimming flexible costs, and send every extra dollar to one debt at a time, using either the avalanche (highest interest first) or snowball (smallest balance first) method.

## Step 1: List every debt

| Debt | Balance | Interest rate | Minimum |
| :--- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Credit card | $1,200 | 24% | $40 |
| Personal loan | $3,000 | 8% | $90 |
| Store card | $400 | 20% | $25 |

## Step 2: Pay every minimum

Missing a minimum often means fees and extra interest. Treat minimums as fixed costs in your [monthly budget](/blog/monthly-budget-template).

## Step 3: Cover essentials and a small buffer

Housing, food, transport and utilities come first. Keep a small buffer so a surprise does not go back on the card.

## Step 4: Find the extra

Track a month of spending, then look for money in the flexible categories: eating out, subscriptions, impulse buys. Even $100 a month sent to debt makes a difference over time. Read [how to budget for beginners](/blog/how-to-budget-for-beginners) for tracking steps.

## Step 5: Pick a payoff method

**Avalanche:** extra money goes to the highest interest rate first. In the table above that is the credit card at 24%. It usually costs the least in interest.

**Snowball:** extra money goes to the smallest balance first. In the table that is the store card at $400. You clear it faster, and its payment rolls onto the next debt.

Choose the one you will stick with. A plan you follow beats a perfect one you drop.

## Step 6: Roll payments forward

When one debt is cleared, add its old payment to the next one. In the snowball example, clearing the store card frees its $25 minimum, which joins your extra money.

## Example

Suppose you can send **$100** extra a month. Using the avalanche method, the credit card gets its $40 minimum plus the $100, so $140 a month. The loan and store card each get their minimum. When the card is gone, its $140 moves to the store card, then the loan.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Paying extra on a low-interest debt while a high-interest one grows
- Adding new debt while trying to pay old debt
- Ignoring a buffer, which pushes the next surprise onto the card
- Stopping the budget once one debt is cleared

## The bottom line

A debt payoff plan is a budget plus a method. List the debts, pay the minimums, find the extra and send it to one debt at a time.
