Most new businesses fail because nobody needed what they built. Validation is how you find that out before you spend your savings.

## Direct answer

To validate a business idea, name a specific customer and problem, talk to at least ten real people with that problem, check the market numbers, run a small cheap test (such as a landing page, a pre-order or a small paid pilot), and decide to go ahead, change the idea or stop. Evidence from real people beats your own enthusiasm.

## 6 steps

### 1. Be specific
Who has the problem? What is it? How do they solve it today?

### 2. Talk to real people
Ask about their past behaviour, not whether they'd like your idea. "Tell me about the last time this happened" beats "Would you buy this?"

### 3. Check the numbers
- How many people have this problem?
- What could you charge?
- What would it cost you to reach and serve each one?

### 4. Run a cheap test
- A one-page website describing the offer
- A waitlist or pre-order
- A small paid pilot for a few customers
- Doing it by hand first

### 5. Look for real signals
Strong signals: money, pre-orders, signed commitments. Weak signals: "sounds great", likes and compliments.

### 6. Decide
Go ahead, adjust the idea or stop. Stopping early is a win.

## Idea validation checklist

- [ ] I can name the customer.
- [ ] They have the problem often.
- [ ] They already spend time or money on it.
- [ ] I've spoken to at least ten of them.
- [ ] Someone has paid or committed.
- [ ] The numbers leave a profit.
