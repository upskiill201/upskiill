A CV (or resume) is a short document that shows an employer why you are worth an interview. Without work history, the key is to show skills and proof in other ways.

## Direct answer

To write a CV with no experience, put your contact details and a short profile at the top, then projects, volunteering and education, then skills. For each item say what you did and what changed. Keep it to one page, tailor it to each job using words from the job post and proofread it carefully.

## CV order for beginners

1. **Name and contact:** phone, email, city, LinkedIn if you have one.
2. **Profile:** two or three lines about who you are and what you want.
3. **Education:** school, dates, relevant subjects or results.
4. **Projects, volunteering, activities:** your best evidence.
5. **Work experience:** any part-time or informal work.
6. **Skills:** specific ones, such as Excel, customer service, languages.

## Write a strong profile

> Reliable school leaver with experience organising community events and volunteering at a food bank. Looking for an entry-level customer service role where I can learn fast and help people.

## Turn activities into bullets

Pattern: **verb + what + result.**

- Organised a school fundraiser for 120 people and raised the funds needed for new equipment.
- Helped customers at a family shop on weekends, handling cash and stock counts.
- Led a four-person group project and presented the findings to the class.

If you have no number, describe the outcome.

## Skills to list

Use the words in the job post, where they are true: communication, teamwork, time management, basic computer skills, languages, customer service. Back them up with examples in the bullets above.

## What to avoid

- Long paragraphs
- Spelling mistakes
- Using one CV for every job
- Lying. Interviewers will ask.
- Fancy fonts and graphics that break automated scanners
