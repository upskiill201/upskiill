Interview questions repeat. If you prepare answers to the common ones, most interviews will feel familiar.

## Direct answer

The most common interview questions are: tell me about yourself, why this job, your strengths and weaknesses, a time you handled conflict, a time you failed, and where you see yourself in five years. Answer each in under two minutes with a true, specific example, using situation, action and result. Prepare four or five flexible stories and adapt them.

## The questions and how to answer

### Tell me about yourself
About a minute: where you are now, one or two relevant things you have done, and why you want this role.

### Why do you want this job?
Link what the role offers to what you want and can do. Mention something specific about the company.

### What are your strengths?
Pick two relevant strengths and give a short example for each.

### What is your biggest weakness?
Choose a real, minor one. Say what you do about it. Example: "I used to avoid asking for help. Now I ask early, and my last project finished on time."

### Tell me about a time you handled conflict
Situation, how you listened and found a solution, and what changed. Keep it calm and fair.

### Tell me about a time you failed
Own the mistake, say what you learned and what you do differently now.

### Describe a time you worked in a team
Describe your role specifically, not just "we".

### Where do you see yourself in five years?
Show ambition that fits the role: learning, growing and taking on more responsibility.

### Why should we hire you?
Match your two or three strongest skills to what the post asks for.

### Do you have any questions?
Always say yes. Ask about the team, what success looks like in three months and next steps.

## The story pattern

1. **Situation:** one sentence.
2. **Action:** what you did.
3. **Result:** the outcome, with a number if you have one.

## Prepare a story bank

Write down five stories: a success, a problem you solved, teamwork, a mistake and a time you learned fast. Most questions can be answered with one of them.

## Questions about your situation

Gaps, leaving a job and salary questions are normal. Keep answers brief, honest and forward-looking.
