## What to Look for in an AI Learning App

Before comparing apps, know what makes one actually work:

1. **Short sessions.** Lessons of 5 to 15 minutes fit into real life. Hour-long lectures do not.
2. **Active practice.** You should answer, apply and recall, not just watch.
3. **Habit mechanics.** Streaks, daily goals and reminders keep you consistent.
4. **Clear progression.** You should know what you have learned and what is next.
5. **A path to building.** Eventually you need to apply AI to real problems.

Most apps nail one or two of these. Very few do all five, which is why combining two apps often works best.

## Sololearn: Best for Community-Driven Beginners

Sololearn offers short lessons in popular programming languages plus AI-related topics, with coding challenges and an active community where learners help each other.

## Kaggle Learn: Best Fully Free Hands-On Machine Learning

Kaggle, owned by Google, is the world's best-known data science community. Its **Learn** section offers short, completely free micro-courses in Python, pandas, machine learning, deep learning and more, all run in the browser with real datasets.

**Strengths:** 100% free, real code on real data, respected in the data community.

**Limitations:** best on a laptop, minimal gamification, assumes you will stay motivated on your own. Better once you know basic Python (see [how long does it take to learn Python?](/blog/how-long-to-learn-python)).

## Mimo: Best for Coding Foundations

Mimo focuses on coding (Python, JavaScript, web development) with short mobile lessons. It is a good foundation if you plan to move into technical AI work, since Python is the language of AI.

**Limitations:** less focused on AI concepts themselves, and full paths often require a subscription.

## Brilliant: Best for Visual Concepts

Brilliant teaches math, data and computer science through interactive visual puzzles, including topics that underpin AI. It is excellent for building intuition.

**Limitations:** most content requires a paid subscription after a limited free experience.

## AI Assistants: Best for Learning AI by Using It

The free tiers of ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini are, in a sense, the most direct way to learn practical AI skills: prompting, iterating and verifying. You can also ask them to tutor you:

```
Teach me one AI concept per day, starting from zero.
Today: explain what "training data" is with an everyday example,
then quiz me with 3 questions and wait for my answers.
```

**Limitations:** no structure, no progress tracking and no accountability. It is easy to have interesting conversations and retain nothing. ## Can You Learn AI on Your Phone?

Yes, for a large part of the journey. Phones are ideal for:

- AI concepts and vocabulary
- Coding fundamentals
- Prompting practice with an AI assistant
- Keeping a daily streak in spare minutes

You will want a laptop when you start training models, writing longer programs or building projects. Many learners use their phone for the daily habit and a laptop for weekend projects.
