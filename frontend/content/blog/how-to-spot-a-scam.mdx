Scammers aren't looking for fools. They're looking for people who are busy, kind or worried. Knowing the pattern protects you.

## Direct answer

To spot a scam, watch for pressure to act quickly, requests for money, codes or passwords, offers that seem too good to be true, unusual payment methods (gift cards, crypto, bank transfer to a stranger), secrecy and messages from unknown or look-alike senders. When in doubt, stop, and check using contact details you find yourself.

## The recipe every scam follows

1. **Contact:** a message, call, ad or profile.
2. **Hook:** a prize, a threat, a bargain, romance or a problem.
3. **Urgency:** "act now or lose out".
4. **Isolation:** "don't tell anyone".
5. **Payment or data:** money, codes or passwords.

## 10 warning signs

1. You didn't expect the contact
2. It creates urgency or fear
3. It asks for payment in odd ways
4. It asks for codes, PINs or passwords
5. The offer is too good to be true
6. The sender's address or number looks slightly wrong
7. Poor spelling or a generic greeting
8. Links that don't match the real website
9. Pressure to keep it secret
10. They won't let you verify independently

## Scam emails and texts

- Don't click links. Open the company's real website yourself.
- Check the sender's actual address.
- Banks won't ask for your full PIN or password.

## Social media and shops

- Check reviews and account age.
- Pay with protected methods.
- Be wary of "investment" advice from new online friends.

For marketplace scams see [how to avoid scams on Facebook Marketplace](/blog/how-to-avoid-scams-on-facebook-marketplace), and for common money scams see [common financial scams](/blog/common-financial-scams).

## If it happens to you

1. Stop all contact.
2. Call your bank right away.
3. Change passwords and enable two-factor authentication.
4. Keep screenshots and records.
5. Report it to your national fraud authority.

You are not alone, and reporting helps others.
