Every purchase is also a decision not to buy something else.

## Direct answer

Opportunity cost is the value of what you give up when you choose one option. If you spend 100 on a night out, the opportunity cost is the next best use of that 100, such as saving it, paying off debt or buying something else. It helps you compare choices honestly.

## Everyday examples

- Buying a coffee every day instead of putting that money toward a goal.
- Taking a job with a longer commute, which costs time as well as money.
- Spending a weekend on a project instead of resting.
- Buying a new phone instead of repairing the old one and using the difference elsewhere.

## How to use it

1. Before buying, ask "What else could this money do?"
2. Compare the two options side by side.
3. Include time, not just price.
4. Choose what fits your priorities.

## Not every choice should be a sacrifice

The goal is not to say no to everything. It is to say yes to what matters most.
