Wondering why you keep buying things you didn't plan? You're not alone, and it usually isn't about willpower.

## Direct answer

People impulse buy because of feelings (stress, boredom, wanting a lift), triggers (sales, social media, seeing something) and designs that rush decisions (countdown timers, one-click checkout). For some people, impulsivity linked to conditions like ADHD plays a part. Understanding your trigger lets you plan a pause.

*This is general information, not medical or financial advice.*

## Common reasons

| Reason | What it feels like |
| :--- | :--- |
| Emotional | Shopping to cheer up or relax |
| Boredom | Scrolling shops with nothing to do |
| Social | Friends or influencers buying, fear of missing out |
| Marketing | "Only 2 left!" and timers |
| Habit | Browsing is part of your routine |
| Impulsivity | Acting before thinking, which some people with ADHD describe |

## Spot your pattern

For two weeks, note: what I bought, how I felt before, where I was. Patterns appear.

## What helps

1. **Name the feeling,** and find another outlet: a walk, a call, a shower.
2. **Add friction:** remove saved cards and apps.
3. **Waiting rule:** 24 to 48 hours. See [how to stop impulse buying](/blog/how-to-stop-impulse-buying).
4. **Plan treats** in your budget so they don't ambush you.
5. **Ask for support** from a friend or an adviser.

## When to get help

If spending causes debt, distress or you feel out of control, talk to a healthcare professional or a free money advice service.
