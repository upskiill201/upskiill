Fraud changes its clothes but not its shape. These are the types you're most likely to meet.

## Direct answer

The most common financial scams are investment and crypto scams, phishing and fake bank messages, impersonation of officials or relatives, romance scams, advance-fee and fake job scams, and money-mule schemes. All of them use trust, fear or greed, add urgency and ask for money or data. The best defence is to pause and verify independently.

## The main types

| Scam | How it works | Warning sign |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Investment / crypto | Promises big or guaranteed returns, shows fake gains | "Guaranteed" profit, pressure to add more |
| Phishing | Fake emails or texts from banks, delivery firms, tax offices | Link to enter login details |
| Impersonation | Pretends to be police, bank staff or a relative in trouble | Demands for urgent transfers or gift cards |
| Romance | Builds a relationship, then asks for money | Never meets in person, a crisis needing money |
| Advance fee | "Pay a fee to receive a prize, loan or job" | Upfront payment for something you didn't ask for |
| Fake job / mule | Asks you to receive and forward money | Pay for "training" or move money for a "boss" |
| Ponzi scheme | Pays old investors with new investors' money | Steady returns no matter what the market does |

## Why they work

They aim at emotions: fear, hope, loneliness, urgency. Smart people fall for them too.

## How to protect yourself

- Never send money to someone you only know online.
- Be sceptical of guaranteed returns. See [what is investment risk](/blog/what-is-investment-risk).
- Verify requests by calling the real organisation.
- Keep your PINs and codes private.
- Use two-factor authentication.

## If you're caught

See the steps in [how to spot a scam](/blog/how-to-spot-a-scam): stop contact, call your bank, change passwords and report it.
