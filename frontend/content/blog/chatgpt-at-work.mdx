Millions of people use AI chatbots at work. Used well, they save time. Used carelessly, they can leak information or spread errors.

## Direct answer

You can often use ChatGPT or similar tools at work for drafting, summarising public material, brainstorming and learning, but check your employer's policy first, never paste confidential or personal data, and always verify what it produces before you use it. If a tool is blocked, ask your manager or IT about approved options.

## Good uses

- Draft or tidy an email, then edit it in your own voice
- Summarise a long public article
- Brainstorm ideas, names or headlines
- Explain a concept you're new to
- Plan a project or a meeting agenda
- Turn rough notes into a clear structure

## What to keep out

| Don't paste | Why |
| :--- | :--- |
| Customer names, emails, health or financial data | Privacy and legal duties |
| Confidential plans or unreleased products | Could be exposed |
| Passwords, keys, tokens | Security |
| Anything your policy forbids | You may break rules |

When unsure, remove names and details or use an approved tool.

## Check everything

AI can sound sure and be wrong. Verify facts, numbers and quotes against a reliable source. You are responsible for what you send.

## If ChatGPT is blocked

- Ask whether there's an approved AI tool
- Ask what is allowed and what isn't
- Don't work around the block with personal devices for work data
