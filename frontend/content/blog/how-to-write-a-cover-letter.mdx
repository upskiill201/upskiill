A cover letter is a short note that says why you want *this* job and why you can do it. It is your chance to sound like a person.

## Direct answer

A good cover letter is three or four short paragraphs: why this role and company, two or three things you have done that match the job, and a polite close. Address it to a named person where you can, use words from the job post and keep it under one page. Don't repeat your CV; add the story behind it.

## Structure

1. **Opening:** the role and your strongest reason you fit.
2. **Evidence:** two or three examples with results.
3. **Why them:** something specific about the company.
4. **Close:** thank them and say you would welcome an interview.

## Example: internship

> Dear Ms Okafor,
>
> I'm applying for the marketing internship at Brightly. I've run my university society's social media for a year, growing our followers from 200 to 900 and bringing in two new sponsors.
>
> In that role I learned to plan content weekly, test posts and read simple analytics. I'd like to bring that to your campaigns, and learn from your team.
>
> I admire how Brightly explains products in plain language, which is how I try to write. Thank you for considering my application. I would be glad to talk.
>
> Kind regards,
> Sam Lee

Change the details to your own true ones.

## With no experience

Use school, volunteering and personal projects as your evidence. Show reliability, willingness to learn and why you want this kind of work.

## Tips

- Find the hiring manager's name if you can.
- Mirror two or three keywords from the post.
- Use plain language.
- Read it aloud to catch awkward lines.
- Proofread twice.

## Mistakes

- Starting with "To whom it may concern" with no effort to find a name
- Copying your CV
- Long paragraphs
- Wrong company name from a previous application

## By email

Put a brief version in the email, attach your CV and use a clear subject: "Application: Marketing Intern, Sam Lee".
