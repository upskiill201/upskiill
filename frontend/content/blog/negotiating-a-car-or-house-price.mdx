Big purchases are where small negotiating skills save the most money. The same steps apply to a used car, a new car or a home.

## Direct answer

To negotiate a car or house price: research what similar ones really sell for, decide your budget and walk-away price, line up alternatives, make a confident first offer with reasons, stay calm, and agree everything in writing. The best leverage is being willing to walk away.

## Before you talk

1. **Research real prices:** recent sales of similar items, not just asking prices.
2. **Set your budget** including fees, insurance, repairs and running costs.
3. **Decide your walk-away price.**
4. **Have alternatives:** other cars, other houses, other sellers.
5. **Check condition:** an inspection or survey gives you facts to negotiate with.

## During the negotiation

- Be polite and friendly.
- Make a first offer you can justify, below your target.
- Give reasons: condition, comparable sales, needed repairs.
- Let silence work.
- Don't show how much you love it.
- Negotiate the whole deal, not just the price: warranty, extras, repairs, completion date.

## Things to watch for

- Pressure to decide today
- Add-on fees at the end
- Monthly payment talk that hides the total price

See [negotiation tactics and strategies](/blog/negotiation-tactics-and-strategies).

## Close properly

- Get the final price and terms in writing.
- Read the contract before you sign.
- Be ready to walk away if the terms change.
