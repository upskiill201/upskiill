## Does Udemy Have a Student Discount?

Udemy does **not have a formal student pricing tier** like Spotify Student or Apple Education pricing. There is no .edu verification system or student-specific plan.

However, Udemy's **flash sale pricing model** effectively achieves a similar outcome: courses listed at $99–$200 are regularly discounted to **$10–$15** for all buyers. Udemy runs these sales frequently — often weekly. The practical advice for students:

- **Check Udemy on Thursday–Friday** when new promotional cycles typically begin
- **Use browser extensions** like Honey to track price drops
- **Avoid buying at full price** — wait a few days and almost any course will go on sale
- **Look for coupons** shared in learning communities and on Reddit

For a student on a budget, Udemy's effective price point of £8–£12 per course makes it one of the cheapest ways to access quality video instruction.

## The Real Challenge: Udemy and Student Schedules

Here is the honest problem with Udemy for students: **lecture weeks and coursework periods will kill your momentum**.

A typical Udemy course is 10–25 hours of video. During a normal student term, finding consistent 30–60 minute blocks for an elective course — on top of reading, essays, seminars, and deadlines — is genuinely difficult. Without any mechanism pulling you back daily, most students buy a Udemy course in September and return to it in December, by which point they've forgotten the first third.

This is backed by data: self-paced online courses across all platforms see **completion rates of 10–15%**. The problem isn't students being lazy — it's that passive video with no daily habit structure is inherently fragile. The moment your academic workload spikes, optional video learning is the first thing dropped.

See [how to actually finish an online course](/blog/how-to-actually-finish-an-online-course) for practical strategies that help — but the root cause is format, not willpower.

## Can Students Put Udemy Certificates on Their CV?

Yes — but with realistic expectations. Udemy certificates are **not formally accredited**. What they signal to employers:

- You identified a skill gap and took initiative to address it ✓
- You completed a course (if you actually did) ✓
- The skill is formally recognised by an institution ✗

The most effective approach is to **pair the certificate with a real project**: "I completed Udemy's Python for Data Science course and built a [specific project], which you can see here." The project does the heavy lifting; the certificate is context.

For students entering tech, design, marketing, or content creation roles, a demonstrable portfolio piece will outperform any certificate. For students in finance, healthcare, or law, a Udemy certificate carries essentially no weight — regulated qualifications are required.
