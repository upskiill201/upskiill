Data scientist is a popular career goal, and a competitive one. A clear route helps.

## Direct answer

To become a data scientist, learn Python, SQL and statistics, practise on real datasets, build a portfolio of projects that answer real questions, and apply for analyst or junior data roles. A degree helps for many titles but a strong portfolio and clear communication matter too. Expect months of steady work.

## Step by step

1. **Learn the basics:** [what data science is](/blog/what-is-data-science-for-beginners).
2. **Python and pandas:** [learn data science from scratch](/blog/learn-data-science-from-scratch).
3. **SQL:** [how to learn SQL](/blog/how-to-learn-sql-for-beginners).
4. **Statistics:** averages, spread, tests, uncertainty.
5. **Projects:** three or four, each with a question, method and clear conclusion.
6. **Portfolio and profile:** write-ups on a simple site or repository.
7. **Apply:** analyst, junior data and related roles.

## Skills employers look for

- Python and SQL
- Cleaning messy data
- Basic statistics
- Charts and communication
- Business sense: knowing which question matters

## Education

Many data scientist roles list a degree in a quantitative subject. Not all do, and some people move across from analyst work. Check the posts you want.

## What the pay data says

US Bureau of Labor Statistics figures for 2025 give state median wages for data scientists between about $69,000 in Mississippi and about $163,000 in Washington. Pay depends heavily on location, employer and experience, so use it as a guide, not a promise.

## With no experience

Begin with small real problems: your own spending, a local dataset, a hobby. Share what you found. Look for data analyst, reporting and operations analyst roles first.
