A mental shortcut that makes compounding feel real.

## Direct answer

The Rule of 72 says: years to double ≈ 72 ÷ annual rate of return. At 6 percent, money doubles in about 12 years. At 9 percent, about 8 years. It is an approximation, useful for quick comparisons.

## Examples

| Annual rate | Years to double (72 ÷ rate) |
| :--- | :--- |
| 2% | 36 |
| 4% | 18 |
| 6% | 12 |
| 8% | 9 |
| 12% | 6 |

## Uses

- Compare savings rates quickly.
- See what inflation does: at 3 percent, prices double in about 24 years.
- Understand how interest on debt grows if you do not pay it down.

## Limits

It is an estimate. Returns on investments are not guaranteed and vary year to year.
