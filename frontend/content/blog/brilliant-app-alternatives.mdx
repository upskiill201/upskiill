Brilliant built a genuinely well-designed niche product: interactive, puzzle-first lessons for math, logic, and STEM reasoning that make you manipulate a concept before naming it. If you like that hands-on philosophy but want it applied to subjects Brilliant doesn't cover — or just want more options — here's an honest rundown of alternatives, including [Teyro](/onboarding/0).

## Direct Answer: Best Brilliant Alternatives

If you specifically want interactive coding practice, Codecademy is the closest match in rigor for that domain. If you want the same "learn by doing" philosophy applied to broader practical skills — coding, business, AI, communication — Teyro is built around that using a 4-phase lesson structure. If price is the concern, Khan Academy offers free, high-quality math and STEM content, though with less puzzle-first interactivity than Brilliant.

## Why People Look Beyond Brilliant

Brilliant's strength — its tight focus on math, logic, and quantitative reasoning — is also its limitation for learners whose goals sit outside that niche. If you want to build coding skill more broadly, understand business fundamentals, or improve communication, Brilliant's format doesn't extend there, since its whole design is built around a puzzle mechanic that works best for clean, right-or-wrong quantitative problems. That's not a flaw so much as a deliberate scope decision — but it means learners with broader goals need a different tool, or several.

## Teyro: Same Philosophy, Wider Scope

Teyro shares Brilliant's core conviction — that active practice beats passive watching — but applies it to subjects that don't reduce as neatly to a single puzzle format. Its 4-phase lesson structure (Learn, Apply, Reflect, Deepen) exists specifically because skills like business strategy or persuasive communication need a different scaffolding than a math puzzle does; there's rarely one clean correct click-to-solve answer. Teyro also uses the same daily-streak-and-XP mechanics that keep Brilliant users returning, applied across coding, business, AI, and communication tracks instead of a single quantitative niche.

## Codecademy: Deeper Coding Specifically

If what drew you to Brilliant was its rigor for a specific technical subject, Codecademy offers a comparable depth of specialization, just for coding instead of math. Its in-browser code editor and language-specific curricula make it the stronger pick if coding proficiency, not broader skill-building, is your singular goal — see our fuller [Teyro vs Codecademy](/blog/teyro-vs-codecademy) comparison for more detail on that specific trade-off.

## Khan Academy: Free and STEM-Focused

For learners specifically prioritizing cost, Khan Academy remains one of the best free resources for math and STEM fundamentals, with a nonprofit model and extensive content library. It leans more toward traditional instructional video and practice problems than Brilliant's puzzle-first interactivity, but the depth and price make it worth considering, especially for students.

## What to Actually Weigh When Choosing

- **Subject match first.** Don't pick an alternative for its gamification alone — make sure it actually covers the skill you want to build.
- **Session length and habit fit.** Brilliant's daily-lesson format works because sessions are short; any alternative that requires long, unstructured sessions will likely see the same low completion rates common across free online courses (often cited around 3-6%).
- **Price versus actual usage.** A free tier you'll actually use consistently beats a paid subscription you'll abandon after two weeks.
