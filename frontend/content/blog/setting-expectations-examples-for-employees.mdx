Vague expectations cause stress. Specific ones let people succeed. Here are examples and scripts.

## Direct answer

Clear expectations are specific and checkable: they say what, by when and to what standard. "Be proactive" is vague; "flag any delay as soon as you see it" is clear. Use short scripts, write expectations down and agree when you'll review them.

## Vague vs clear

| Vague | Clear |
| :--- | :--- |
| Be a team player | Share your progress in the daily stand-up and help colleagues who ask |
| Communicate better | Reply to customer emails within one working day |
| Do quality work | Check every report against the checklist before sending |
| Be on time | Be at your desk, ready, by 9:00 for team meetings |
| Take initiative | Suggest one improvement a month |

## Scripts

### For a new employee
> In your first month, I'd like you to learn our three main systems and shadow five customer calls. By day 60 I'd like you to handle calls with a buddy listening. How does that sound?

### For deadlines
> I need the draft by Wednesday noon, so I can review before the Thursday meeting. If it will be late, tell me by Tuesday.

### For quality
> Good means figures checked, no spelling errors and a one-line summary at the top.

### For a difficult employee
> Over the past month three reports were late and had errors. I need reports on time and checked. Let's agree a checklist and review progress each Friday for four weeks.

### For a missed expectation
> I expected the report on Friday and it arrived Monday. Can you tell me what happened? What do you need to make Friday work?

## Tips

- Use numbers and dates.
- Explain why it matters.
- Ask them to repeat it back.
- Write it down.
