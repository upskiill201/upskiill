"Network" sounds like something confident people do at events. In practice it is a handful of short, polite conversations. Here is how to do it from zero.

## Direct answer

To network for a job, ask people for advice, not for a job. Message people one step away from you (alumni, former colleagues, friends of friends, people in roles you want), send a short note with one specific question, say thank you, and stay in touch. A conversation first often leads to a referral or a tip about a role before it is advertised.

## Who to contact

1. **People you already know:** relatives, teachers, past managers, classmates.
2. **Friends of friends:** ask for a warm introduction.
3. **Alumni** of your school or course.
4. **People in the job you want:** on LinkedIn or in online groups.
5. **Communities:** local meetups, industry groups, volunteer projects.

## A message that works

> Hi Sam, I'm a recent graduate hoping to move into customer support. I saw you made that move at Brightly. Could I ask one question: what helped you most in your first six months? Thanks either way.

Why it works:
- It is short and specific.
- It explains why you chose them.
- It asks for something easy to give.
- It does not ask for a job.

## On LinkedIn

- Make sure your profile has a photo, a clear headline and a short summary. See [LinkedIn profile tips](/blog/linkedin-profile-tips-for-job-seekers).
- Add a short note to every connection request.
- Comment helpfully on posts from people in your field before you message them.

## The conversation

1. Thank them for their time.
2. Ask about their path, what the work is really like, and what they would do in your place.
3. Ask: "Is there anyone else you think I should talk to?"
4. Do not ask for a job. If there is a role, they may offer.

## Follow up

- Send a thank you the same day, mentioning something they said.
- Tell them if you acted on their advice. People love hearing that.
- Stay in touch every few months with a short update.

## Common mistakes

- Sending the same copied message to dozens of strangers.
- Asking "do you have any jobs?" straight away.
- Writing long messages.
- Only reaching out when you need something.
