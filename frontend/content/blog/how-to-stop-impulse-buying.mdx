Most impulse buys aren't about the thing. They're about a feeling, a trigger or a clever trick. Seeing that is half the fix.

## Direct answer

To stop impulse buying, learn your triggers, add friction between wanting and buying, and use a waiting rule. Wait 24 to 48 hours for anything non-essential, remove saved cards and shopping apps, shop with a list, and ask "will I still want this next week?". Track purchases you regret to learn your pattern.

## 10 tactics

1. **Find your triggers:** stress, boredom, sales emails, social media.
2. **Waiting rule:** 24 hours for small items, 7 days for bigger ones.
3. **Wish list instead of checkout.**
4. **Remove saved card details.**
5. **Unsubscribe from promo emails and mute shopping ads.**
6. **Delete shopping apps** from your phone.
7. **Shop with a list,** and don't shop hungry.
8. **Use cash or a set limit** for discretionary spending.
9. **Ask three questions:** do I need it? Where will it go? What am I giving up?
10. **Track regret purchases** for a month.

## Online and Amazon

- Don't keep your card saved.
- Log out after shopping.
- Use a wish list and review it weekly.
- Beware "limited time" timers.

## Food and clothes

- Eat before you shop.
- For clothes, wait and check what you already own.

## Tricks used on you

Countdown timers, "only 2 left", free shipping thresholds, loyalty points and personalised ads are designed to speed you up. Slowing down beats them.
