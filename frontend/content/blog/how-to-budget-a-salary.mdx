A salary is not a budget. It is a number that arrives on payday and begins to disappear. Here is how to **budget your salary** so you decide where it goes before it is gone.

## Direct answer: how to budget a salary

Start with your take-home pay (after tax). Subtract your fixed bills first, then split what is left into everyday spending, savings, fun money and a small buffer. Move savings out on payday, set aside bill money, and check your plan weekly. Plan with the amount you receive, not the salary on your contract.

## Step 1: Work out your take-home pay

Look at your payslip or bank statement for what arrives each month. If you also have irregular income (tips, side work), plan only with your reliable amount and treat the rest as a bonus.

## Step 2: List fixed costs

Rent or housing, utilities, phone, insurance, transport passes, minimum debt payments. These are due whatever else happens, so they come off the top.

## Step 3: Split what is left

What remains after fixed costs is your **spendable** money. Divide it into four jobs:

- **Everyday:** groceries, transport, household items
- **Savings:** into a separate account, automatically
- **Fun:** money you can spend without guilt
- **Buffer:** for unplanned costs

## A worked example

Take-home salary of **$2,400** a month:

| Category | Amount |
| :--- | ---: |
| Rent | $800 |
| Utilities | $150 |
| Groceries | $350 |
| Transport | $150 |
| Phone | $50 |
| Minimum debt payment | $100 |
| **Fixed and essential total** | **$1,600** |
| Savings | $300 |
| Fun money | $350 |
| Buffer | $150 |
| **Left to assign** | **$0** |

The essentials add up to $1,600, leaving $800 to assign: $300 + $350 + $150 = $800. Income minus spending equals zero, so every dollar has a job.

## What to do on payday

Set a routine so it does not depend on remembering:

1. Move your savings amount to a separate account.
2. Put bill money aside, or set up automatic payments.
3. Transfer your weekly spending amount to the account or card you use day to day.
4. Check that the rest matches your plan.

## If you are paid weekly or biweekly

Write each bill with its due date, then assign it to the paycheck that arrives just before. Example: use the first paycheck of the month for rent and utilities, and the second for groceries, transport and savings. Keep a monthly total so you can see both checks add up to your plan. In months with three paychecks, use the extra one for your buffer or a sinking fund.

## Common mistakes

- **Budgeting with gross pay.** You will plan money you never receive.
- **Saving what is left.** Often nothing is left. Save first.
- **Ignoring annual costs.** Divide them by twelve and set the amount aside.
- **Never reviewing.** A ten-minute weekly check catches problems early.

## The bottom line

To budget a salary, start from take-home pay, cover fixed costs, give the rest jobs, and automate savings on payday.
