Aim high, measure what matters.

## Direct answer

OKRs pair an objective, a clear and inspiring goal, with key results, measurable outcomes that show whether you reached it. Set a few per quarter, review progress regularly and focus on outcomes rather than tasks.

## Example

**Objective:** Make onboarding delightful for new customers.

**Key results:**
1. Raise first-week activation from 40 percent to 60 percent.
2. Cut average setup time from 20 minutes to 10.
3. Reach a satisfaction score of 4.5 out of 5 from new users.

## Common mistakes

- Too many OKRs.
- Key results that are tasks, not outcomes.
- Setting and forgetting.
- Using them for performance punishment.

## How to run them

1. Set at the start of the period.
2. Check progress weekly or fortnightly.
3. Score honestly at the end.
4. Learn and reset.
