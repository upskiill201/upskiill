Confident speakers aren't born that way. They use a few repeatable habits.

## Direct answer

To speak confidently, decide your main point before you open your mouth, say it first and then give one or two reasons, slow down, pause instead of saying "um", and practise out loud. Confidence comes from structure and preparation more than personality.

## 7 habits

1. **Lead with your point.** "My recommendation is X. Here are two reasons."
2. **Keep it short.** Thirty seconds in a meeting is often enough.
3. **Slow down.** Fast speech sounds anxious.
4. **Pause.** A silent beat feels powerful, not awkward.
5. **Cut filler.** Replace "um" with a breath.
6. **Use your voice.** Vary pitch and finish sentences firmly rather than going up.
7. **Practise out loud.** Record yourself on your phone.

## In meetings

- Speak early; it gets harder later.
- Prepare one line you can add.
- Build on what others said: "Adding to Sam's point..."

## In interviews

- Use short stories. See [common interview questions](/blog/common-interview-questions-and-answers).
- Pause before answering.
- Sit upright and breathe.

## Handling nerves

Breathe out slowly, drop your shoulders, plant your feet and focus on helping the listener rather than being judged.
