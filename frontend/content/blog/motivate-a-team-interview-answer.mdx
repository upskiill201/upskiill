This question tests how you think about people. A short structure and an example make a strong answer.

## Direct answer

A good answer says you start by understanding what motivates each person, make the goal and its purpose clear, give people some control, recognise effort specifically and remove blockers. Then give a short real example with a result. Keep it to about two minutes.

## Structure

1. **Principle:** "People are motivated by different things, so I start by listening."
2. **Actions:** purpose, autonomy, recognition, removing blockers.
3. **Example:** situation, what you did, result.
4. **Close:** what you learned.

## Sample answer

> I start by finding out what matters to each person. On my last project, one teammate was losing energy, so I asked what was going on. He felt his work was invisible. I explained how his module affected customers, gave him ownership of the next release and thanked him specifically in our team meeting. His output improved and we shipped a week early. I've learned that a conversation usually beats a speech.

Change it to your true story.

## What interviewers listen for

- Treating people as individuals
- Clear goals
- Fair recognition
- Honesty in tough times

## What to avoid

- "I'd give them a bonus" as the only answer
- Generic quotes
- Ignoring underperformance
