Interviewers ask about conflict because everyone has it. They want to see how you handle it.

## Direct answer

Answer with a short true story using situation, action and result. Show that you stayed calm, listened to the other person, focused on the problem and not the person, and reached a workable outcome. End with what you learned. Keep it to one or two minutes and don't blame anyone.

## Structure

1. **Situation:** one or two sentences of context.
2. **Your action:** how you listened and what you suggested.
3. **Result:** what changed.
4. **Lesson:** what you do now.

## Sample answer

> On a group project at university, a teammate and I disagreed about how to split the work. Instead of arguing in the group chat, I asked to talk one to one. I listened to his concerns, which were about a clash with his job, and suggested he take the research while I took the presentation. We agreed on deadlines and checked in twice. We finished on time and got a good mark. Since then, I raise disagreements early and ask what the other person needs.

Change it to fit your real experience.

## Variations

- **Difficult coworker:** focus on understanding their view and agreeing a way of working. See [how to deal with difficult coworkers](/blog/how-to-deal-with-difficult-coworkers).
- **Disagreeing with a manager:** show respect, evidence and willingness to follow the final decision.
- **Conflict in a team:** show how you helped everyone be heard.

## What to avoid

- Blaming
- A story where you simply won
- A story with no resolution
- Saying you've never had a conflict
