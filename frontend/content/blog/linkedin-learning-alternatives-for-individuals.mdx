LinkedIn Learning was built with a very specific buyer in mind: HR and L&D teams rolling out training across an entire company, complete with admin dashboards and assigned learning paths. If you're an individual learner without a company-provided seat, that enterprise-first design can feel like it's not really built for you — which is a fair read, and worth addressing directly with some real alternatives, including [Teyro](/onboarding/0).

## Why LinkedIn Learning's Design Doesn't Always Fit Individuals

LinkedIn Learning's real strength — deep integration with enterprise training programs and LinkedIn profile certificates — comes from being built primarily for the B2B buyer. Individuals paying out of pocket without an employer-provided license are essentially using an enterprise product designed around top-down course assignment rather than personal, self-directed habit-building. That's not a dealbreaker, but it does mean the format (longer video courses, admin-oriented navigation) wasn't optimized with a single self-motivated learner's daily routine in mind.

## Teyro: Built Around the Individual Habit From the Start

Where LinkedIn Learning assumes an assigned learning path, Teyro assumes nothing is assigned — you're building your own daily habit, one 10-15 minute lesson at a time, reinforced by streaks and XP that only make sense in a single-user, self-directed context. Its 4-phase lesson structure (Learn, Apply, Reflect, Deepen) is built to make each short session feel complete on its own, which matters more for an individual squeezing in practice around a job than for a company mandating a training module by a deadline.

## Coursera and Udemy: Individually-Purchasable Depth

If your goal is closer to LinkedIn Learning's original format — structured video courses, just without the enterprise wrapper — Coursera and Udemy are both natively built for individual purchase. Coursera leans toward university-affiliated content and recognized certificates; Udemy leans toward a huge, cheap catalog with variable instructor quality. Neither uses gamification mechanics, so the completion challenge common to all self-paced video courses (dropout is the norm, not the exception, for unstructured online learning) still applies.

## What Actually Matters for Solo Learners

- **No admin dependency.** As an individual, you don't need (or want) features built around assigning courses to a team — look for platforms designed around a single person's motivation loop.
- **A free tier that's actually usable, not just a trial.** A 7-day trial pressures you into a decision before you know if the habit will stick; an ongoing free tier lets you test the format properly.
- **Gamification that matches solo motivation.** Streaks and XP work because they replace the external accountability (a manager, a cohort) an individual learner doesn't have.
