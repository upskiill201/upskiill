A pricing strategy is simply a method for deciding what to charge. Here are six.

## Direct answer

Common small-business pricing strategies are cost-plus (cost plus a margin), value-based (price by what it's worth to customers), competitor-based (price relative to the market), tiered (good, better, best), bundling (packages) and psychological pricing (for example, ending prices in .99). Most small businesses start with cost-plus to protect profit, then move toward value-based pricing.

## The six strategies

### 1. Cost-plus
Cost per unit plus a margin. Simple and safe, but may leave money on the table.

### 2. Value-based
Price by the benefit to the customer. Strong when your product saves time or money.

### 3. Competitor-based
Price near, above or below others. Useful as a check, not as the only method.

### 4. Tiered
Offer basic, standard and premium options. People often pick the middle one.

### 5. Bundles
Combine items at a combined price. Raises the average sale.

### 6. Psychological
Small cues like a "from" price, charm prices or a visible anchor price.

## Which to use

| Situation | Try |
| :--- | :--- |
| New product, unsure | Cost-plus, then test |
| Clear benefit to the customer | Value-based |
| Crowded market | Tiered and bundles |
| Selling several items | Bundles |

## Before you choose

## Test, don't guess

Change one thing, watch sales for a few weeks and keep what works.
