## Direct Answer: Slow Motion & Speed Editing at a Glance (40–60 Word Summary)

To edit slow motion video, reduce playback speed proportional to your recording frame rate (e.g., slowing 60fps footage to 40% on a 24fps timeline produces 2.5x smooth slow motion). For clips shot at standard 30fps, use Optical Flow interpolation to generate artificial in-between frames and prevent stuttering.

| Speed Technique | Target Frame Rate | Optimal Playback Speed | Ideal Use Case | Interpolation Method |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Standard Slow Motion** | 60 fps | 40% – 50% speed | Sports, cinematic walk shots, b-roll | Nearest Frame / Frame Blend |
| **Ultra Slow Motion** | 120 fps – 240 fps | 10% – 20% speed | Water droplets, explosive action, stunts | Frame Blend |
| **Optical Flow Slow-Mo**| 24 fps / 30 fps | 25% – 50% speed | Re-timing standard footage | Optical Flow (AI Motion Estimation) |
| **Time Lapse / Hyperlapse** | Any (30 fps base) | 400% – 2500% (4x–25x) | Sunsets, city traffic, construction | Frame Sampling |
| **Speed Ramping** | 60 fps+ | Dynamic (300% -> 30% -> 100%) | Action cuts, music beat drops, reels | Bezier Curve Velocity |

## The Physics of Frame Rates: Why Slow Motion Becomes Choppy

Understanding frame rate math is the secret to flawless speed control:

```
[Camera Sensor Recording]
        │
        ├──> Shot at 24 fps ───> Slowed to 50% on 24fps timeline ───> 12 fps (CHOPPY / STUTTERING)
        │
        ├──> Shot at 60 fps ───> Slowed to 40% on 24fps timeline ───> 24 fps (PERFECT SMOOTH SLOW-MO)
        │
        └──> Shot at 120 fps ──> Slowed to 20% on 24fps timeline ───> 24 fps (5x CINEMATIC ULTRA SLOW-MO)
```

### The Slow Motion Playback Formula:
```
Slowest Smooth Playback % = (Project Timeline FPS / Recorded Footage FPS) * 100
```

- **60fps on 24fps project:** (24 / 60) * 100 = 40% playback speed.
- **120fps on 24fps project:** (24 / 120) * 100 = 20% playback speed.
- **240fps on 24fps project:** (24 / 240) * 100 = 10% playback speed.

If your camera records at standard 24fps or 30fps and you slow it down, each frame displays twice or three times, causing a jarring strobe effect. To fix this, you must apply **Optical Flow**.

## Step-by-Step: How to Edit Slow Motion Video

Follow this standard procedure in Premiere Pro, DaVinci Resolve, Final Cut Pro, or CapCut:

```
[Import High-FPS Clip (60/120fps)]
               │
               ▼
[Step 1: Set Timeline to 24fps Cinema Standard]
               │
               ▼
[Step 2: Re-time Clip via Speed/Duration (e.g., 40%)]
               │
               ▼
[Step 3: Enable Optical Flow Frame Interpolation]
               │
               ▼
[Step 4: Pitch Shift Audio / Mute High-Pitched Mic]
               │
               ▼
[Step 5: Apply Dynamic Lighting & Brightness Curves]
```

### Step 1: Conforming Footage or Direct Speed Adjustment
There are two ways to slow down high frame-rate video:
1. **Interpret Footage (Conform Method):** In your project media bin, right-click the 60fps clip -> **Modify -> Interpret Footage** -> Set "Assume this frame rate" to `24.000`. When dropped onto the timeline, it automatically plays in smooth 100% slow motion with zero dropped frames.
2. **Timeline Re-timing (Speed/Duration):** Drop the clip onto your sequence, press `Ctrl/Cmd + R`, and set speed to `40%`.

### Step 2: Activating Optical Flow
When slowing down 30fps clips, navigate to **Time Interpolation -> Optical Flow** (or *Smooth Slow Motion* in mobile video apps). The software analyzes pixel vector motion vectors across adjacent frames and paints new intermediate images, creating smooth slow motion out of standard video.

### Step 3: Handling Slow-Motion Audio
Slowing down a video slows down its audio track, transforming speech into an eerie, demonic rumble.
- **For Talking Clips:** Check **Maintain Audio Pitch** to preserve human voice resonance.
- **For B-Roll Clips:** Unlink and delete the slowed camera microphone track. Replace it with atmospheric room tone, cinematic whoosh sound effects, and ambient bass drones.

## What is Speed Ramping and How to Master It

Speed ramping (velocity mapping) is the technique of seamlessly transitioning from lightning-fast hyper-speed to ultra-slow motion within a single continuous camera shot.

```
Speed Ramping Velocity Curve:
Speed %
 400% │     ┌───┐ (Fast Whip Transition)
      │    /     \
 100% │───/       \                                  /─── (Return to Normal)
      │            \                                /
  40% │             \______________________________/ (Slow-Motion Action Beat)
      └──────────────────────────────────────────────── Timeline Time (Seconds)
```

### How to Build a Speed Ramp in 4 Steps:
1. **Enable Clip Speed Keyframing:** Right-click your timeline clip -> **Show Clip Keyframes -> Time Remapping -> Speed**.
2. **Add Velocity Pins:** Place keyframe markers (`Cmd/Ctrl + Click`) right before the action begins and right after the dramatic climax finishes.
3. **Elevate and Lower Bands:** Drag the pre-action segment upward to `300% - 500%`. Drag the central climax segment downward to `30% - 40%`.
4. **Smooth Bezier Handles:** Split the keyframe marker into two halves and curve the velocity handles to create a smooth S-curve rather than a harsh angular speed jump.

## How to Convert Normal Video to Time Lapse

Converting ordinary, lengthy video recordings into high-speed time lapses compresses hours of construction, landscape sunrises, or deep-work sessions into dynamic 15-second clips.

### Method 1: Using Desktop & Mobile Video Editors
1. Import your continuous 10-to-60 minute video clip into your editor.
2. Speed up the clip playback by **800% to 2500% (8x to 25x speed)**.
3. Apply a subtle **Directional Blur (Angle matching camera movement, Length 3–5)** to simulate authentic camera shutter motion blur.
4. Mute the original camera audio track and replace it with an energetic, rhythmic soundtrack.

### Method 2: Online Free Speed Adjusters
If you don't have dedicated desktop editing software installed, you can use browser-based tools to change video speed online:
- **Clipchamp / 123apps Video Speed Increaser:** Upload video, select speed multiplier (2x, 4x, 8x, 16x), and download the processed MP4 without watermarks.
- **EZGIF Speed Changer:** Lightweight browser tool for quick short-form time lapse conversions.

## How to Fix Lighting and Convert Low Quality Video to High Quality HD

Slow motion footage often suffers from two common issues: dark exposure (because shooting at 120fps requires faster shutter speeds, letting in less light) and compression noise.

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Low-Light / Low-Quality Input (720p / Dark Shadows)   │
│                 │                                      │
│                 ▼                                      │
│  [Step 1: Exposure Lift & Gamma Curves]                │
│  [Step 2: AI Neural Denoising & Spatial Sharpening]   │
│  [Step 3: Super-Resolution Upscaling (1080p / 4K)]    │
│                 │                                      │
│                 ▼                                      │
│ Crisp, Bright, High-Fidelity Master Video (HD / 4K)    │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### How to Make a Dark Video Brighter:
1. **Lift Shadows and Blacks:** Open the Lumetri Color or Color Wheels panel. Raise the **Shadows** and **Lift** sliders by +15 to +25 to reveal shadow details without washing out true blacks.
2. **Boost Midtone Gamma:** Gently pull up the middle tonal curve to brighten faces and subjects naturally.
3. **Compensate with Denoise:** When brightening dark footage, sensor noise often appears. Add a mild **Spatial Noise Reduction** filter to clean up digital grain.

### How to Convert Low Quality Video to High Quality Online:
- Use neural AI upscalers (like CapCut AI Video Enhancer, Topaz Video AI, or TensorPix) to reconstruct missing pixel edges and transform low-bitrate 480p/720p footage into crisp 1080p and 4K masters.
