Recruiters search LinkedIn for people before they post jobs. A clear profile helps them find you and decide to message.

## Direct answer

To improve your LinkedIn profile, use a friendly clear photo, a headline that says what you do or want to do (with keywords), a short plain-language summary, specific bullets for projects and experience, and a handful of relevant skills. Add a short note when you connect with people, and share or comment occasionally so your name is seen.

## Profile checklist

### Photo and banner
A clear, friendly face, plain background, no group shots.

### Headline
Not just "Student". Say what you do and want:
- "Business student | Aspiring data analyst | Excel, SQL"
- "Customer service assistant | Calm under pressure | Seeking operations roles"

### Summary (About)
Three to five lines: who you are, what you are good at, what you want next.

> I'm a final-year business student who enjoys turning messy information into clear reports. I've built dashboards for a student society and volunteer as a peer tutor. I'm looking for an entry-level analyst role.

### Experience and projects
Use the pattern **verb + what + result.** Include volunteering and projects.

### Skills
List 5 to 10 relevant ones and ask people who've seen you work for endorsements.

### Education
Add school, dates and relevant modules.

## For students

Add societies, projects, volunteering and relevant coursework. See [how to write a CV with no experience](/blog/how-to-write-a-cv-with-no-experience).

## How to get noticed

1. Use keywords from job posts you want.
2. Turn on "open to work" if you want recruiters to see it.
3. Connect with alumni and people in your field, with a short note.
4. Comment thoughtfully on posts in your area.
5. Share what you are learning or building.

## Mistakes

- A blank About section
- A headline with only "Seeking opportunities"
- Typos
- Posting things you'd not want an employer to read
