## 1. Master the Golden Rule: Show, Don't Tell

Telling gives information; showing gives an experience. Instead of stating an emotion directly, paint physical sensory evidence:

```
❌ TELLING: "Sarah was terrified of the dark hallway."
✅ SHOWING: "Sarah pressed her back against the damp brick, holding her breath until her ribs ached as footsteps dragged across the floorboards above."
```

* **Engage All 5 Senses:** Don't just describe what characters see—incorporate temperature, texture, smells, and ambient sound.

---

## 2. Sentence Rhythm and Pacing Variation

Writing has musicality. If every sentence has the exact same 10-word length, the reader's brain falls asleep.

> *"This sentence has five words. Here are five more words. Five-word sentences are okay. But several together become monotonous. Listen to what happens when you vary the sentence length. You change the pace. You create rhythm. Music. A short sentence punches. A longer sentence takes the reader on an expansive journey through thought, feeling, and atmosphere."*

---

## 3. Subtext in Dialogue: What Characters Leave Unsaid

In real life, people rarely say exactly what they mean. Great dialogue relies on **subtext**—underlying desires and tension:

* **Tip:** Never have a character explain exposition in dialogue ("As you know, Bob, our company is going bankrupt...").
* **Exercise:** Write a scene where two characters argue about washing the dishes, but the subtext is their fear of breaking up.
