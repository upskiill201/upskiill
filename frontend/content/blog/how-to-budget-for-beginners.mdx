Most people think budgeting means a spreadsheet, a lot of willpower and giving up everything fun. It does not. A budget is a plan for your money, written down before you spend it, so the month does not run out before your money does.

This guide shows **how to budget for beginners** in seven steps. You need a pen, your last month of bank or mobile money history, and about an hour. ## Direct answer: how to start budgeting

To start budgeting, work out your take-home income, track your spending for a week, list your fixed costs, put needs first, give the rest a job (savings, spending, a buffer), and check your plan for ten minutes every week. Adjust it each month as real life shows you what it actually costs.

## Step 1: Know your real income

Use your **take-home** pay, the money that lands in your account after tax and deductions, not your salary on paper. If your income changes month to month, use your lowest recent month as the number you plan with. Anything extra becomes a bonus you allocate later.

## Step 2: Track a week of spending

You cannot plan what you cannot see. For seven days, write down every purchase, even a coffee. Better still, scroll through last month's bank or mobile money history and sort each line into a category. Most beginners are surprised by two or three categories that are much bigger than they thought.

## Step 3: List your fixed costs

Fixed costs are the bills that arrive whether or not you remember them: rent or housing, utilities, phone, insurance, transport passes, minimum debt payments. Add them up. This number is the floor under your budget.

## Step 4: Put needs before wants

Needs keep you housed, fed, healthy and able to earn: housing, basic food, transport to work or study, essential bills. Wants are everything that makes life nicer but could wait: eating out, subscriptions, upgrades. When money is tight, needs get paid first. Our guide to the [50/30/20 rule](/blog/50-30-20-rule-budget) shows how to give each group a share of your income.

## Step 5: Give every unit of money a job

Take your income, subtract your fixed costs, and split what remains into:

- **Everyday spending** (groceries, transport, small purchases), as a weekly limit
- **Savings** (even a small amount, to start an emergency buffer)
- **Fun money** you can spend guilt-free
- **A buffer** for things you did not predict

A weekly limit works better than a monthly one for everyday spending, because it is easier to see you are over on Thursday than on the 28th.

## Step 6: Plan for the costs that are not monthly

Annual fees, birthdays, school costs, repairs and holidays wreck budgets because they arrive from nowhere. Estimate them for the year, divide by twelve and set that amount aside each month. These small pots are often called sinking funds.

## Step 7: Check in for ten minutes every week

Pick a day. Compare what you planned with what happened, move money between categories if you need to, and look ahead to next week. This is the habit that makes budgets last.

## A simple monthly budget example

Imagine take-home income of **$2,000** a month:

| Category | Amount |
| :--- | ---: |
| Housing | $700 |
| Utilities and phone | $160 |
| Groceries | $300 |
| Transport | $150 |
| Insurance | $80 |
| Eating out and fun | $220 |
| Savings | $300 |
| Buffer | $90 |
| **Total** | **$2,000** |

Change the amounts to fit your life. What matters is that the total equals your income, so every dollar has a job. If you want a free copy to fill in, see our [monthly budget template](/blog/monthly-budget-template).

## Common beginner mistakes

- **Making it too strict.** A budget with no fun money is one you will quit by week three.
- **Forgetting small purchases.** Tracking for a week catches them.
- **Skipping the weekly check.** A budget you never look at is just a document.
- **Giving up after one bad month.** Every month teaches you what to change.

## The bottom line

Budgeting is not about restriction. It is knowing where your money goes so you can choose where it goes. Start with one week of tracking, build one simple page, and check it every week.
