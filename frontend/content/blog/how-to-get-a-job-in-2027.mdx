To **get a job in 2027**, choose one target role, close the skill gaps, build proof you can do the work, write applications that pass both software and humans, and prepare for interviews properly. The same plan works for a **career change in 2027**. Teyro's [How to Get Hired](/courses/how-to-get-hired) course walks through it in short daily lessons.

## Direct answer

The plan has six steps: **1. pick one target role**, **2. find your skill gaps**, **3. learn the gaps with daily practice**, **4. build proof** (projects, results, references), **5. apply smartly** (tailored CV, referrals, networking), **6. prepare for interviews**. Focus beats volume: ten tailored applications usually beat a hundred generic ones.

## Step 1: Pick one target role

"Any job in tech" is too vague to plan for. Pick a specific title, such as junior data analyst, frontend developer or project coordinator. Read 20 real job ads for it and note what keeps coming up. See [best careers for 2027](/blog/best-careers-2027) if you are still choosing.

## Step 2: Find your skill gaps

Make two lists: what the job ads ask for and what you can already do. The difference is your learning plan. Career changers usually have more transferable skills than they think, like communication, organisation and problem solving.

## Step 3: Learn the gaps with daily practice

Short, daily practice fits around a current job. Fifteen minutes a day adds up to more than 90 hours a year. Good starting points:

- Coding: [Python for Beginners](/courses/python-for-beginners)
- Data: [SQL and Databases for Beginners](/courses/sql-and-databases-for-beginners)
- AI at work: [AI at Work: Beginner](/courses/ai-at-work-beginner)
- People skills: [Clear Writing at Work](/courses/clear-writing-at-work)

## Step 4: Build proof

Employers trust evidence. Depending on the role, that could be:

- Two or three projects with a short write-up
- A portfolio site or GitHub profile
- Results from your current job, with numbers where you have them
- Freelance or volunteer work in the new field

## Step 5: Apply smartly

### Write a CV that passes screening

Many employers filter applications with software. Use a simple layout, standard section headings and the job ad's key terms where they truthfully describe you. Keep it to one or two pages.

### Use your network

Referrals often skip the filter completely. Tell people what role you are aiming for, ask for short conversations, and share what you are learning on LinkedIn. Teyro's [How to Get Noticed by Employers](/courses/how-to-get-noticed-by-employers) covers this.

### Track everything

Keep a simple sheet of where you applied, who you spoke to and what happened. Follow up after a week.

## Step 6: Prepare for interviews

1. Prepare five short stories about problems you solved, using situation, action and result.
2. Practise answering out loud, not just in your head.
3. Research the company and prepare two good questions.
4. Prepare to negotiate. The first offer is often not the best one.

[Acing Job Interviews](/courses/acing-job-interviews) and [Negotiation Skills for Beginners](/courses/negotiation-skills-for-beginners) help with these.

## A career change in 2027: extra tips

- **Bridge roles help.** Move sideways within your company or industry first.
- **Reframe your past.** A teacher has presentation and planning skills. A retail manager has people and numbers skills.
- **Be patient with titles.** A slightly lower title in a growing field can pay off within a couple of years.
