Zero-based budgeting sounds like you end the month with nothing. It means the opposite: **every unit of money has a job before the month begins.** Nothing is left to drift into random spending.

## Direct answer: what zero-based budgeting is

In a zero-based budget, your income minus all planned spending, saving and debt payments equals exactly zero. You list your income, then assign every unit to a category, including savings and a buffer. It is a plan, not a rule to spend everything.

## How to do it in five steps

1. **Write your take-home income** for the month.
2. **List fixed costs:** rent, utilities, phone, insurance, minimum debt payments.
3. **Add everyday costs:** groceries, transport, household items, using your tracked spending as a guide.
4. **Add savings and goals:** emergency buffer, a trip, a course, extra debt payments.
5. **Adjust until the difference is zero.** If you have money left, give it a job. If you are over, trim a category.

## A worked example

Take-home income of **$2,000**:

| Category | Amount |
| :--- | ---: |
| Rent | $700 |
| Utilities and phone | $160 |
| Groceries | $300 |
| Transport | $150 |
| Insurance | $80 |
| Fun | $200 |
| Emergency fund | $250 |
| Extra debt payment | $100 |
| Annual costs (set aside) | $60 |
| **Total** | **$2,000** |

Income $2,000 − planned total $2,000 = **$0.** Every dollar has a job.

## Zero-based vs 50/30/20

| | Zero-based | 50/30/20 |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Method | Assign every unit of money | Three percentage buckets |
| Effort | More | Less |
| Detail | High | Low |
| Best for | Tight or irregular budgets | A quick starting split |

Many people start with the [50/30/20 rule](/blog/50-30-20-rule-budget) and move to zero-based when they want more control.

## Tips that make it work

- Use a [monthly budget template](/blog/monthly-budget-template) so the totals update automatically.
- Include a small "miscellaneous" row. Real months never match the plan.
- If you are paid unevenly, plan with your lowest month and assign extra income as it arrives.
- Review weekly and move money between categories when needed. The plan is allowed to change.

## The bottom line

Zero-based budgeting is a simple idea with a big effect: money you have not assigned is money you will not remember.
