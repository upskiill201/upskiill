Most people accept the first number because asking feels awkward. A polite, prepared ask is normal and often works.

## Direct answer

To negotiate salary, research typical pay for the role, decide your target and your minimum, wait for the offer, then reply warmly with a specific number backed by your value. If they can't meet it, ask about other terms such as a signing bonus, leave or an early review. Get the final agreement in writing.

## Before you negotiate

1. **Research:** pay for similar roles in your place, from job posts and trusted salary sources.
2. **Know your numbers:** your target, your minimum, and your alternatives (another offer or staying put).
3. **List your value:** results, skills and what you'll bring.
4. **Practise aloud.**

## What to say for a job offer

> Thank you, I'm excited about the role. Based on my research and the experience I bring, I was hoping for around 62,000. Is there flexibility?

Then stop talking. Silence is part of negotiating.

## An email example

> Subject: Re: Offer for Marketing Coordinator
>
> Hi Priya, thank you for the offer. I'm very keen to join. Having looked at the role and similar positions, I'd like to ask whether we could agree a salary of 62,000, given my experience running campaigns for two years. Happy to talk it through. Best, Sam

Numbers are examples. Use your own research.

## For a raise

1. Collect your results from the past year.
2. Book a conversation with your manager.
3. Say what you've delivered and the figure you're asking for.
4. Ask what is needed if the answer is not now, and agree a date to review.

## If the answer is no

- Ask what would change it.
- Negotiate other items: bonus, leave, hours, training, title.
- Agree a review in three to six months.
- Decide whether the offer works for you.

## Mistakes

- Giving an exact number too early
- Apologising for asking
- Making ultimatums
- Accepting verbally and not checking the written offer
