If you must pay to receive a prize, it is not a prize.

## Direct answer

Advance fee fraud asks you to pay a fee first in return for a much larger reward that never arrives. Common versions include lottery prizes you never entered, inheritance claims from strangers, guaranteed loans with upfront fees and jobs that require you to pay for training or equipment.

## Common versions

- "You won a lottery. Pay a processing fee."
- "A relative left you money. Pay legal costs."
- "Your loan is approved. Pay the insurance first."
- "You got the job. Pay for your equipment."

## Warning signs

1. You did not enter or apply.
2. Payment is requested by gift card, crypto or transfer.
3. Urgency and secrecy.
4. Poor spelling or unofficial email addresses.

## What to do

- Stop communicating.
- Do not pay.
- Verify using official websites.
- Report it.
