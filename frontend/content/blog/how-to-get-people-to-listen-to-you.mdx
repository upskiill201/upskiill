If people don't listen, it's rarely because they're rude. It's usually because your message is long, unclear or doesn't seem to matter to them.

## Direct answer

To get people to listen, make one clear point and say it first, keep it short, connect it to what they care about, use a steady voice and pauses, and show you have listened to them. In meetings, speak early, hold your ground calmly if interrupted and follow up in writing.

## Why people tune out

- Too many points
- A long lead-up before the point
- No clear reason to care
- A flat or rushed voice
- They don't feel heard themselves

## 6 ways to be heard

1. **One point.** "The main thing is..."
2. **Point first.** Then reasons.
3. **Make it matter to them.** "This will save you two hours a week."
4. **Slow down and pause.** See [how to speak confidently](/blog/how-to-speak-confidently).
5. **Listen first.** People listen to those who listened to them.
6. **Ask for a response.** "What do you think?"

## In meetings

- Speak early.
- Say "I have one suggestion" to signal you'll be brief.
- If interrupted: "Let me finish this point, then I'd love your view."
- Follow up with a short written summary.

## Changing minds without pushing

Ask questions, acknowledge their view, then share your reasons. People change their minds more readily when they don't feel attacked.

## As a leader

Be consistent, explain the why and keep updates short. See [how to be a good leader](/blog/how-to-be-a-good-leader).
