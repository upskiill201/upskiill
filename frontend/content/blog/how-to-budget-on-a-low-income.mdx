"Save 20% of your income" is easy advice when you earn a lot. On a small income, rent and food already use almost everything. This guide shows **how to budget on a low income** without pretending you have money you do not.

The aim is not perfection. It is control: knowing where every unit of money goes so the month stops ending in a surprise. If you would like short daily lessons that are built for this situation, our [Budgeting for Beginners course](/courses/budgeting-for-beginners) covers it, free to start.

## Direct answer: how to budget on a low income

List your take-home income, then list essential costs first: housing, food, transport, utilities and minimum debt payments. Give everyday spending a weekly limit, keep a small amount for fun so you stick to the plan, and save whatever is realistic automatically, even a few dollars each payday. Review it weekly.

## Step 1: Use your real, lowest number

Use take-home income, after tax. If your pay varies, plan with your lowest recent month. Extra income then becomes a bonus you assign on purpose rather than money that disappears.

## Step 2: Find where it actually goes

Look at a month of bank or mobile money history. Sort every line into categories. On a low income, small leaks matter: delivery fees, transfer charges, subscriptions, snacks that add up. You do not need to cut everything. You need to see it.

## Step 3: Protect the essentials in order

When money is short, decide the order before the month starts:

1. Housing and utilities (so you keep your home and power)
2. Food
3. Transport to work or study
4. Minimum debt payments
5. Phone and essential bills

Everything else comes after these.

## Step 4: Give everyday spending a weekly limit

Divide your monthly food and everyday money into weeks. If you have $240 for food and small purchases in a month, that is about $60 a week. A weekly number makes overspending visible on Wednesday, not at the end of the month. Use cash or a separate account for it if that helps you stop at the limit.

## Step 5: Keep a little fun money

A low-income budget with no fun in it usually breaks. Choose a small, fixed amount for yourself and spend it without guilt. It is what keeps the rest of the plan alive.

## Step 6: Start a tiny buffer

Your first goal is not an investment. It is a small buffer so a broken phone or a missed shift does not become debt. Start with the cost of one or two weeks of food, then grow it slowly.

## Step 7: Make saving automatic

Move a small fixed amount the day you are paid. Even **$5 a week** is $260 in a year. Because it leaves before you see it, you do not have to rely on willpower.

## A low income budget example

Suppose take-home pay is **$1,200** a month:

| Category | Amount |
| :--- | ---: |
| Rent and utilities | $520 |
| Food (about $50 a week) | $210 |
| Transport | $90 |
| Phone | $20 |
| Minimum debt payment | $50 |
| Everyday and personal | $100 |
| Fun money | $60 |
| Savings | $50 |
| Buffer for surprises | $100 |
| **Total** | **$1,200** |

Your numbers will differ. The point is the shape: essentials first, a firm weekly food limit, a small amount for fun, and savings that happens automatically.

## Ways to free up money

- Cancel one subscription you do not use. Check the last three months.
- Compare phone, transport and insurance prices once a year.
- Plan a few cheap meals for the week before you shop.
- Ask about payment plans if a bill is due and you are short, before you miss it.
- Look for fee-free accounts or transfers if charges keep appearing.

## When your income is irregular

Plan with your lowest month. In a better month, fill your buffer first, then the next month's bills, then anything else. This smooths out the weak months.

## The bottom line

Budgeting on a low income is about control, not cuts. Cover the essentials, set weekly limits, keep a little fun, and save a small amount automatically.
