People use "leader" and "manager" as if they were the same job. They overlap, but the skills are different, and knowing which one a situation needs makes you better at both.

## Direct answer

**Management skills** organise work: planning, scheduling, budgeting, tracking and making processes run well. **Leadership skills** guide people: setting direction, communicating a purpose, motivating, giving feedback and developing others. Most roles need both, and both can be learned.

## Side by side

| | Management skills | Leadership skills |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Focus | Tasks, systems, results | People, direction, change |
| Question | "How do we get this done?" | "Where are we going and why?" |
| Examples | Planning, budgeting, scheduling, reporting | Communicating a vision, coaching, inspiring trust |
| Time frame | Often short and medium term | Often medium and long term |
| Risk if missing | Chaos, missed deadlines | Low motivation, drift |

A well-known distinction from the writer John Kotter is that management copes with complexity, while leadership copes with change.

## Examples

**Management:** you build the schedule for a product launch, assign tasks, track progress and report the budget.
**Leadership:** you explain why the launch matters, help a nervous teammate through their first presentation and agree how the team will work when pressure rises.

Both matter on the same project.

## Leadership skills vs traits

Traits are qualities such as confidence, curiosity or calm. Skills are abilities you can practise: giving feedback, delegating, running a good conversation. You can develop skills deliberately, which is why training works. See the [leadership skills list with examples](/blog/leadership-skills-list-and-examples).

## Can they be learned?

Yes, both. Few people are "natural" at everything. The approach is the same: choose one skill, practise in a real situation, ask for feedback, repeat.

## Which do you need?

- **New to a team lead role:** you need management basics (planning, priorities) and early leadership habits (listening, clear expectations). See [new manager tips](/blog/new-manager-tips).
- **Leading a project without authority:** leadership skills such as influence matter more. See [how to lead without being the boss](/courses/how-to-lead-without-being-the-boss).
- **Senior roles:** more direction, culture and change, with less hands-on management.

## How to build both

1. List three management skills and three leadership skills you use.
2. Choose the one weakest in your current role.
3. Practise it weekly for a month and ask for feedback.
4. Read [how to be a good leader](/blog/how-to-be-a-good-leader) for the daily habits.
