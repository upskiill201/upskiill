Most people are afraid of speaking in front of a group. Preparation and a clear structure take most of the fear away.

## Direct answer

For public speaking, decide the one message you want people to remember, structure your talk as opening, three points and a close, practise out loud several times, slow down, pause, and talk to the audience as if helping them. Nerves are normal and usually less visible than you think.

## Preparation

1. **One message.** If they remember one thing, what is it?
2. **Know your audience.** What do they care about?
3. **Structure:** opening hook, three points, closing action.
4. **Notes, not a script:** key words, not full sentences.
5. **Practise aloud,** at least three times, and time it.

## Structure that works

| Part | Purpose |
| :--- | :--- |
| Opening | Grab attention with a question, story or surprising fact |
| Three points | Each with a short example |
| Close | Repeat the message and give a clear next step |

## Delivery

- Slow down.
- Pause after key points.
- Make eye contact with friendly faces.
- Use your hands naturally.
- Vary your voice.

## Calming nerves

- Breathe out for longer than you breathe in.
- Arrive early and check the room.
- Drink water.
- Reframe nerves as energy.
- Focus on the audience, not on yourself.

## For students

Present to friends first, record yourself and ask for one thing to improve.

## If something goes wrong

Pause, breathe, continue. If you lose your place, glance at notes. It's fine.
