There are lots of SQL courses. A few checks will help you pick one you'll finish.

## Direct answer

Choose a SQL course that has you write queries in every lesson, covers `SELECT`, `WHERE`, grouping, joins and changing data, runs in your browser so there is nothing to install, and has a free way to start. Treat certificates as a bonus: practice on real data matters more.

## What to look for

1. **Hands-on in every lesson,** not just videos.
2. **A real dataset** that makes questions interesting.
3. **Clear order:** reading, filtering, grouping, joining, changing.
4. **Feedback** when your answer is wrong.
5. **Short lessons** you can fit in.
6. **A free start** so you can test it.

## Free vs paid

| | Free | Paid |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Good for | Trying SQL, building the habit | Deeper structure, recognised names |
| Watch out for | Only a free first module | Cost, long video lectures |

Terms change, so check what is free before you commit.

## Certificates

Some courses give certificates, free or paid. Employers value skill more, so keep a few queries you wrote and can explain. Teyro gives a verifiable certificate for each course you finish, which is Teyro's own and not accredited by a university or employer.
