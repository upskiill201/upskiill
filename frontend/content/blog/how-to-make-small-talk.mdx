Small talk feels pointless until you realise it's how people decide they like and trust each other.

## Direct answer

To make small talk, open with something you share (the place, event or day), ask an open question, really listen, follow up on what they say and offer a little about yourself. Keep it light, be curious, and end warmly. It's a skill you can practise.

## The FORD idea

Easy topics: **F**amily (careful), **O**ccupation, **R**ecreation, **D**reams. Choose what fits the person and setting.

## Step by step

1. **Open:** "How do you know the host?" or "Did you have a good weekend?"
2. **Ask an open question:** one that needs more than yes or no.
3. **Listen:** pick up a detail.
4. **Follow up:** "You mentioned running. What got you into it?"
5. **Share:** a little of yourself, then ask back.
6. **Close:** "I'm going to say hi to Sam. Great to meet you."

## At work

- Ask about the project, the weekend or a recent event.
- Remember small details and mention them later.
- Keep it positive.

## At events

- Arrive early when it's less crowded.
- Stand near food or drinks, where people chat.
- Join pairs and small groups with a smile.

## With strangers

Comment on the shared setting. Keep it light and friendly, and respect it if they don't want to chat.

## What to avoid

- Interrogating with questions
- Complaining
- Controversial topics early on
- Only talking about yourself
