Think of the colleague everyone asks for advice, who isn't their manager. That's informal leadership.

## Direct answer

Informal leadership is influence based on trust, skill and example rather than a job title. An informal leader mentors others, solves shared problems, keeps morale up and speaks up constructively, all without formal authority. It builds credibility and can lead to formal roles.

## Formal vs informal

| | Formal leader | Informal leader |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Source of influence | Job title, authority | Trust, expertise, example |
| Can assign work | Yes | No, must persuade |
| Accountable for | Team results | Their own contribution and the example they set |

## Examples at work

- Showing a new colleague how things really work
- Organising a fix for a recurring delay
- Running a shared document or checklist
- Calming a tense discussion
- Speaking up kindly when something is unfair
- Sharing credit

## Strengths and risks

**Strengths:** builds trust, helps teams, creates career evidence.

**Risks:** doing leadership work without recognition, or stepping on a manager's toes. Keep your manager informed and aligned.

## How to grow it

1. Be reliable.
2. Help others.
3. Listen first.
4. Take small initiatives.
5. Share credit.
