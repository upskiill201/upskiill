The data sits in between: some jobs are shrinking fast, most jobs are changing, and many new ones are being created. Knowing which is which gives you time to act. ## Direct Answer: What Jobs Will AI Replace?

AI and automation will mostly replace **routine, rules-based screen work**. According to the World Economic Forum's *Future of Jobs Report 2025*, the fastest-declining jobs through 2030 include **postal service clerks, bank tellers, data entry clerks, cashiers and ticket clerks, administrative assistants and executive secretaries**, with graphic designers appearing on the declining list for the first time. Most of these roles will shrink rather than disappear entirely.

| Job | Why It's At Risk | Projected Trend to 2030 |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Postal service clerks** | Digital communication, automated sorting | About -40% (WEF) |
| **Bank tellers and related clerks** | Digital banking, AI customer service | About -35% (WEF) |
| **Data entry clerks** | AI extraction and automated processing | About -34% (WEF) |
| **Cashiers and ticket clerks** | Self-checkout, digital ticketing | Large absolute decline (WEF) |
| **Administrative assistants and executive secretaries** | AI scheduling, drafting, summarising | Large absolute decline (WEF) |
| **Accounting, bookkeeping and payroll clerks** | Automated accounting software | Declining (WEF) |
| **Graphic designers** | Generative AI design tools | Newly declining (WEF) |
| **Computer programmers** | AI code generation | -7% in the US, 2025–2035 (BLS) |

## What Jobs Will AI Eliminate in the Next 5 Years?

"Eliminate" overstates what most forecasts say. What is happening is a **steady contraction** in roles dominated by routine tasks. The WEF report, based on a survey of more than 1,000 employers representing over 14 million workers, expects **92 million jobs to be displaced by 2030** while **170 million new ones are created**.

The at-risk jobs share a profile:

- **Routine:** the same kind of task, repeated
- **Rules-based:** clear rules determine the right output
- **Digital:** input and output are text, numbers or images on a screen
- **Low accountability:** errors are easy to catch and fix

If most of your working day fits all four, your role is likely to change significantly within five years.

## Why These Jobs Specifically?

### Clerical and Administrative Work

Data entry, scheduling, filing, invoice processing and form handling are exactly what modern AI does well: read documents, extract information and fill systems. These roles are expected to see the largest declines in absolute numbers.

### Banking and Retail Counters

Digital banking, self-checkout and AI chat support reduce the need for in-person transaction staff. This trend began before generative AI and is accelerating.

### Routine Creative Work

Graphic designers appearing on the WEF declining list reflects generative AI's ability to produce routine visuals quickly. High-end creative direction and brand work remain human-led, but production-level design work is under pressure.

### Routine Programming

BLS notes that "computer programming work continues to be automated" and that companies are using AI to automate repetitive programming tasks. At the same time, software developer jobs are projected to grow 10%. The work is shifting from typing code to designing and overseeing it. See [coding vs programming](/blog/coding-vs-programming).

## What Jobs Will Be Gone by 2030?

Very few jobs will be **completely** gone by 2030. Labour markets change more slowly than technology headlines suggest: adoption takes time, regulation varies, and many businesses keep human roles for customer preference or trust.

A realistic 2030 picture:

- **Sharp declines:** postal clerks, bank tellers, data entry clerks
- **Significant declines:** cashiers, administrative assistants, bookkeeping clerks, telemarketers
- **Transformed roles:** customer service, content writing, translation, graphic design, junior programming, paralegal work
- **Growing roles:** software development, data and AI, cybersecurity, healthcare, care work, green energy, skilled trades

---

## What Jobs Will Be Gone by 2050?

Honestly, nobody knows. Predictions 25 years out have a poor track record: in 2000, few predicted smartphones, app developers or social media managers.

Plausible candidates for major decline by 2050, if current trends continue:

- Most routine data processing and back-office work
- Basic customer service and call centres
- Many driving jobs, **if** autonomous vehicles become safe, legal and cheap at scale
- Routine warehouse work, as robotics matures
- Standardised translation and transcription

Just as important: **new jobs will emerge** that we cannot name yet. Every major technology shift, from electricity to the internet, destroyed some jobs and created many others. The WEF already projects a net gain of 78 million jobs by 2030.

## Is AI Going to Take My Job? A Quick Self-Check

Answer honestly:

1. Is more than half of my day spent on repetitive screen tasks?
2. Could someone write clear rules for most of what I do?
3. Is my work mostly producing text, numbers or images from other text, numbers or images?
4. Would a mistake in my work be easy to spot and fix?
5. Do I rarely need to build relationships or make judgement calls?

**Mostly yes:** your role is likely to change significantly. Start building new skills now.
**Mixed:** parts of your job will be automated; learn to use AI for those parts.
**Mostly no:** your role is relatively resilient, but learning AI will still make you more effective.

## How to Protect Your Career From AI

1. **Learn AI tools for your current role.** Become the person on your team who uses AI best.
2. **Move toward judgement.** Take on reviewing, deciding and owning outcomes.
3. **Add a technical skill.** Basic coding, data analysis or automation multiplies your value. Start with [how to teach yourself to code](/blog/how-to-teach-yourself-to-code).
4. **Build relationships.** Clients, colleagues and networks are hard to automate.
5. **Keep learning.** With 39% of core skills expected to change by 2030 (WEF), continuous learning is the real safety net.
