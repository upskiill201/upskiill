You may have been told you need "more executive presence". It sounds vague, but it comes down to a few visible habits.

## Direct answer

Executive presence is how you come across as credible, composed and ready for more responsibility. It shows in how you structure what you say, how you listen, how you handle pressure, and whether people trust you to follow through. It can be learned through practice, whatever your title.

## What it looks like

| Habit | Example |
| :--- | :--- |
| Point first | "I recommend we delay launch by a week. Here's why." |
| Concise | A 30-second answer instead of a 3-minute tour |
| Calm under challenge | "Good question. Let me think for a moment." |
| Listens | Summarises what others said before answering |
| Owns decisions | "I decided X because Y. If it fails, I'll fix it." |
| Follows through | Does what they said, by when they said |

## Building it

1. **Structure your message.** Point, reasons, ask.
2. **Slow down.** See [how to speak confidently](/blog/how-to-speak-confidently).
3. **Prepare for tough questions.** Write the three hardest ones and rehearse answers.
4. **Use stories.** One short, relevant example beats a list.
5. **Listen fully.** Presence includes attention.
6. **Dress and show up appropriately** for your setting.
7. **Be consistent.** Trust builds when you act the same under pressure.

## In meetings and interviews

Speak early, stay concise and ask good questions. See [how to ace a job interview](/blog/how-to-ace-a-job-interview).

## Not the same as

Being loud, formal or stern. Many strong leaders are quiet and clear.
