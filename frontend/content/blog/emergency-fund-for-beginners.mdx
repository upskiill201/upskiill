*This is general education, not personal financial advice.*

An emergency fund turns a crisis into an inconvenience.

## Direct answer

An emergency fund is money kept only for unexpected costs or a gap in income. A common rule of thumb is three to six months of essential expenses, but starting with a smaller target, like one week or one month of essentials, is fine. Keep it in a separate, easy-to-access savings account and build it with small automatic transfers.

## Why you need one

Without a buffer, a surprise bill goes on a credit card or loan, and costs more. A buffer reduces stress and debt.

## How much?

| Target | Good for |
| :--- | :--- |
| One week of essentials | First milestone |
| One month | A solid start |
| Three to six months | A common rule of thumb |

Essentials means rent, food, utilities, transport and minimum debt payments.

## How to build it

1. **Work out monthly essentials.**
2. **Pick a first target.**
3. **Open a separate account.**
4. **Automate a small transfer on payday.**
5. **Add windfalls:** tax refunds, gifts, side income.
6. **Top it up after you use it.**

## What counts as an emergency

A car repair needed for work, a medical bill, urgent travel, job loss. A sale or a holiday isn't.

## Where to keep it

Easy to reach but not too easy to spend. A separate savings account is typical. Check deposit protection in your country.
