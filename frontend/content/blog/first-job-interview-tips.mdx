Your first interview feels hard because you have no work history to point to. The good news is that employers hiring for entry roles know that.

## Direct answer

For a first job interview, show reliability, willingness to learn and clear examples from school, clubs, volunteering, family work or projects. Prepare short true stories, arrive early, dress neatly, listen carefully and ask a couple of questions. Employers hiring beginners are mostly judging attitude and potential.

## What they are really checking

1. Will you turn up and be reliable?
2. Can you communicate politely?
3. Will you learn and take feedback?
4. Do you really want this job?

## Where to find examples with no work history

- School or college group projects
- Clubs, teams, societies
- Volunteering
- Helping at a family business
- Caring for family members
- Selling or making things online

## Answers to prepare

- **Tell me about yourself:** one minute on who you are now and why this job.
- **Why this job?** Something specific about the role or place.
- **Strengths:** two, each with an example.
- **Teamwork:** a group project with your own role.
- **Availability:** be clear and honest.

See [common interview questions](/blog/common-interview-questions-and-answers).

## On the day

- Arrive about ten minutes early.
- Switch your phone off.
- Greet people politely and make eye contact.
- Listen to the whole question.
- Ask one or two questions, such as what a good first month looks like.

## What to avoid

- Saying "I have no experience" and stopping there
- Bad-mouthing a school, teacher or past employer
- Guessing answers you don't know instead of saying how you'd find out

## After the interview

Send a short thank you and then wait. If you are not chosen, ask for feedback politely.
