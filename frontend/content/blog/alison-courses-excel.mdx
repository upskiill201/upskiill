**Alison** offers genuinely free Excel courses covering formulas, formatting, functions, and pivot tables in a self-paced video format. Like most free self-paced platforms, the biggest obstacle isn't cost — it's finishing what you start.

## What's Actually Covered in Alison's Excel Courses?

Alison offers several distinct Excel courses at different levels:

- **Diploma in Microsoft Excel** — a comprehensive beginner-to-intermediate overview: formulas, cell formatting, charts, basic functions.
- **Excel Skills for Business** — more workplace-oriented, focused on spreadsheets used in actual business reporting.
- **Advanced Excel Functions and Formulas** — pivot tables, VLOOKUP/XLOOKUP, conditional formatting, data validation.

Each course is structured as short video modules followed by multiple-choice assessments. The content is genuinely practical for beginners, though production quality is more basic than paid platforms like LinkedIn Learning or Coursera.

## The Real Cost of "Free" on Alison

Here's what "free" actually means on Alison:

1. **The course itself** — no cost, no time limit, unlimited access to lesson content.
2. **Ads during lessons** — free accounts see advertising breaks, which can interrupt learning flow.
3. **The certificate** — this is where Alison makes money. Completing a course gives you a certificate of completion, but downloading, printing, or verifying it costs **$20–$40+** depending on format (digital PDF vs. physical mailed certificate).

For learners who genuinely just want the knowledge and don't need a certificate, Alison is legitimately free. For learners who want proof of completion for a CV or LinkedIn profile, factor in the certificate fee.

## The Completion Problem: Free Courses Are Easy to Abandon

Alison's biggest weakness isn't content quality — it's the same problem affecting nearly every free, self-paced course: **nothing brings you back**. There's no streak to protect, no daily reminder, no social accountability. You bookmark the course, get through module one, and life happens.

Industry data consistently shows **completion rates of 10–15%** for self-paced online courses, and free courses tend to sit at the lower end of that range since there's no financial sunk cost pushing you to finish.
