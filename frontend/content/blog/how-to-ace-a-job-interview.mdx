An interview is a conversation where the employer decides whether you can do the job and work well with them. The good news: preparation changes the result more than charm does.

## Direct answer

To ace a job interview, understand the role and company, prepare four or five true stories that show your skills (situation, what you did, result), practise saying them out loud, answer questions clearly and briefly, ask your own good questions and send a short thank you afterwards. Honest, specific stories beat rehearsed speeches.

## 10 tips

1. **Read the job post twice** and underline what they want.
2. **Research the company:** what they do, who they serve, recent news.
3. **Prepare stories** using situation, action, result. See [common interview questions](/blog/common-interview-questions-and-answers).
4. **Practise out loud,** ideally with a friend or by recording yourself.
5. **Plan the logistics:** route, time, tech for video calls. See [phone and video interview tips](/blog/phone-and-video-interview-tips).
6. **Arrive early** and be polite to everyone you meet.
7. **Listen to the whole question** and pause before answering.
8. **Be specific:** numbers, names of tools, what changed.
9. **Ask questions** about the team, what success looks like and next steps.
10. **Follow up** within a day.

## Answer structure

Use a simple pattern for behavioural questions:

- **Situation:** one sentence of context.
- **Action:** what *you* did.
- **Result:** what changed, with a number if possible.

## If you have no experience

Draw on school projects, volunteering, clubs and part-time work. See [first job interview tips](/blog/first-job-interview-tips).

## Nerves

Slow your breathing, take a sip of water, and it is fine to say "let me think for a moment". Most interviewers expect nerves.

## Be honest

Do not invent experience. If you don't know, say how you would find out. Honesty is easier to defend than a made-up story.
