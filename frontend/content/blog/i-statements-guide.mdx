"You always..." starts fights. "I feel..." starts conversations.

## Direct answer

An I statement describes your experience without blame. Use the formula: "When [situation], I feel [feeling], because [reason]. I would like [request]." It lowers defensiveness and makes your request clear.

## Examples

| Blaming | I statement |
| :--- | :--- |
| You never listen to me. | When I am interrupted, I feel unheard. I would like to finish my point. |
| You are always late. | When meetings start late, I feel stressed about the schedule. Could we start on time? |

## Mistakes

- Using "I feel that you...", which is blame in disguise.
- Over-explaining.
- Using it as a weapon.
