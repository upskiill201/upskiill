"Leadership skills" appears on every job description and CV, but what do they actually mean? Here is a practical **list of leadership skills with examples**, so you can recognise them, describe them and build them.

## Direct answer

Leadership skills are the abilities that help you guide and support people towards a goal. The most useful ones are communication, listening, decision-making, delegation, feedback, empathy, accountability, problem-solving, adaptability, time and priority management, motivating others and handling conflict.

## The 12 skills, with examples

### 1. Communication
**Example:** explaining a new project so everyone can say what it is and why it matters.
**Build it:** after any briefing, ask someone to explain it back to you.

### 2. Listening
**Example:** letting a teammate finish, then summarising their concern before answering.
**Build it:** in your next conversation, wait two seconds before you reply.

### 3. Decision-making
**Example:** choosing between two suppliers with incomplete information and explaining why.
**Build it:** write down the options, the risks and your reason for the choice.

### 4. Delegation
**Example:** handing a client report to a junior colleague with the deadline, the standard and your availability.
**Build it:** delegate the outcome, not the exact method.

### 5. Giving feedback
**Example:** "In the meeting you cut the slides to three and the client followed the plan. Do that again."
**Build it:** give one specific piece of feedback a week.

### 6. Empathy
**Example:** noticing a normally cheerful teammate is quiet and checking in privately.
**Build it:** ask "How are you finding the workload?" and listen.

### 7. Accountability
**Example:** saying "I missed that deadline, here is what I will change."
**Build it:** admit mistakes quickly and describe the fix.

### 8. Problem-solving
**Example:** finding the real cause of repeat delays, not just fixing the symptom.
**Build it:** ask "why" several times before choosing a solution.

### 9. Adaptability
**Example:** changing a plan when a key person leaves and explaining it calmly.
**Build it:** after any surprise, ask what you can control.

### 10. Priority and time management
**Example:** telling the team the three things that matter this week and what can wait.
**Build it:** choose a maximum of three priorities each week.

### 11. Motivating others
**Example:** linking a routine task to the customer it helps.
**Build it:** learn what each person cares about. See [how to motivate a team](/courses/how-to-motivate-a-team).

### 12. Managing conflict
**Example:** bringing two colleagues together to agree who owns what.
**Build it:** address issues early and privately. See [how to manage conflict at work](/courses/how-to-manage-conflict-at-work).

They overlap but are not the same. See [leadership skills vs management skills](/blog/leadership-skills-vs-management-skills).

## How to develop leadership skills

1. Pick one skill from the list.
2. Practise it in one real situation this week.
3. Ask someone you trust for feedback.
4. Repeat for a month, then pick the next skill.
