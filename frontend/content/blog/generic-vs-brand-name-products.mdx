You often pay for packaging and marketing, not for a better product.

## Direct answer

Generic and store-brand products are often much cheaper than name brands and are similar for many basics. Compare ingredients or active ingredients on the label, try a small pack first and keep brand names only where you can really tell the difference.

## Good places to go generic

- Pantry staples: flour, rice, pasta, sugar, tinned vegetables.
- Cleaning products and basic toiletries.
- Over-the-counter medicines with identical active ingredients, if appropriate for you.
- Batteries, bin bags and basic kitchen items.

## Where brands may be worth it

- Items where taste or texture matters to you.
- Products where performance really differs, like some detergents.
- Anything with a specific safety or medical requirement.

## How to test

1. Buy the generic and the brand once.
2. Compare side by side.
3. Switch where you cannot tell the difference.
