Many purchases begin with a feeling, not a need.

## Direct answer

Emotional spending is buying to change how you feel. Common triggers are stress, boredom, loneliness, tiredness and social comparison. You can stop it by recognising your triggers, adding a pause before you buy and replacing the buzz with other coping tools.

## Spot your pattern

For two weeks, note each non-essential purchase with:
- Time of day
- How you felt before
- What you bought
- How you felt a day later

Patterns usually show up fast.

## Ways to interrupt it

1. **Pause for 24 hours.**
2. **Name the feeling** out loud or in writing.
3. **Do something else** for 10 minutes, such as a walk or a call.
4. **Remove the easy route:** stored cards, apps, one-click checkout.
5. **Create a treat budget** so some spending is planned.

## When to get help

If spending feels out of control or leads to serious debt, speak to a doctor, counsellor or a free debt advice service in your country.
