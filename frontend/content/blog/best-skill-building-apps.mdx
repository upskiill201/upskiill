## How to Actually Evaluate a Skill-Building App

Feature lists don't tell you much — most apps in this category claim streaks, XP, and "bite-sized" lessons. What separates the good ones is harder to see from a landing page:

1. **Does every session require you to actually do something?** Passive video with a progress bar isn't skill-building — it's a course with extra steps.
2. **Is the session length honestly short?** Time a lesson yourself before trusting "bite-sized" marketing copy.
3. **Does the app admit its own limits?** A trustworthy skill-building app tells you where it stops being sufficient on its own (usually: real projects, deeper study, mentorship) rather than implying it alone gets you to mastery.
4. **Does progress compound, or does every lesson feel disconnected from the last?** Genuine skill-building apps build a coherent path, not a random grab-bag of unrelated exercises.

## One App Covering Multiple Skills vs. Several Specialized Apps

This is a real trade-off worth thinking through deliberately. Splitting your daily learning time across three separate apps — one for coding, one for a language, one for business — means maintaining three separate streaks, three separate habit triggers, and three separate places your motivation can quietly lapse. In practice, most people who try this end up consistent on one and slowly abandon the other two.

A single platform covering multiple skill tracks under one streak (Teyro's approach) avoids this fragmentation — you can shift from a coding track to a business fundamentals track without resetting your habit progress to zero, because the underlying streak and XP system doesn't care which skill you're actively building that week.

Specialized apps still make sense once you have a narrow, advanced goal — deep coding practice with a specific community, or serious math olympiad prep — where the depth of a single-purpose tool outweighs the convenience of consolidation.
