## 1. Skimming: Reading for the Gist

When you have 5 minutes to review a 20-page report before a meeting:

* **The Top-and-Tail Method:** Read the first two paragraphs completely, read the first sentence of every middle body paragraph, and read the final summary paragraph in full.
* **Inspect Visual Hierarchy:** Look at charts, bullet points, and callout boxes.

---

## 2. Scanning: The Human Search Engine

When taking timed reading tests (like IELTS, TOEFL, or CBSE board exams):

1. Read the question first and underline the **Target Keyword** (e.g. *"In what year was the telescope invented?"*).
2. Move your eyes in a zigzag or 'Z' pattern across the text without reading full sentences until your peripheral vision locks onto the keyword or numbers.
3. Once located, switch immediately to **Intensive Reading** for the surrounding 2 sentences to extract the answer.

---

## 3. Intensive vs. Extensive Reading: The Retention Engine

* **Intensive Reading:** Deconstructs syntax, unfamiliar vocabulary, and underlying logical arguments. Ideal for studying programming algorithms (see [Duolingo for Python](/blog/duolingo-for-python) and [how to learn business skills](/blog/how-to-learn-business-skills)).
* **Extensive Reading:** Builds background knowledge and cognitive endurance by exposing you to thousands of words across varied subjects without pausing to look up every dictionary definition.
