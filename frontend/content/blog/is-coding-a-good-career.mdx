Is coding a good career? In 2026, the honest answer is: yes, but it is a different career than it was five years ago. Salaries are still among the highest of any profession, and demand for skilled developers is projected to keep growing. At the same time, AI tools now write routine code, entry-level hiring is more competitive, and "I did a 12-week course" is no longer enough on its own. The winners are people who build real problem-solving ability. ## Direct Answer: Is Coding a Good Career?

**Yes, coding is still a good career in 2026**, especially for people who pair coding with problem-solving, design and domain knowledge. US software developers earned a median of **$135,980** in May 2025, and the US Bureau of Labor Statistics projects **10% job growth** from 2025 to 2035. Routine code-writing roles are shrinking as AI automates them, so skill depth matters more than ever.

| Factor | Verdict | Details |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pay** | Strong | Developer medians well above national averages |
| **Demand** | Good, but shifting | Growth in developers, data and AI; decline in routine programming |
| **Entry-level access** | Harder than 2021 | Portfolios and practical skills matter more |
| **Remote work** | Excellent | One of the most remote-friendly professions |
| **AI impact** | Transforming, not ending | Routine code automated; judgement more valuable |
| **Stress** | Moderate | Deadlines and bugs, but often flexible hours |

## How Much Do Coders Make?

US Bureau of Labor Statistics median annual pay, May 2025:

| Role | Median Pay | Projected Growth 2025–2035 |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Computer and information research scientists** | $140,300 | +22% |
| **Software developers** | $135,980 | +10% |
| **Data scientists** | $120,230 | +35% |
| **Software QA analysts and testers** | $104,300 | +6% |
| **Web and digital interface designers** | $104,000 | +6% |
| **Computer programmers** | $100,390 | -7% |
| **Web developers** | $92,650 | +4% |

A few things to keep in mind:

- **These are US medians.** Salaries in other countries are lower in absolute terms but often high relative to local averages, and remote work lets many developers earn from international companies.
- **Specialisation matters.** AI and machine learning, cloud infrastructure, security and backend roles usually pay more.
- **Experience matters most.** Senior developers often earn far more than juniors at the same company.

## Is Coding a High Income Skill?

Yes, and not only for full-time developers. Coding raises income in three ways:

1. **As a career:** developer, data and AI roles pay well above average.
2. **As a multiplier in your current job:** an analyst, marketer or accountant who can automate reports with Python becomes much more productive, which often leads to promotions.
3. **As side income:** simple websites, automation scripts and small tools are sellable freelance services. See [skills to learn to make money on the side](/blog/skills-to-learn-to-make-money-on-the-side).

What makes coding high-income is **leverage**: code you write once can run a million times. Few skills scale like that.

## Is Coding in High Demand?

Demand is strong but no longer uniform:

- **Growing:** software developers (+10%), data scientists (+35%), computer and information research scientists (+22%), according to BLS projections for 2025–2035.
- **Declining:** computer programmers (-7%), because "computer programming work continues to be automated" and companies use AI for repetitive programming tasks, per BLS.
- **Globally:** the World Economic Forum's *Future of Jobs Report 2025* lists software developers among the top five fastest-growing jobs by absolute numbers through 2030, alongside farmworkers, delivery drivers, construction workers and shop salespeople.

In short, the demand has shifted from "people who can type code" to "people who can solve problems with code." That is great news for learners who focus on real projects rather than memorising syntax.

## Is Coding Still Worth Learning With AI?

This is the question on everyone's mind. Our honest take:

**What AI changed:** AI assistants can now write boilerplate code, explain errors, generate tests and draft entire functions. Tasks that took a junior developer a day can take an hour.

**What AI did not change:** someone still has to understand the problem, judge whether AI-generated code is correct and secure, design how systems fit together, and take responsibility when things break. You cannot review code you do not understand.

**What that means for you:**

- Learn fundamentals deeply; they are how you catch AI's mistakes.
- Learn to work *with* AI tools, not around them.
- Build domain knowledge (finance, health, education, logistics) that makes your code valuable.
- Focus on skills listed in [skills that won't be replaced by AI](/blog/skills-that-wont-be-replaced-by-ai).

Coding is not dying. Low-skill coding is being automated, and high-skill coding is becoming more powerful.

## Is Programming a Stressful Job?

It varies more by company than by profession. Common stress sources:

- Deadlines and shifting priorities
- Production bugs affecting real users
- On-call rotations at some companies
- Keeping up with fast-moving tools

Common benefits:

- Flexible hours and remote options
- Deep, satisfying problem-solving work
- Clear progress and strong pay

Most developers describe the job as calm most of the time, with intense bursts. Choosing a team with a healthy culture matters as much as choosing the right technology.

## Can I Learn Coding on My Own and Get a Job?

Yes. Many developers are self-taught, and employers increasingly hire based on demonstrated skill. What you need:

1. **3 to 5 portfolio projects** that solve real problems (not just tutorial clones)
2. **A public GitHub profile** showing consistent work
3. **Working knowledge of one stack** (for example Python + Django + SQL, or JavaScript + React + Node)
4. **Git, testing and deployment basics**
5. **Interview practice**, explaining your code and your decisions

Timeline: typically 9 to 18 months of part-time study. See [how long does it take to learn coding?](/blog/how-long-does-it-take-to-learn-coding) and [self-taught vs bootcamp vs degree](/blog/self-taught-vs-bootcamp-vs-degree).

## Who Is Coding a Good Career For?

Coding tends to suit people who:

- Enjoy solving puzzles and figuring out why things do not work
- Are comfortable with being stuck for a while
- Like building things and seeing them used
- Are willing to keep learning throughout their career

It may not suit people who strongly dislike screen work, want a fixed set of skills that never changes, or are only interested in the salary. Pay alone rarely sustains someone through the frustrating early months.

The best way to find out is cheap: try it for 30 days. Fifteen minutes a day is enough to know whether you enjoy the process.
