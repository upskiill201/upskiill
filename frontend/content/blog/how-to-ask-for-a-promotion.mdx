Most promotions don't arrive by waiting. Asking well, with evidence, is a normal part of a career.

## Direct answer

To ask for a promotion, request a conversation with your manager, show the results you've delivered and the extra responsibility you already carry, name the role you want and ask what is needed to get there. Prepare specific examples, stay calm and agree next steps. If it is a no, ask for criteria and a date to review.

## Before you ask

1. **Check the bar.** What does the next level require?
2. **List your results.** Numbers, outcomes, feedback.
3. **Show you already work at the next level.** Projects, mentoring, bigger scope.
4. **Pick your moment.** After a win or near a review.

## What to say

> Thanks for meeting. I'd like to talk about my next step. Over the last year I led the onboarding redesign, which cut ramp-up time by two weeks, and I've been training new starters. I'd like to be considered for the senior role. What would you need to see from me to get there?

## Sample email to request a meeting

> Subject: Chat about my development
>
> Hi Priya, could we find 30 minutes this week to talk about my career path and the senior analyst role? I'd like to share what I've delivered this year and hear your view on next steps. Thanks, Sam

## If the answer is no

- Ask what skills or results are missing.
- Agree on a timeline to review again.
- Write it down and follow up.
- If there is no path, look elsewhere with your new evidence.

## Mistakes

- Comparing yourself to colleagues
- Giving ultimatums
- Talking about how long you've been there instead of what you've done
- Asking without evidence
