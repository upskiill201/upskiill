Getting hired is a sequence of small steps. Do them in order and the process becomes manageable.

## Direct answer

To get hired: choose a target role, make your CV, cover letter and online profile clear and specific, find openings (including ones not advertised), speak to people, prepare short true stories for interviews, follow up, and negotiate calmly. Employers hire people who show they can do the work, so show proof.

## The steps

### 1. Choose a target
Pick one or two job types. A focused search beats a vague one.

### 2. Build your documents
- A CV matched to the role: [how to write a CV with no experience](/blog/how-to-write-a-cv-with-no-experience)
- A cover letter for the important ones: [how to write a cover letter](/blog/how-to-write-a-cover-letter)
- A clear LinkedIn profile: [LinkedIn profile tips](/blog/linkedin-profile-tips-for-job-seekers)

### 3. Find openings
Use job boards, company pages and your network. See [how to find a job](/blog/how-to-find-a-job).

### 4. Talk to people
Referrals and conversations open doors. See [how to network for a job](/blog/how-to-network-for-a-job).

### 5. Prepare for interviews
Have stories ready about what you did and what changed. See [how to ace a job interview](/blog/how-to-ace-a-job-interview).

### 6. Follow up
Send a thank you within a day and check in politely if you hear nothing.

### 7. Handle a no
Ask for feedback, learn and keep going.

### 8. Negotiate the offer
Ask questions, consider the whole package and respond in writing when you can.

## No experience yet?

Start with proof. See [how to get a job with no experience](/blog/how-to-get-a-job-with-no-experience).

## What employers notice

- A CV that matches the role
- Specific results and examples
- A polite, prompt reply
- Preparation for the company and the role
