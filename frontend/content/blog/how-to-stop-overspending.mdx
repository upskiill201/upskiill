Overspending is rarely about one big purchase. It's lots of small ones you didn't notice.

## Direct answer

To stop overspending, find where your money goes, set a limit for each category, wait before non-essential purchases, remove easy ways to buy (saved cards, shopping apps), and make saving automatic so there's less left to spend. Review weekly and be kind to yourself when you slip.

## 9 steps

1. **Track a month** of spending. See [how to budget for beginners](/blog/how-to-budget-for-beginners).
2. **Find the leaks:** subscriptions, takeaways, small daily buys.
3. **Set category limits,** and use a separate account or card for them.
4. **Pay yourself first.** Move savings out on payday.
5. **Use a pause.** Wait 48 hours for non-essentials. See [how to stop impulse buying](/blog/how-to-stop-impulse-buying).
6. **Remove friction toward spending:** delete saved cards, unsubscribe from sales emails.
7. **Know your triggers:** stress, boredom, social media.
8. **Plan fun spending** so you don't rebel against your budget.
9. **Review weekly.**

## Common areas

### Credit cards
Only spend what you can repay in full. Check statements often.

### Food
Plan meals, use a list and set a takeaway limit. See [how to budget for groceries](/blog/how-to-budget-for-groceries).

### Clothes
Wait before buying, and ask "do I have something similar?"

## If it feels out of control

Overspending can be linked to stress or conditions such as ADHD. Helpful strategies include automation and limits. If it's affecting your life, consider talking to a healthcare professional or a free money advice service.
