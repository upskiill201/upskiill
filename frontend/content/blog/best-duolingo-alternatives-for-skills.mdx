## Why Comparing on "Features" Misses the Point

Most comparison posts list features — streaks, XP, offline mode — as if they're independently interesting. They're not. The entire value of the Duolingo model rests on one behavioral outcome: **do you actually open the app tomorrow.** Every feature only matters insofar as it protects that outcome. So the comparison below is organized around the things that actually predict whether you'll still be using the app in week six, not week one.

### 1. Does It Cover More Than One Skill?

This matters more than it looks. If you're only ever going to learn one thing, a narrow app is fine. But most people who abandon courses aren't abandoning a single subject — they're abandoning the *habit* of daily learning altogether. A platform where your streak covers coding today and a business fundamentals track next month means the habit survives even when your specific goal shifts. That's the structural advantage Teyro leans on: one login, one streak, many skill tracks, so switching what you're learning doesn't reset your progress to zero.

### 2. Is the Daily Session Actually Short?

Every app in this space claims "bite-sized." Test it: open a lesson and time it. Anything routinely over 15 minutes will lose to a busy Tuesday, no matter how good the content is. Mimo and Duolingo itself are the most disciplined about this; check any new app against that bar before trusting the marketing copy.

### 3. Do You Practice, or Just Watch?

The single biggest quality gap between apps in this category is whether each lesson makes you *do* something — write a line of code, answer a scenario, make a choice — versus just watch a video and tap "continue." Passive lessons dressed up with a streak counter don't actually solve the completion problem; they just delay it. Teyro's Learn → Apply → Reflect → Deepen structure exists specifically to force an active step into every session, not just the ones that feel convenient to build.

### 4. What Happens When You Get Good at the Basics?

Duolingo's honest limitation is that it doesn't turn you into a functional, fluent speaker on its own — you eventually need immersion and real conversation. The same ceiling exists for every app here. The best ones are transparent about it and design an off-ramp: Teyro's Deepen phase and project-oriented tracks are built to bridge from "I finished the lesson" to "I can actually use this," rather than pretending the app alone gets you to mastery.
