## 1. Deconstructing Conversational Reciprocity (The Ping-Pong Framework)

One of the most common challenges for individuals on the autism spectrum is **monologuing**—speaking at length about a favorite special interest without realizing the listener has lost focus.

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│             THE CONVERSATIONAL PING-PONG FORMULA            │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│  [Step 1: Receive]   ──> Listen actively & identify 1 hook  │
│  [Step 2: Acknowledge]─> Validate: "That makes total sense" │
│  [Step 3: Contribute] ──> Share 1 brief relevant thought (20s)│
│  [Step 4: Pass]      ──> Return the ball with an open question│
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### The 20-Second Rule
Keep your initial response under 20 to 30 seconds. If the other person asks follow-up questions, continue; if they give short one-word replies, pass the conversation back to them with an open question (e.g., *"How did you get interested in that?"*).

## 2. Deciphering Nonverbal Cues and Tone

Nonverbal communication accounts for over 60% of interpersonal context. Here are explicit indicators to watch for:

* **Open Engagement Cues:** Feet pointed toward you, nodding, relaxed shoulders, leaning in slightly.
* **Disengagement Cues:** Checking a phone or watch, scanning the room for exits, angled torso turning away, flat monotone responses.
* **Healthy Eye Contact Alternative:** If direct eye contact feels overwhelming, look at the bridge of the person's nose or between their eyebrows—to the listener, it appears completely natural.

## 3. Workplace & Group Social Skills

In professional settings, social competence is closely tied to psychological safety, clear task handoffs, and diplomatic boundary setting.

1. **Clarifying Ambiguity:** Instead of guessing vague instructions, use scripts like: *"To ensure I deliver exactly what you need, let me recap the 3 main deliverables..."*
2. **Navigating Office Small Talk:** Use the **FORD Framework** to ask easy, low-pressure questions about hobbies, weekend plans, or project progress.
3. **De-escalating Misunderstandings:** Practice active reframing: *"I want to make sure I understand your perspective—are you suggesting we change the project deadline?"*
