*This is general education, not personal financial advice.*

Money results come from repeated small choices. Habits do the heavy lifting because they don't rely on willpower.

## Direct answer

Good money habits include spending less than you earn, tracking where money goes, saving automatically on payday, keeping an emergency fund, avoiding expensive debt and pausing before big purchases. Habits that hold people back include spending everything you earn, ignoring your numbers, relying on high-interest credit and chasing quick wins.

## Habits that help

| Habit | How to start |
| :--- | :--- |
| Track spending | Review your last month once a week |
| Pay yourself first | Set up an automatic transfer on payday |
| Emergency fund | Start with one small goal. See [emergency fund for beginners](/blog/emergency-fund-for-beginners) |
| 48-hour rule | Wait two days before non-essential purchases. See [how to stop impulse buying](/blog/how-to-stop-impulse-buying) |
| Avoid costly debt | Pay more than the minimum when you can |
| Review subscriptions | Cancel what you don't use |
| Increase savings with pay | When income rises, save part of the raise |

## Habits that hold you back

- Spending the whole raise (lifestyle creep). See [lifestyle creep](/blog/lifestyle-creep)
- Ignoring the numbers
- Using credit for everyday spending
- Buying to feel better
- Falling for [scams and schemes](/blog/common-financial-scams)

## Making habits stick

1. Start tiny.
2. Attach it to something you already do.
3. Automate it.
4. Track it.
5. Reward consistency, not amount.
