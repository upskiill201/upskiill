Attackers often find it easier to trick a person than to hack a system.

## Direct answer

Social engineering is manipulating people into revealing information, sending money or giving access, by using trust, urgency, fear or authority. Typical forms are phishing, pretending to be someone official or building a story that makes you want to help. The defence is to slow down and verify through a separate channel.

## Common tactics

- **Pretexting:** a believable story to get information.
- **Urgency:** "do this now or lose access".
- **Authority:** "I am from the bank or police".
- **Reciprocity:** a small favour followed by a request.
- **Fear:** "your account will be closed".

## How to protect yourself

1. **Pause** whenever you feel rushed.
2. **Verify identity** through a number or site you already trust.
3. **Do not share** passwords or codes.
4. **Report** suspicious attempts.
