The thrill of shopping often peaks before the purchase, not after.

## Direct answer

Dopamine is a chemical linked to motivation and anticipation. Seeing a desirable item, a discount or a "limited time" offer can trigger anticipation, which feels good and pushes you to act. That rush often fades soon after buying, which is why impulse purchases can disappoint.

## How shopping uses your reward system

- **Anticipation:** adding to cart can feel as good as owning.
- **Novelty:** new things are exciting, especially online.
- **Scarcity:** "only 3 left" creates urgency.
- **Variable rewards:** you never know what deal you will find, which keeps you browsing.

## How to take back control

1. **Add a delay:** wait 24 to 48 hours before buying.
2. **Move the thrill:** try a walk, a call with a friend or a hobby.
3. **Remove cues:** delete shopping apps and unsubscribe from marketing emails.
4. **Use a wish list** instead of checking out.
5. **Notice the feeling,** and ask what you actually need right now.
