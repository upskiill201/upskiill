Hundreds of people can apply to the same job. Getting noticed is about being clear, specific and easy to say yes to.

## Direct answer

To get noticed by employers, show proof you can do the work, send a CV and cover letter tailored to the job, keep a findable LinkedIn profile, contact people directly instead of only applying online, and follow up politely. Specific evidence beats general adjectives.

## 8 ways to get noticed

1. **Know what they want.** Read the post closely and list the top five needs.
2. **Show proof.** A small project, a result, a portfolio page. See [self-taught skills that get you hired](/blog/self-taught-skills-that-actually-get-you-hired).
3. **Tailor your CV.** [How to write a CV with no experience](/blog/how-to-write-a-cv-with-no-experience).
4. **Write a short cover letter.** [How to write a cover letter](/blog/how-to-write-a-cover-letter).
5. **Fix your LinkedIn.** [LinkedIn profile tips](/blog/linkedin-profile-tips-for-job-seekers).
6. **Contact a human.** A short, specific message to someone on the team. [How to network for a job](/blog/how-to-network-for-a-job).
7. **Make it easy.** Clear subject lines, sensible file names, correct links.
8. **Follow up.** One polite check-in after about a week.

## Ideas for proof

- A mini project that solves a real problem
- A one-page plan for how you'd improve something the company does
- Volunteering with a measurable outcome
- A short course with a task you completed

## What gets CVs skipped

- Typos
- No link to the job's keywords
- Walls of text
- Missing contact details
