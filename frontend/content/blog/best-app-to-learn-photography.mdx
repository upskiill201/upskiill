## The Concepts You Actually Need First

A lot of beginner photography content jumps straight into gear talk — which lens, which camera body — before covering the concepts that make any camera work well. The foundational stuff, in order:

1. **The exposure triangle** — aperture, shutter speed, ISO, and how they trade off against each other.
2. **Composition principles** — rule of thirds, leading lines, framing, negative space.
3. **Light** — understanding direction and quality of light matters more than gear in almost every case.
4. **Basic editing** — exposure correction, white balance, cropping — the digital darkroom equivalent of a good print.
5. **Genre-specific technique** — portraits, landscapes, street photography each add their own nuances once you have the basics.

These first three or four concepts are genuinely learnable through short, quiz-and-scenario style lessons, because they're rule-based enough to test directly: "which shutter speed freezes motion," "which composition choice draws the eye where," and so on. That's exactly the kind of content that gamified formats handle well — you learn the rule and immediately apply it to an example, instead of watching 20 minutes of a photographer narrating their process.

## Why Watching Tutorials Isn't the Same as Learning

There's a specific trap in photography education: watching someone edit a beautiful photo feels like learning, but it mostly teaches you to recognize good editing, not to do it yourself. The skill only forms when you make the same decisions yourself and get it a little wrong before getting it right.

This is where Teyro's Learn → Apply → Reflect → Deepen structure is useful for a skill like photography. Learn introduces a concept (say, how aperture affects depth of field), Apply has you predict or choose the right setting for a scenario, Reflect connects it back to a photo you might take this week, and Deepen goes further into the technical "why" if you're curious. It's a much smaller time investment than a full tutorial, but it forces the same recall a real shooting situation would.

## A Realistic Path From Zero

- **Week 1**: Learn the exposure triangle and basic composition rules in short daily sessions. Shoot with whatever camera you have — phone is fine.
- **Week 2**: Add light and basic editing concepts. Start a habit of taking 10-20 photos a day with intention, even mundane ones.
- **Week 3-4**: Pick a genre you're drawn to (portraits, street, landscape) and focus your shooting there. Review your own photos critically against the composition and exposure concepts you've learned.
- **Ongoing**: Join a critique community or share work with someone whose opinion you trust. This is the step apps can't replace — real feedback on real photos.
