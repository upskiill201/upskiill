The line between needs and wants isn't always clear, and that's exactly why it matters for money.

## Direct answer

Needs are what you must have to live and work safely: food, shelter, utilities, basic clothing, essential health care and transport to work. Wants make life nicer but you can live without them. A simple test: "What happens if I don't have this?" If nothing serious, it's a want.

## Examples

| Need | Want |
| :--- | :--- |
| Groceries | Takeaway and snacks |
| Rent or mortgage | A bigger home than you need |
| Phone with calls and data | The newest model |
| Warm clothing | Brand-name fashion |
| Transport to work | A car upgrade |
| Basic internet | Many streaming subscriptions |
| Medicine | Cosmetic products |

## The grey areas

Many things are a need in basic form and a want in upgraded form. Ask: what is the basic version, and what am I paying extra for?

## A simple test

1. **What happens without it?**
2. **Is there a cheaper way to meet the same need?**
3. **Will I still care about it in a month?**
4. **Does it match what I value?**

## For students

Needs: tuition, books, groceries, transport. Wants: eating out often, extra subscriptions, new gadgets. See [how to budget as a student](/blog/how-to-budget-as-a-student).

## Use it in your budget

Cover needs first, then give wants a set amount. See [50/30/20 rule](/blog/50-30-20-rule-budget).

## Watch out for creep

As income grows, wants quietly turn into "needs". See [lifestyle creep](/blog/lifestyle-creep).
