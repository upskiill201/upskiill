## Why Duolingo Works — And Where It Stops Working

Duolingo solved a real problem: how to get hundreds of millions of people to practice something voluntarily, every day, for years. The mechanism is proven and powerful:

- **Streaks** make you feel loss-averse about skipping ("I can't break my 30-day streak")
- **Short sessions** remove activation energy (5 minutes feels doable even on terrible days)
- **XP and levels** make invisible progress visible
- **Daily reminders** keep the habit top-of-mind

These mechanics work because they exploit fundamental behavioral psychology — and they work for **any** subject matter. But Duolingo chose to apply them almost exclusively to language learning. The gap isn't in the mechanism; it's in the subject scope.

### Where Duolingo's Scope Becomes a Limitation

If your goals include any of these, Duolingo cannot help:

- **"I want to learn to code"** — Duolingo's coding expansion (Duolingo Math) is basic arithmetic, not programming
- **"I want to understand AI"** — No AI literacy track whatsoever
- **"I want to improve business skills"** — No business fundamentals, strategy, or analytics
- **"I want to communicate better at work"** — No professional communication, presentation, or writing skills

Duolingo is a language learning app that happens to use gamification. Teyro is a skill-building platform that uses the same gamification for a much broader purpose.
