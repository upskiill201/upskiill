"Which 3 jobs will survive AI?" became one of the most searched career questions of the past year, largely thanks to a viral claim attributed to Bill Gates. The honest answer is more hopeful and more useful than a list of three: far more than three kinds of jobs will survive, but almost every job will change, and the people who thrive will be the ones who keep learning. Here is what the viral claim says, what the data actually shows, and which jobs are most likely to still be around in 10 years.

---

## Direct Answer: Which Jobs Will Survive AI?

A viral claim attributed to Bill Gates names **coders, energy experts and biologists** as the three jobs that will survive AI, but its exact source is unclear and no serious forecast limits survival to three jobs. Data from the World Economic Forum and the US Bureau of Labor Statistics shows growth in **technology, healthcare, care work, skilled trades, education and green energy** roles through the next decade.

| Job Category | Why It's Resilient | Example Roles |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Healthcare** | Physical care, trust, accountability, ageing populations | Nurses, nurse practitioners, physiotherapists |
| **Skilled trades** | Hands-on work in unpredictable physical spaces | Electricians, plumbers, HVAC technicians |
| **Care and social work** | Human connection and empathy | Social workers, counsellors, care workers |
| **Education** | Mentorship, motivation, human relationships | Teachers, coaches, trainers |
| **Tech and AI** | Building, directing and overseeing AI | Software developers, data scientists, security analysts |
| **Green energy** | Physical infrastructure and policy complexity | Solar and wind technicians, energy engineers |
| **Leadership** | Accountability and complex judgement | Managers, founders, strategists |

## The Viral "3 Jobs" Claim, Explained

The claim, widely reported in 2025 and again in 2026, says Gates believes only three professions will withstand AI:

1. **Coders**, because humans will still be needed to build, debug and improve AI systems.
2. **Energy experts**, because the field is too complex and high-stakes to automate fully.
3. **Biologists**, because scientific discovery relies on creativity and intuition that AI supports but cannot replace.

**Our assessment:** the reasoning behind each is sensible, and all three are reasonable examples of resilient work. But the framing, "only three jobs will survive," does not match any credible labour-market forecast, and the original source of the quote is unclear. Treat it as a conversation starter, not a career plan.

## What the Data Actually Says

### World Economic Forum: Future of Jobs Report 2025

Based on a survey of more than 1,000 employers representing over 14 million workers:

- **170 million new jobs** are expected to be created by 2030
- **92 million jobs** are expected to be displaced
- That is a **net gain of 78 million jobs**
- **39% of workers' core skills** are expected to change by 2030

The fastest-growing jobs in absolute numbers include **farmworkers, delivery drivers, software developers, building construction workers and shop salespeople**, with strong growth in **care jobs** like nursing, social work and counselling. Technology roles, such as big data specialists and AI and machine learning specialists, are among the fastest-growing by percentage.

### US Bureau of Labor Statistics: Projections 2025–2035

| Occupation | Projected Change | Median Pay (May 2025) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Data scientists | +35% | $120,230 |
| Computer and information research scientists | +22% | $140,300 |
| Software developers | +10% | $135,980 |
| Software QA analysts and testers | +6% | $104,300 |
| Computer programmers | -7% | $100,390 |

Notice the split inside tech itself: routine programming is shrinking while software development, data science and research are growing. AI is not wiping out tech jobs. It is reshaping them toward judgement and design.

## What Jobs Will AI Never Replace?

"Never" is a strong word, and no job is untouched by AI. But some types of work are highly resistant to full automation because they depend on things AI struggles with:

### 1. Physical Work in Unpredictable Environments

Electricians, plumbers, mechanics and construction workers operate in messy, variable physical spaces. Robotics is advancing, but replicating a skilled tradesperson's adaptability in a century-old house is far harder than generating text.

### 2. Human Care and Trust

Nurses, therapists, social workers and care workers provide something people want from humans: empathy, presence and trust. AI can assist with records and diagnosis support, but the relationship is the job.

### 3. Accountable Judgement

Judges, surgeons, senior engineers and executives make decisions where someone must be responsible. Society and law require a human to be accountable, even when AI informs the decision.

### 4. Leadership and Persuasion

Leading teams, negotiating, building relationships and motivating people remain deeply human.

### 5. Novel Creativity and Discovery

Scientists, researchers and inventors pose new questions. AI accelerates discovery, but humans still decide what is worth discovering.

## What Jobs Will Still Be Around in 10 Years?

Based on the direction of current data, these are strong bets for 2036:

| Field | Roles Likely to Grow or Persist |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Technology** | Software developers, data scientists, AI engineers, cybersecurity analysts, cloud engineers |
| **Healthcare** | Nurses, nurse practitioners, physiotherapists, home health aides, mental health counsellors |
| **Skilled trades** | Electricians, plumbers, HVAC technicians, wind and solar technicians |
| **Education** | Teachers, corporate trainers, instructional designers, coaches |
| **Care** | Childcare workers, eldercare workers, social workers |
| **Business** | Managers, product managers, sales professionals with strong relationships |

The common thread: jobs survive when humans provide something AI cannot fully provide, or when the job involves **directing and checking AI**.

## Will AI Take Over Coding Jobs?

It is ironic that coders appear on the "survivors" list while coding is one of the most AI-affected fields. Both are true:

- **Routine code writing is being automated.** BLS projects computer programmer jobs to decline 7% from 2025 to 2035.
- **Software development is growing.** BLS projects 10% growth for software developers over the same period.

The difference is scope. People who only type code from specifications are exposed. People who solve problems, design systems and review AI-generated code are in demand. See [is coding a good career?](/blog/is-coding-a-good-career) and [coding vs programming](/blog/coding-vs-programming).

## How to Make Your Career AI-Resilient

1. **Learn to use AI in your field.** People who use AI well will outperform people who do not.
2. **Build human skills deliberately.** Communication, leadership, negotiation and judgement.
3. **Deepen domain expertise.** AI is generic. Your knowledge of a specific industry is not.
4. **Take on accountable work.** Own outcomes, not just tasks.
5. **Learn continuously.** With 39% of core skills expected to change by 2030, learning is the real job security.
