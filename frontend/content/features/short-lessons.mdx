## Why 15 Minutes Is the Sweet Spot

### Attention Science

Research on spaced learning shows that knowledge is encoded into long-term memory most effectively when study sessions are **short and spread out** — not long and crammed. A 15-minute session produces more lasting learning than a 2-hour marathon because your brain has time to consolidate between sessions.

### Willpower Economics

Starting is the hardest part. A 15-minute commitment feels achievable even on your worst day. A 60-minute lecture feels like a chore. The difference is not in the content — it's in the activation energy required to start. Teyro's lesson format is designed to make starting feel effortless.

### Real-Life Scheduling

You have 15 minutes:
- Between meetings
- During your commute
- While waiting for coffee
- Before bed

You probably don't have a 2-hour block on a Tuesday afternoon. Teyro works with your actual schedule, not the fantasy of an uninterrupted study block.

## The Science Behind Short Lesson Retention

### Spaced Repetition

Short, daily sessions naturally create the spacing effect — information reviewed at increasing intervals is retained 50–80% better than massed practice. Teyro's daily missions are essentially spaced repetition built into the format.

### Active Recall

Each mission forces you to retrieve information (not just recognize it). Active recall produces significantly stronger memory traces than passive review. The 15-minute format is ideally suited for retrieval practice because it matches the attention window for focused retrieval.

### Interleaving

Teyro's diverse skill tracks (coding, business, AI, communication) mean you naturally interleave different subjects. Research shows interleaving — mixing related topics — produces better long-term retention than blocking (studying one topic exhaustively before moving on).
