## Why are Teyro lessons so short?

Because the lesson you actually do beats the perfect lesson you put off. Most online courses fail in week two, not on day one — an hour of video is easy to start and easy to postpone. A lesson that takes a few minutes has no good excuse attached to it.

Short does not mean shallow. Each lesson covers **one idea**, and makes you use it before you move on.

## What happens inside a lesson?

Every Teyro lesson runs in the same four steps:

| Step | What you do | Why it matters |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Learn** | Meet one new idea, explained simply | One idea at a time keeps it clear |
| **Apply** | Use it straight away, with instant feedback | Doing it is what makes it stick |
| **Reflect** | Put it in your own words | Explaining it shows you understood |
| **Deepen** | Optional extras when you want more | Curious learners can keep going |

The steps are covered in more detail on [immediate practice](/features/immediate-practice) and [reflection](/features/reflection-prompts).

## How much time should I spend each day?

You set a daily goal when you start — from about **5 minutes (Casual) to 20 minutes (Intense)** — and you can change it any time in settings. The right goal is the one you would still hit on your worst day. Most people do better starting small and adding lessons when they feel like it than setting a big goal and breaking it.

## What stops short lessons from feeling pointless?

Progress you can see. Each lesson earns XP, moves your [streak](/features/streaks-and-freeze) forward, counts towards three [daily quests](/features/daily-chests) and pushes you up the [weekly league](/features/leagues-leaderboards). Small sessions add up on screen, so they feel like they add up in your head too.

If you have struggled to finish courses before, [why you never finish online courses](/blog/why-you-never-finish-online-courses) explains the pattern this is designed to break.
