## The Watch-Then-Do Trap

### Why Watching Isn't Learning

There's a seductive trap in passive learning: watching someone solve a problem makes your brain feel like you understand it. The concept is familiar, so your brain rates it as "learned." But familiarity is not competence. You can watch 20 coding tutorials and still not be able to write a basic function.

This phenomenon is called the **illusion of competence** — mistaking recognition for recall. Teyro eliminates this illusion by requiring immediate practice: you can't proceed in a mission until you've actually solved the problem yourself.

### The Delayed Practice Problem

Most platforms separate instruction from practice by 10–30 minutes of video. By the time you reach the exercise, you've forgotten half of what you just watched. The context has faded, the examples are gone, and you're essentially starting from scratch.

Immediate practice eliminates this decay. You practice while the instruction is still fresh — within seconds, not minutes. This tight feedback loop is the single most powerful driver of retention.

## How Teyro Structures Immediate Practice

Every Teyro mission follows the 4-phase cycle, with practice starting in Phase 2:

1. **Learn** (2–4 min) — A concept introduced through interactive explanation, not a lecture
2. **Apply** (5–8 min) — You're given a problem to solve immediately, using what you just learned
3. **Reflect** (2–3 min) — You check your understanding with a quick self-assessment
4. **Deepen** (1–2 min) — You move to a slightly harder challenge that builds on the same concept

The "Apply" phase starts within 3 minutes of starting the lesson. There's no long video to sit through. You learn by doing, with guidance available when you're stuck.

## Immediate Practice by the Numbers

### Retention Rates

Studies comparing passive and active learning consistently show dramatic differences:

- **Passive learning** (watching, reading): ~10–20% retention after 1 week
- **Active practice** (doing, problem-solving): ~60–75% retention after 1 week
- **Teaching others**: ~90% retention (Feynman Technique)

Teyro's immediate practice model places learners in the 60–75% range by default — and the teaching element comes naturally when you complete enough missions to internalize the material.

### Time Efficiency

A learner who watches 30 minutes of video and then practices for 10 minutes retains less than someone who spends 15 minutes doing practice exercises with brief explanations interspersed. Teyro's format is more time-efficient for actual skill development, even though the clock says "less time."
