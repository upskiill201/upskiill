## The Udemy Model: Why It Fails Most Learners

Udemy's pitch is seductive: "Buy any course for under $20, learn anything, anytime." The catalog is genuinely staggering. If you want a course on a specific, narrow topic — say, "Salesforce reporting dashboards for retail" — Udemy probably has one. For that specific need, Udemy is an excellent resource.

But Udemy's model creates three structural failure points:

### 1. The Purchasing Paradox

Buying a course triggers a dopamine response — the same reward your brain seeks from the learning itself. You get the satisfaction of purchasing without doing any work. This means the purchase itself can substitute for actual learning, creating a cycle where you keep buying new courses to get the "I'm learning" feeling, without finishing old ones.

### 2. The Duration Problem

Most Udemy courses are 10–40 hours of video content. That's a significant time commitment that requires sustained, uninterrupted focus — a resource most working adults don't have in sufficient quantity. A 30-hour course you watch 30 minutes per week takes 30 weeks to complete. Over 7 months, with a 50% dropout rate after the first few weeks, the math is brutal.

### 3. The Isolation Problem

Udemy courses are self-paced with no external accountability. There's no streak to break, no community to impress, no instructor checking progress. You're alone with your motivation — which, as behavioral science confirms, is the least reliable learning mechanism for anything beyond short bursts.
