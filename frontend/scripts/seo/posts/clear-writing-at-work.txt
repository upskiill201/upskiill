=== active-vs-passive-voice
title: Active vs Passive Voice: How to Choose for Clear Writing
desc: Active voice names who does the action, passive voice hides or delays it. Learn the difference, examples, when passive is fine and how to fix it.
tags: active vs passive voice, active voice, passive voice, passive voice examples, clear writing at work
rel: plain-language-writing-guide
q: What is the difference between active and passive voice? | In active voice the subject does the action. In passive voice the subject receives it.
q: Is passive voice wrong? | No. It is useful when the doer is unknown or unimportant, but overuse can make writing vague.
q: How do I spot passive voice? | Look for a form of "to be" plus a past participle, as in "was approved".
---
Who did what? Active voice answers it.

## Direct answer

In active voice, the subject performs the action: "The team approved the plan." In passive voice, the subject receives it: "The plan was approved by the team." Prefer active voice for clear, direct writing, and use passive when the actor is unknown or unimportant.

## Examples

| Passive | Active |
| :--- | :--- |
| The report was written by Sam. | Sam wrote the report. |
| Mistakes were made. | We made mistakes. |
| The server was restarted. | Ops restarted the server. |

## When passive is fine

- The doer is unknown: "The window was broken."
- The action matters more: "Your order has been shipped."
- Diplomacy: "The deadline was missed" (use sparingly and honestly).

## How to fix it

1. Find "was", "were", "been" plus a verb.
2. Ask who did it.
3. Move that person to the front.

## The bottom line

Active voice is usually shorter, clearer and more accountable.

Write a clear subject line, lead with your main point, keep it short, be polite, check names and attachments, avoid unnecessary reply-all and end with a clear next step. Re-read before sending.

3. Put the point in the first line.

8. Attach files you mention.

12. Proofread.

Clear, kind, short.

=== bottom-line-up-front-writing
title: Writing Summaries at Work: Put the Main Point First

tags: writing summaries, executive summary, how to write a summary at work, summary template, concise reports
rel: bluf-bottom-line-up-front
q: What is an executive summary? | A short overview at the start of a document that gives the main points, findings and recommendations.
q: How long should it be? | Usually a paragraph or a few bullets.
q: What should it include? | The purpose, key findings, recommendation and next step.
---
Busy readers read the top. Make it count.

## Direct answer

A work summary should give the conclusion, the key reasons and the next step in a few lines at the top. Write it last, then check that someone who reads only the summary could act.

## A three-line format

1. **What:** the conclusion or recommendation.
2. **Why:** two or three key reasons.
3. **Next:** what you need and by when.

## Example

"We should switch to the new supplier. It is 12 percent cheaper and delivers two days faster. Approval is needed by 30 June to avoid renewal fees."

## Tighten a draft

- Cut introductions.
- Delete repeated points.
- Replace long phrases with short ones.

## The bottom line

If the reader stops after three lines, they should still know what to do.

q: Should I edit and write at the same time? | Better to draft first, then edit.
---
First drafts are for ideas. Edits are for readers.

## Direct answer

To edit well, draft first, leave it, then edit in passes: structure, clarity, brevity and finally typos. Read aloud, cut filler, replace long words with short ones and make sure each paragraph has one idea.

## 12 tips

1. Write first, edit later.
2. Let it rest.
3. Read aloud.
4. Cut the first paragraph if it only warms up.
5. Delete "very", "really", "just".
6. Prefer short words.
7. Use active voice.
8. One idea per paragraph.
9. Split long sentences.
10. Replace jargon with plain words.
11. Check facts and numbers.
12. Proofread last.

## A pass system

| Pass | Focus |
| :--- | :--- |
| 1 | structure and order |
| 2 | clarity |
| 3 | brevity |
| 4 | typos |

## The bottom line

Writing is rewriting.

A readability tool is a mirror, not a boss.

## Direct answer

Readability tools such as Hemingway Editor flag long sentences, complex words, adverbs and passive voice, and estimate a reading grade. Use them to spot problems, then decide for yourself. Aim for clear sentences, not a perfect score.

=== concise-writing-tips
title: Concise Writing: 15 Ways to Cut Words Without Losing Meaning
desc: Fifteen practical ways to write concisely at work: replace phrases, cut filler, merge sentences and use lists, with before and after examples.
tags: concise writing, how to write concisely, cut words, brevity in writing, wordy phrases
rel: editing-tips-for-writers, active-vs-passive-voice
q: What is concise writing? | Writing that uses only the words needed to deliver a clear message.
q: Does shorter always mean better? | No. Keep what the reader needs. Cut what they do not.
q: What are common wordy phrases? | "In order to", "due to the fact that" and "at this point in time".
---
Every extra word is a small tax on the reader.

## Direct answer

Write concisely by cutting filler words and phrases, replacing long phrases with short ones, using active voice, removing repetition and using lists for steps. Check that each sentence earns its place.

## Replacements

| Wordy | Concise |
| :--- | :--- |
| in order to | to |
| due to the fact that | because |
| at this point in time | now |
| a large number of | many |
| has the ability to | can |
| in the event that | if |

## 15 tips (short list)

1. Cut introductions.
2. Delete "very" and "really".
3. Prefer verbs to noun phrases.
4. Use active voice.
5. Merge repeated sentences.
6. Use bullets for lists.
7. Limit sentences to one idea.
8. Remove "I think" where you are sure.
9. Replace jargon.
10. Use numbers.
11. Cut qualifiers.
12. Remove redundant pairs ("each and every").
13. Start with the point.
14. Delete the last sentence if it repeats.
15. Read aloud.

## The bottom line

Write the thought, then trim the extras.

q: How do I spot my own errors? | Take a break, read aloud, change the font or read backwards.

A small error can undermine a big message.

## Direct answer

To proofread, wait before checking, read slowly or aloud, check names, numbers, dates and links and look for correct words used wrongly. Use tools as help, not as the last line of defence.

## Checklist

- [ ] Names and titles spelled correctly
- [ ] Numbers, dates and currencies right

Slow down for the last check.

q: What is tone of voice in writing? | The attitude your words convey, such as friendly, formal, urgent or neutral.

q: Can text sound rude by accident? | Yes. Without voice and expression, short messages can feel cold.
---
The same facts can sound helpful or harsh.

## Direct answer

Tone is how your message sounds to the reader. To keep it clear and kind, greet people, say please and thanks, be specific rather than blunt and read your message from the reader's point of view before sending.

## Examples

| Harsh | Friendly and clear |
| :--- | :--- |
| Send it now. | Could you send it by 3pm today? Thanks. |
| You did this wrong. | I noticed the figures differ from the template. Could we check them together? |
| Why was this late? | Can you help me understand what delayed this, so we can plan better? |

## Tips

1. Match the formality to the reader.
2. Be direct without being sharp.
3. Avoid sarcasm in text.
4. Read it aloud.

## The bottom line

Be clear about the ask and kind about the delivery.
