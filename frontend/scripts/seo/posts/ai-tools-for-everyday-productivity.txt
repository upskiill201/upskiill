Notes you do not have to type are notes you are more likely to keep.

## Direct answer

AI note-taking tools can transcribe spoken words, summarise long text, extract action items and make notes searchable. Review the output for accuracy, ask permission before recording others and check where your data is stored and who can access it.

## What they do well

- Turn voice notes into text.
- Summarise long articles or lecture notes.
- Pull out tasks and deadlines.
- Search across notes by meaning.

## Tips for better notes

1. Give a short instruction: "Summarise in five bullet points with action items."
2. Check names, numbers and dates.
3. Keep a human edit step before sharing.
4. Store important notes in a place you control.

## Privacy checklist

- Do you have consent to record?
- Does your organisation allow the tool?
- Where is the data stored and for how long?
- Is your data used to train models?

## The bottom line

Great for saving time. Always check accuracy and privacy.

tags: ai meeting summaries, ai meeting notes, summarize meeting with ai, meeting action items ai, ai for meetings
rel: ai-note-taking-tools, ai-for-meetings
q: Can AI summarise a meeting? | Yes, from a transcript or recording it can produce a summary, decisions and action items.
q: What should I check? | Names, numbers, dates and whether decisions were captured correctly.
q: Do I need consent? | Often yes. Tell participants and follow your organisation's rules and local laws.
---
The value of a meeting is what happens after it.

## Direct answer

To get a useful AI meeting summary, start with a transcript or recording, ask for decisions, action items with owners and deadlines and open questions, then check the result before sending it. Get consent to record and follow your workplace policy.

## A prompt that works

"Summarise this meeting transcript. List: 1) decisions made, 2) action items with owner and deadline, 3) open questions, 4) a three-sentence summary. Do not add information that is not in the transcript."

## Workflow

1. Get consent and record or transcribe.
2. Paste or upload the transcript.
3. Use the prompt above.
4. Review for accuracy.
5. Send within a few hours.

## Common issues

- Misattributed speakers.
- Missing context.
- Invented details, so check anything important.

## The bottom line

Short, accurate, owner-assigned notes make meetings worthwhile.

=== custom-gpts-guide
title: Custom GPTs and Custom Assistants: How to Build One for Your Work
desc: Custom assistants let you save instructions and knowledge for repeated tasks. Learn what to include, how to test them and privacy points to check.
tags: custom gpts, custom gpt, build a custom assistant, ai assistant instructions, reusable ai prompts
rel: chatgpt-prompts-for-beginners
q: What is a custom GPT or assistant? | A version of an AI chatbot configured with your own instructions, and sometimes files, for a specific task.
q: Do I need to code? | Generally not. You write instructions in plain language.
q: How do I make a good one? | Define its role, audience, steps, format and rules, then test with real examples.
---
If you keep pasting the same instructions, you need a saved assistant.

## Direct answer

A custom assistant stores your instructions, and sometimes reference files, so you can reuse them for a repeated task, such as drafting customer replies in your tone. Write clear instructions covering role, audience, steps, output format and rules, test with real examples and keep sensitive data out unless your policy allows it.

## What to put in the instructions

1. **Role:** "You help draft replies to customer emails."
2. **Audience and tone:** friendly, concise, plain language.
3. **Steps:** read the message, identify the question, draft a reply.
4. **Format:** greeting, answer, next step.
5. **Rules:** do not promise refunds, ask if information is missing.

## Test it

Try five real examples, note failures and refine the instructions.

## Keep it safe

- Do not include confidential information unless allowed.
- Check sharing settings.
- Update it when your process changes.

## The bottom line

A well-written instruction is a reusable shortcut. Test it before you trust it.
