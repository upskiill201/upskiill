A famous idea with a complicated evidence trail.

## Direct answer

Power posing, standing in an expansive pose to feel more confident, became popular after early studies claimed hormone and behaviour changes. Later studies did not replicate those effects, though some people report feeling more confident. Treat it as a harmless warm-up, not a proven fix. Preparation and practice matter more.

## What does seem to help

1. **Rehearse** out loud.
2. **Breathe slowly** before speaking.
3. **Stand tall and open** because it helps your voice and presence.
4. **Know your first sentence.**
5. **Reframe nerves** as energy.

## A short pre-talk routine

- Two minutes of slow breathing.
- Roll your shoulders and relax your jaw.
- Say your opening line once quietly.

## The bottom line

Good posture helps, but preparation is the real power move.

q: What is the best replacement? | A short pause.
---
The pause feels longer to you than to your audience.

## Direct answer

To reduce filler words, slow down, prepare your key points and replace "um" with a silent pause. Record yourself to notice your patterns, practise out loud and have a friend signal when you use them. You will not remove them all, and that is fine.

## Steps

1. **Record** a minute of speech and count them.
2. **Slow your pace.**
3. **Plan your first sentence** of each point.
4. **Pause** instead of filling.
5. **Practise** in low-stakes settings.
6. **Review** progress weekly.

## Tips

- Know the next idea before finishing the current one.
- Breathe at the end of sentences.
- Do not obsess. Clear content matters more.

## The bottom line

A confident pause beats a filler word.

Use pauses before and after important points, between ideas and after questions. A one to two second pause gives listeners time to think and makes you sound more confident. Practise by marking pauses in your notes and rehearsing aloud.

- After an important sentence.
- Before a surprising fact.
- After a question.
- When changing topic.

4. Record and listen.

Pause to let your point land.

=== speaking-with-authority

tags: speaking with authority, speak with authority, sound authoritative, confident speaking, credible speaker
rel: what-is-executive-presence, how-to-speak-confidently
q: How do I sound more authoritative? | Speak clearly at a steady pace, use plain words, lead with your conclusion and avoid apologising or hedging unnecessarily.
q: Does a deep voice help? | Not as much as clarity, pace and confidence.
q: Can I be authoritative and kind? | Yes. Authority comes from competence and clarity, not harshness.
---
Authority is clarity delivered calmly.

## Direct answer

Speak with authority by leading with your conclusion, using plain language, keeping a steady pace, pausing, making eye contact and avoiding unnecessary hedging. Be honest about what you do not know. Authority comes from clarity and competence, not volume.

## 8 habits

1. **Lead with the point.**
2. **Use plain, specific words.**
3. **Slow down.**
4. **Pause.**
5. **Cut hedges** like "just" and "kind of".
6. **Keep your voice steady,** finishing sentences downward.
7. **Prepare** your key facts.
8. **Admit uncertainty** simply and say how you will find out.

## Example

Instead of "I just think maybe we could possibly look at...", say "I recommend we do X because Y."

## The bottom line

Say what you mean clearly, then stop.

---
The questions are where credibility is won.

## Direct answer

For Q&A, listen to the whole question, pause, repeat or rephrase it if the room needs it, answer briefly and check you have addressed it. If you do not know, say so and promise a follow-up. Stay calm with hostile questions.

## Steps

1. **Prepare** the ten hardest questions.
2. **Listen** fully without interrupting.
3. **Pause** before answering.
4. **Answer in one to three sentences,** then offer detail.
5. **Bridge** back to your main message.
6. **Close** with a summary.

## Tricky situations

| Situation | Response |
| :--- | :--- |
| Do not know | "I do not know. I will find out and get back to you by Friday." |
| Hostile | acknowledge, answer the fact, move on |
| Rambling question | "Let me check I have it: you are asking about X?" |

## The bottom line

Be honest, brief and calm.

---
A good structure is a road map your audience can follow.

## Direct answer

Structure a talk with a hook, a clear main message, two or three supporting points with evidence, a summary and a specific call to action. For busy business audiences, state your conclusion first and then explain.

## Template

1. **Hook:** a question, story or surprising fact.
2. **Main message:** one sentence.
3. **Point one,** with evidence.
4. **Point two,** with evidence.
5. **Point three,** with evidence.
6. **Summary:** repeat the message.
7. **Call to action:** what should they do next?

## Tips

- Write the main message first.
- One idea per slide.
- Rehearse the transitions.

## The bottom line

Tell them what you will say, say it, then say what to do.

## Direct answer

To speak up in meetings, prepare one or two points beforehand, speak early before the conversation hardens, use a clear opener like "I have a suggestion", build on others' ideas and stay calm if interrupted. You can also share ideas in writing before the meeting.

## Tips

1. **Prepare a point** and a question.
2. **Speak in the first ten minutes.**
3. **Use signposts:** "Two quick points."
4. **Reference others:** "Building on Sam's idea..."
5. **Handle interruptions:** "Let me finish this point."
6. **Follow up in writing.**

## If you are not invited to speak

Ask the chair for a moment: "Could I add something?"

## The bottom line

Prepared voices are heard. Start small and build the habit.

=== bluf-bottom-line-up-front
title: BLUF: Bottom Line Up Front, With Examples for Emails and Meetings
desc: BLUF means putting your conclusion or request first. Learn how to apply it in emails, reports and meetings with before and after examples.
tags: bluf, bottom line up front, bluf writing, bluf email examples, concise communication
rel: communicating-with-executives, plain-language-writing-guide
q: What does BLUF stand for? | Bottom Line Up Front: putting the main point first and the detail after.
q: Where does BLUF come from? | It is widely associated with military communication, and is now common in business writing.
q: Does BLUF suit every message? | It suits most work messages. For sensitive news, you may add context first.
---
Readers should not have to hunt for the point.

## Direct answer

BLUF means starting with your conclusion, decision or request, then giving the supporting detail. It saves readers time and makes your message clear. Use it in emails, reports and meetings.

## Before and after

**Before:**
"Hi, I have been looking at last quarter's numbers and there are a few things I wanted to mention about the budget..."

**After:**
"We need an extra 5,000 for the launch. Here is why, and what I recommend."

## How to apply

1. Write your main point in one sentence.
2. Put it first.
3. Add three supporting points.
4. End with the next step.

## Email template

**Subject:** Decision needed by Friday: venue for the offsite
**Body:** I recommend Venue B. It fits our budget and has availability. Please approve by Friday. Details below.

## The bottom line

Say the point first. Explain second.

q: What is upspeak? | Rising at the end of statements so they sound like questions. Practise ending statements with a steady or falling tone.
---
Your voice carries your message and your confidence.

## Direct answer

A confident voice comes from steady breathing, a calm pace, clear articulation and finishing sentences firmly. Practise breathing exercises, read aloud, record yourself and slow down. Avoid trailing off and unnecessary upward inflection.

## Exercises

1. **Breathe:** inhale for four counts, exhale for six.
2. **Hum and relax** your jaw before speaking.
3. **Read aloud** for five minutes daily.
4. **Record and listen.**
5. **Practise endings:** finish with a clear, steady tone.

## Habits

- Drink water.
- Warm up before big talks.
- Speak from standing when you can.

## The bottom line

Slow, steady and clear sounds confident.
