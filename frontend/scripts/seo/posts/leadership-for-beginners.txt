## Direct answer

Common leadership styles include authoritative (sets direction), democratic (seeks input), coaching (develops people), pacesetting (leads by high standards), affiliative (builds harmony) and servant (puts the team first). The best leaders choose the style to fit the situation and the people.

## Quick guide

| Style | Works well when | Risk |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Authoritative | a clear vision is needed | can feel top-down |
| Democratic | you need buy-in | slow decisions |
| Coaching | developing people | takes time |
| Pacesetting | skilled, motivated team | burnout |
| Affiliative | repairing trust | avoids hard feedback |
| Servant | building long-term teams | may be slow in a crisis |

## How to adapt

1. Read the situation: urgency, skill and trust.
2. Ask what the team needs.
3. Change style when circumstances change.

## The bottom line

Learn several styles and use the right one at the right time.

q: What are the downsides? | It can neglect details and processes, and depends on the leader's energy.

## Direct answer

Transformational leadership inspires people with a shared vision, models integrity, encourages new thinking and supports individuals' growth. It is effective in times of change, but works best with attention to detail, process and wellbeing.

## Four components

1. **Inspirational motivation:** a clear, exciting vision.
2. **Idealised influence:** being a role model.
3. **Intellectual stimulation:** challenging assumptions.
4. **Individual consideration:** coaching and caring about people.

## How to practise

- Explain the why behind goals.
- Invite ideas and challenge respectfully.
- Coach people individually.
- Match words with actions.

## Limits

Vision needs execution. Pair it with clear plans and accountability.

## The bottom line

Inspire, support and deliver.

q: What is situational leadership? | A model that says leaders should adjust their style based on the follower's competence and commitment for a specific task.
q: What are the four styles? | Directing, coaching, supporting and delegating.
q: Does the same person always need the same style? | No. A person may need direction on a new task and delegation on a familiar one.
---
One size does not fit all, even for the same person.

## Direct answer

Situational leadership says to match your style to the person's readiness for the specific task. Use directing for beginners, coaching for developing people, supporting for capable but unsure people and delegating for skilled, confident people.

## The four styles

| Readiness | Style | What you do |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| New, enthusiastic | directing | give clear instructions |
| Learning, some doubts | coaching | explain and encourage |
| Capable, variable confidence | supporting | listen and build confidence |
| Skilled and confident | delegating | give ownership |

## How to use it

1. Think of a task and a person.
2. Assess skill and confidence for that task.
3. Choose the style.
4. Adjust as they grow.

## The bottom line

Meet people where they are, then help them move on.

q: What is the difference between a leader and a manager? | Managers plan, organise and control work. Leaders set direction and inspire people. Good managers often do both.

You can manage tasks, but you lead people.

## Direct answer

Management is about organising work: planning, budgeting, tracking and solving problems. Leadership is about direction and influence: setting a vision, inspiring and developing people. Most roles need both, in different proportions.

## Comparison

| | Manager | Leader |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Focus | tasks and systems | people and direction |
| Asks | how and when? | what and why? |
| Tools | plans, processes | vision, trust |
| Authority | position | influence |

## Building both

**Management skills:** planning, delegation, prioritising, meetings.
**Leadership skills:** communication, coaching, vision, integrity.

## The bottom line

Great teams need clear direction and good organisation.

## Direct answer

A one-on-one is a regular private meeting between a manager and a team member. Hold it weekly or fortnightly for about 30 minutes, let the employee lead the agenda, ask open questions, give feedback and note actions. Do not cancel it casually.

## A simple agenda

1. **How are you?** (5 minutes)
2. **Their topics.** (10 minutes)
3. **Progress and blockers.** (10 minutes)
4. **Feedback both ways.** (5 minutes)
5. **Agree actions.**

## Good questions

- What is going well?
- What is getting in your way?
- What would you like more or less of from me?
- What do you want to learn next?

## Mistakes

- Using it only for status updates.
- Cancelling often.
- Talking more than listening.

## The bottom line

Regular, protected time shows people they matter.
