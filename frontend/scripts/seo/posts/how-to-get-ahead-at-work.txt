=== how-to-ask-for-a-raise
title: How to Ask for a Raise: Preparation, Timing and Scripts
desc: How to ask for a raise: gather evidence, research the market, choose timing, make a clear request and plan for the answer, with a script.
tags: how to ask for a raise, asking for a raise, ask for a pay rise, raise request script, salary increase
rel: how-to-ask-for-a-promotion, negotiating-with-your-boss
q: When should I ask for a raise? | After a success, at a review or before budgets are set, not during a crisis.
q: How much should I ask for? | A researched figure supported by market data and your results.
q: What if they say no? | Ask what would need to change and agree a timeline for review.
---
A good case, calmly made.

## Direct answer

To ask for a raise, collect evidence of your results, research market pay, choose a good time, request a meeting, state your ask with reasons and listen. If the answer is no, agree on what would change it.

## Script

"I would like to discuss my compensation. Over the past year I achieved X and Y, and market data suggests a range of Z. I would like to move to [figure]."

## Steps

1. List achievements with numbers.
2. Research pay ranges.
3. Pick the moment.
4. Make the ask.
5. Follow up in writing.

## The bottom line

Ask clearly, with proof, and keep the relationship positive.

=== career-ladder-explained
title: Career Ladder vs Career Lattice: Understanding Your Path at Work
desc: A career ladder moves up. A lattice moves across too. Learn the difference, how to plan your path and why skills matter more than titles.
tags: career ladder, career lattice, career path planning, career growth paths, career development
rel: career-growth-tips, how-to-get-ahead-at-work
q: What is a career ladder? | A path of promotions within one field or organisation, each with more seniority.
q: What is a career lattice? | A path that includes lateral moves, new skills and different roles, not only upward steps.
q: Which is better? | It depends on your goals. Many people benefit from combining both.
---
Progress is not only upward.

## Direct answer

A career ladder is a series of promotions upward. A career lattice allows sideways and diagonal moves to build skills and experience. Many careers combine both. Plan around skills and impact, not only job titles.

## Planning your path

1. **Decide what you want** to learn and do.
2. **Look at roles** you admire and their skills.
3. **Find gaps.**
4. **Seek projects** that build them.
5. **Talk to your manager** and mentors.

## The bottom line

Skills open doors. Titles follow.

=== impact-statements
title: Impact Statements: Describe Your Work in Results for CVs and Reviews
desc: Impact statements show what you achieved, not just what you did. Learn the formula and examples for resumes, reviews and promotion cases.
tags: impact statements, impact statements examples, resume achievements, performance review achievements, quantify your impact
rel: how-to-write-a-cv-with-no-experience, how-to-ask-for-a-promotion
q: What is an impact statement? | A short statement showing the result of your work, ideally with numbers.
q: What is a formula? | Action verb plus what you did plus the result.
q: What if I do not have numbers? | Use scale, time saved, quality improvements or feedback.
---
Duties describe a job. Impact describes you.

## Direct answer

An impact statement describes the result of your actions, such as "Cut report preparation time by 30 percent by creating a template." Use the formula: action, what you did, result. Quantify where you can.

## Examples

| Duty | Impact |
| :--- | :--- |
| Answered customer emails | Reduced average response time from 24 to 8 hours |
| Managed social media | Grew followers by 40 percent in six months |
| Prepared reports | Built a template that saved the team four hours a week |

## Tips

1. Start with strong verbs.
2. Add numbers or scale.
3. Keep each to one line.

## The bottom line

Show the difference you made.

---

## Direct answer

A brag document is a running list of your achievements, with dates and results. Update it weekly or monthly and use it to prepare for performance reviews, ask for raises and update your resume.

## What to include

- What you delivered and the result.
- Positive feedback.
- Skills you learned.
- Problems you solved.
- Help you gave others.

## Simple format

| Date | What I did | Result |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 12 May | Built onboarding checklist | New hires productive two days faster |

## The bottom line

Capture it now. Use it later.
