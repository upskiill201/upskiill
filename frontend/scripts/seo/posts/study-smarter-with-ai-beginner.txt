"I am a visual learner" is one of the most repeated ideas in education, and one of the least supported.

## Direct answer

Many people prefer to learn in a certain way, but studies have not found good evidence that teaching to a preferred "learning style" improves outcomes. Better results come from methods like retrieval practice, spaced repetition and choosing the format that fits the content: diagrams for maps, practice for skills.

## What does help

1. **Retrieval practice:** testing yourself.
2. **Spaced repetition:** reviewing over time.
3. **Interleaving:** mixing related topics.
4. **Worked examples:** studying solved problems.
5. **Explaining ideas** in your own words.

## Match the format to the content

| Content | Useful format |
| :--- | :--- |
| Geography | maps and diagrams |
| Music | listening and playing |
| Maths | practice problems |
| Language | speaking and listening |

## Using AI

Ask an AI to quiz you, explain a concept three ways or create practice questions, then check with reliable sources.

## The bottom line

Use many methods. Choose by content and evidence, not by label.

=== cornell-notes-method
title: Cornell Notes: How to Use the Cornell Method Step by Step
desc: Learn the Cornell note-taking method with a page layout, how to write cues and summaries and how to turn the cue column into a quiz for revision.
tags: cornell notes, cornell method, cornell note taking, how to take cornell notes, cornell notes template
rel: note-taking-methods
q: What are Cornell notes? | A note-taking system that divides the page into notes, cues and a summary to support review.
q: How do I set up the page? | Draw a narrow left column for cues, a wide right column for notes and a box at the bottom for a summary.
q: How do I review with them? | Cover the notes, read the cues and try to recall the answers.
---
A page layout that builds revision into the notes.

## Direct answer

Cornell notes divide a page into three parts: a main notes area on the right, a narrow cue column on the left and a summary at the bottom. During class you take notes, afterwards you write cues as questions and then a short summary. To revise, cover the notes and answer the cues.

## Set up the page

```text
+-----------+------------------------+
| Cues      | Notes                  |
| (questions|                        |
| keywords) |                        |
+-----------+------------------------+
| Summary                            |
+------------------------------------+
```

## Steps

1. **Take notes** in the right column during the lesson.
2. **Write cues** in the left column after class: questions or keywords.
3. **Write a summary** in two or three sentences.
4. **Review:** cover the notes and answer the cues from memory.
5. **Repeat** at spaced intervals.

## Use AI wisely

Ask an AI to turn your cues into practice questions, then answer them without looking.

## The bottom line

The cue column turns your notes into a self-test.

rel: how-to-study-with-ai, chatgpt-for-studying
q: When should I start revising? | As early as possible, giving yourself time for repeated review rather than cramming.
q: How many hours should I revise a day? | It depends on your situation. Regular, focused sessions with breaks work better than marathon days.
q: How can AI help me revise? | Create quizzes, explain tricky ideas and suggest practice questions, while you do the recall.
---
A plan turns panic into progress.

## Direct answer

For effective revision, list every topic, estimate how strong you are in each, schedule short focused sessions, use active recall and past papers instead of rereading, review old material at spaced intervals and rest properly. AI can help generate practice questions, but you must do the recall.

## A simple weekly plan

1. **List topics** and rate each red, amber or green.
2. **Schedule sessions** of 25 to 50 minutes with breaks.
3. **Start with red topics.**
4. **Use active recall:** close the book and write what you remember.
5. **Do past papers** under time conditions.
6. **Review** earlier topics every few days.
7. **Plan rest,** sleep and exercise.

## Using AI responsibly

- Ask for practice questions on a topic.
- Ask it to explain an answer you got wrong.
- Do not use it to complete assessed work.

## The bottom line

Start early, test yourself and review often.

The line is not "AI or no AI". It is "what does your school allow".

## Direct answer

Using AI is acceptable only within your school or course rules. Use it to understand, practise and get feedback, write your own work, and acknowledge AI help when required. Do not submit AI-generated text as your own where that is not allowed.

## Safer uses

- Explain a concept in simpler words.
- Create practice questions.
- Give feedback on your own draft, if allowed.
- Help plan a study schedule.

## Risky uses

- Writing the assignment.
- Inventing sources.
- Answering exam questions.

## Habits

1. **Read the policy** for each course.
2. **Ask** when it is unclear.
3. **Keep drafts** to show your process.
4. **Cite** AI help as instructed.

## The bottom line

Use AI to learn more, not to avoid learning.

=== summarising-textbooks-with-ai
title: How to Summarise a Textbook Chapter With AI (and Actually Learn It)

tags: summarising textbooks, summarize textbook with ai, ai study summary, ai flashcards, notebooklm style study
rel: how-to-study-with-ai, note-taking-methods
q: Can AI summarise a textbook chapter? | Yes, if you provide the text, but check the summary against the original because it can omit or distort details.
q: Is reading a summary enough to learn? | No. Active recall and practice are what build memory.
q: What should I ask for? | Key ideas, definitions, examples and a set of practice questions.
---
A summary is a map. Learning is walking the route.

## Direct answer

To use AI with a textbook chapter, read the chapter first, then ask the AI for key ideas, definitions and examples based only on the text you provide, and ask for practice questions. Check the summary against the book and test yourself instead of only rereading the summary.

## A workflow

1. **Skim and read** the chapter.
2. **Write your own summary** in five lines.
3. **Ask AI** to summarise using only the text you provide.
4. **Compare** the two and fix gaps.
5. **Ask for ten practice questions.**
6. **Answer from memory,** then check.
7. **Make flashcards** for what you missed.

## Prompt

"Using only this text, list the five key ideas, define each term and give me eight questions that test understanding. Do not answer them yet."

## Cautions

- Check facts and figures.
- Respect copyright and course rules about uploading material.

## The bottom line

Use the summary to organise, and practice to remember.
