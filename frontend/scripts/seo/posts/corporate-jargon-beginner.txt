=== synergy-meaning
title: Synergy Meaning at Work: What It Really Refers To
desc: Synergy means the combined effect is greater than the sum of the parts. Learn the meaning, examples, why it became jargon and plain alternatives.
tags: synergy, synergy meaning, what does synergy mean, synergy examples, corporate jargon
rel: corporate-jargon-meaning-and-examples, business-buzzwords-list
q: What does synergy mean? | When people or parts working together produce a better result than they would separately.
q: Why is synergy seen as jargon? | It is overused, often without specific meaning, so it can sound empty.
q: What can I say instead? | Be specific, such as "working together will save us two weeks".
---
One of the most mocked words in business, with a real idea underneath.

## Direct answer

Synergy means that combined effort produces more than the parts alone, as in "1 + 1 = 3". In business it is used for mergers and teamwork. Because it is often vague, replace it with the specific benefit: what improves, by how much.

## Examples

- "The merger creates synergy" often means savings from shared costs.
- "Our teams have good synergy" means they work well together.

## Plain alternatives

| Instead of | Say |
| :--- | :--- |
| We need more synergy | We should work together on X to save time |
| Synergies from the merger | Savings and shared strengths we expect |

## The bottom line

If you use it, explain what it will do.

## Direct answer

Low-hanging fruit are the easiest tasks or opportunities that give a quick benefit for little effort. They are good for momentum, but do not let them crowd out important longer-term work.

## Examples

- Fixing a confusing form that causes support tickets.
- Cancelling unused software subscriptions.
- Updating an outdated page that gets lots of traffic.

## Plain alternatives

- Quick wins
- Easy improvements
- Simple fixes first

Originally about data speed, now about how much you can take on.

- "Do you have bandwidth to review this?"
- "I do not have the bandwidth until Thursday."

| :--- | :--- |

---
A summary tells you what. A deep dive shows you why.

## Direct answer

A deep dive is a detailed examination of a topic, such as a session dedicated to understanding a problem or analysing data. Ask for one when you need more than a summary. A plain alternative is "a closer look".

1. State the question.

Scalable means something can grow, in users, volume or revenue, without breaking or becoming too expensive. In software it is about handling more load. In business it is about growing faster than costs.

=== action-items-meaning
title: Action Items: What They Are and How to Write Good Ones
desc: Action items are specific tasks assigned after a meeting. Learn what makes a good action item, with examples, an owner and a due date, and a template.
tags: action items, action items meaning, action items examples, meeting action items, how to write action items
rel: ai-meeting-summaries, corporate-jargon-meaning-and-examples
q: What is an action item? | A specific task, with an owner and a deadline, that comes out of a meeting or discussion.
q: What makes a good one? | It starts with a verb, has one owner and a clear due date.
q: Where should I record them? | In meeting notes or a task tool that everyone can see.
---
Meetings without action items are conversations.

## Direct answer

An action item is a specific task assigned during a meeting, with an owner and a deadline. Write them with a verb, a clear outcome and a date, such as "Sam to send the revised budget to finance by Friday."

## Template

**Who:** Sam
**What:** Send revised budget to finance
**By when:** Friday

## Tips

1. One owner per item.
2. Be specific.
3. Share the list right after the meeting.
4. Review at the next meeting.

## The bottom line

Clear owner, clear task, clear date.

=== ballpark-figure-meaning
title: "Ballpark Figure" Meaning: How to Estimate and How to Say It
desc: A ballpark figure is a rough estimate. Learn how to give one safely, what to include and alternatives like rough estimate or approximate range.
tags: ballpark figure, ballpark meaning, ballpark estimate, rough estimate, business idioms
rel: corporate-jargon-meaning-and-examples, business-buzzwords-list
q: What is a ballpark figure? | A rough estimate that is close enough for planning.
q: How do I give one without being held to it? | Give a range and state assumptions.
q: What can I say instead? | "Rough estimate" or "approximate range".
---
Close enough to decide, not exact enough to sign.

## Direct answer

A ballpark figure is a rough estimate. When you give one, say it is approximate, offer a range and state your assumptions so nobody treats it as a firm quote.

## Example

"As a ballpark, the project could cost between 8,000 and 12,000 depending on scope. I will confirm after we define requirements."

1. Use a range.

A good ballpark is honest about its limits.

=== roi-meaning
title: ROI Meaning: Return on Investment Explained With Examples
desc: ROI stands for return on investment. Learn the formula, simple examples, how to use it for decisions and its limits.
tags: roi, roi meaning, return on investment, how to calculate roi, roi formula
rel: corporate-jargon-meaning-and-examples, how-to-price-a-product
q: What does ROI stand for? | Return on Investment.
q: What is the ROI formula? | ROI equals net profit divided by cost, multiplied by 100 to give a percentage.
q: Does a high ROI always mean a good choice? | Not necessarily. Consider time, risk and other benefits.
---
Was it worth it?

## Direct answer

ROI, or return on investment, measures how much you gain compared with what you spent. The formula is ROI = (gain − cost) ÷ cost × 100. A project that costs 1,000 and returns 1,300 has an ROI of 30 percent.

## Example

| Item | Amount |
| :--- | :--- |
| Cost | 1,000 |
| Return | 1,300 |
| Net gain | 300 |
| ROI | 30% |

## Limits

- It ignores time. A 30 percent return in a year differs from one over five years.
- It ignores risk.
- Some benefits are hard to measure.

## The bottom line

A useful quick check, not the whole story.
