## Direct answer

Smishing is a scam sent by text message that tries to trick you into clicking a link, calling a number or sharing personal details. Common messages claim a delivery problem, an account lock, a refund or an unpaid toll. Do not click, check the claim through the official app or website, then report and block the sender.

## Common examples

- "Your parcel could not be delivered. Pay a fee."
- "Your bank account is locked. Verify now."
- "You have a refund waiting."
- A message from a "family member" with a new number asking for money.

## Warning signs

1. Urgency or threats.
2. A link from an unknown number.
3. Spelling or odd phrasing.
4. Requests for passwords, codes or payment.
5. A sender you do not usually hear from.

## What to do

- **Do not click or reply.**
- **Contact the organisation** using a number or app you already trust.
- **Report and block** the number.
- **If you clicked,** change passwords and tell your bank if details were shared.

## The bottom line

Pause, verify independently and never rush because a text says so.

q: How can I spot one? | Be wary of guaranteed high returns, secrecy, pressure to reinvest and trouble withdrawing money.
---
Returns that look too steady and too high are a warning.

## Direct answer

A Ponzi scheme is a fraud that pays existing investors using money from new investors rather than real profit. It collapses when new money dries up. Warning signs include guaranteed high returns, unclear strategies, pressure to reinvest and difficulty cashing out.

## How it works

1. The operator promises high, steady returns.
2. Early investors get paid, which builds trust.
3. They recommend it to others.
4. New money pays old investors.
5. When recruitment slows or many withdraw, it collapses.

## Red flags

- Guaranteed or unusually steady returns.
- A secret or vague strategy.
- Unlicensed sellers.
- Pressure to bring in friends.
- Delays in withdrawing money.

## Protect yourself

- Check the seller and product with your country's financial regulator.
- Ask how returns are generated.
- Never invest money you cannot afford to lose.
- Get independent advice.

## The bottom line

If an investment promises high returns with little risk, assume something is wrong. This is general education, not advice.

---
Both collapse. They just fail in different ways.

## Direct answer

In a pyramid scheme, you pay to join and earn mainly by recruiting others, and money flows up through levels. In a Ponzi scheme, you invest and are paid fake "returns" from newer investors' money. Both fail when new money slows and both are illegal in many countries.

## Key differences

| | Pyramid scheme | Ponzi scheme |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Promise | Earn by recruiting | Earn by investing |
| Money flow | Up the levels | From new to old investors |
| Role of products | Often token | Often vague investments |

## Warning signs

- Pay to join or buy a starter kit.
- Income depends on recruiting.
- Pressure to bring in friends and family.
- Vague answers about real product sales.

## The bottom line

If recruiting matters more than selling, walk away. This is general education, not advice.
