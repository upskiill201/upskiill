=== what-is-a-domain-name
title: What Is a Domain Name? How to Choose and Buy One
desc: A domain name is your website's address. Learn how domains work, how to choose a good one, what the extensions mean and how to register it safely.
tags: domain name, what is a domain name, how to choose a domain name, buy a domain, domain registrar
rel: how-to-make-a-website-for-beginners, how-to-launch-a-website
q: What is a domain name? | The human-readable address of a website, such as example.com, that people type to reach it.
q: How do I choose a good domain? | Keep it short, easy to spell and say aloud, avoid hyphens and numbers and check that the name is not trademarked.
q: Does a domain include hosting? | No. A domain is the address. Hosting is where your website files live.
---
A domain is the address, hosting is the house.

## Direct answer

A domain name is your website's address, like `example.com`. You register it through a domain registrar, usually for a yearly fee, and point it to your hosting. Choose a short, memorable name with a common extension such as `.com`, and avoid hyphens and tricky spellings.

## How to choose

1. **Keep it short** and easy to say.
2. **Avoid hyphens and numbers.**
3. **Check spelling** by saying it to a friend.
4. **Check the name** is not a trademark and the social handles are free.
5. **Choose the right extension.** `.com` is familiar. Country and new extensions can also work.

## How to register

1. Search for the name at a registrar.
2. Check the yearly price and renewal price.
3. Turn on privacy protection if offered.
4. Enable auto-renew so you do not lose it.
5. Point it to your host using DNS records.

## Domain vs hosting

| | Domain | Hosting |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| What it is | the address | where files live |
| Paid | yearly | monthly or yearly, or free tiers |

## The bottom line

Pick something short, register it with a reputable provider and keep it renewed.

=== what-is-dns
title: What Is DNS? How Domain Names Reach Your Website
desc: DNS translates domain names into IP addresses. Learn how it works, the main record types (A, CNAME, MX, TXT) and how to fix common propagation delays.
tags: dns, what is dns, dns records explained, a record cname, dns propagation
rel: custom-domain-setup, what-is-a-domain-name
q: What is DNS? | The Domain Name System, which translates names like example.com into the IP addresses computers use.
q: What are DNS records? | Settings that tell the internet where to send traffic for your domain, such as to your website or email provider.
q: How long does DNS propagation take? | Changes can appear within minutes, but sometimes take up to a day or more depending on caching.
---
DNS is the internet's phone book.

## Direct answer

DNS (Domain Name System) translates domain names into IP addresses. You manage it with DNS records at your domain registrar or DNS provider. The main records are A (to an IP address), CNAME (alias to another name), MX (email) and TXT (verification and email security).

## Common record types

| Record | Purpose | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| A | points a name to an IPv4 address | `example.com → 203.0.113.10` |
| AAAA | points to an IPv6 address | |
| CNAME | alias to another name | `www → example.com` |
| MX | where email goes | |
| TXT | verification, SPF, DKIM | |

## How a visit works

1. You type a domain in your browser.
2. Your device asks a DNS resolver for the address.
3. The resolver finds the records.
4. Your browser connects to that address.

## Propagation

DNS answers are cached. After a change, some people see the old answer until the cache expires, set by the TTL value.

## The bottom line

DNS connects your domain to your host and email. Change one record at a time and check results.

q: Can I get a certificate for free? | Yes. Many hosts and services issue free certificates automatically, for example through Let's Encrypt.
---
The padlock in the address bar is not decoration. It means traffic is encrypted.

## Direct answer

An SSL/TLS certificate enables HTTPS, which encrypts data between your visitors and your site and confirms the site is who it claims to be. Most modern hosts include free certificates that renew automatically. Check that your site loads over `https://` and redirects from `http://`.

## Why you need it

- Protects passwords and form data.
- Browsers mark non-HTTPS sites as not secure.
- Required for many browser features.
- Builds trust and is a small ranking signal.

## How to get one

1. Choose a host that offers automatic HTTPS.
2. Add your domain and verify it.
3. Enable HTTPS and force redirects from HTTP.
4. Check the certificate renews automatically.

## Test it

Visit your site and check the padlock. Fix "mixed content" warnings by making all images and scripts load over HTTPS.

## The bottom line

Turn on HTTPS from day one. It is free and expected.
