Clear and kind, both at once.

## Direct answer

Assertive communication means saying what you think, feel and need directly and respectfully. It sits between passive (not speaking up) and aggressive (steamrolling others). Use "I" statements, be specific, keep a calm tone and listen to the response.

## Three styles

| Style | Sounds like |
| :--- | :--- |
| Passive | "It is fine, whatever you want." |
| Aggressive | "You always get this wrong." |
| Assertive | "I need the report by Friday. Can you do that?" |

## A simple formula

"When [situation], I feel [feeling], because [reason]. I would like [request]."

## Practise

1. Start with small requests.
2. Keep your voice steady.
3. Use short sentences.
4. Hold your position politely.

## The bottom line

You can be respectful and still be clear.

## Direct answer

Vocal variety means changing your pitch, pace, volume and pauses so your message stays interesting. Emphasise key words, slow down for important points, speed up slightly for lighter ones and pause for impact. Record yourself and practise reading stories aloud.

## Four tools

| Tool | Use it to |
| :--- | :--- |
| Pitch | show emotion and questions |
| Pace | signal importance and energy |
| Volume | draw attention |
| Pauses | let key points land |

## Drill

Read this sentence five ways: "I did not say he stole the money." Emphasise a different word each time and notice how the meaning changes.

## The bottom line

Vary your delivery, and people keep listening.

## Direct answer

Common persuasion techniques include reciprocity (give first), social proof (show others do it), commitment (start small), liking (build rapport), authority (show expertise), scarcity (real limits), framing (present options clearly) and stories. Use them honestly and never to deceive.

## Techniques

1. **Reciprocity:** help first.
2. **Social proof:** "Most of the team already uses it."
3. **Commitment:** ask for a small first step.
4. **Liking:** find common ground.
5. **Authority:** share evidence and credentials.
6. **Scarcity:** only when it is true.
7. **Framing:** present benefits that matter to them.
8. **Storytelling:** make it human.

## Ethics check

Would you be comfortable if they knew exactly how you were persuading them?

## The bottom line

Persuade by being useful and honest.

## Scripts

| Situation | Say |
| :--- | :--- |

After the meeting, send a short note: "Following our discussion, my proposal is..."

## The bottom line

A few ready phrases lower the barrier to speaking.

q: How much eye contact is normal? | Often about half to two-thirds of the time while talking, but it varies by culture and person.
q: What if eye contact makes me uncomfortable? | Look at the bridge of the nose or the forehead, or glance away briefly to think.
q: How do I do eye contact on video calls? | Look at the camera while you speak and at the screen while you listen.
---
Look at people, not through them.

## Direct answer

Natural eye contact means looking at the person for a few seconds at a time, then glancing away, roughly half to two-thirds of the time while listening and a little less while speaking. Norms vary across cultures. On video, look at the camera when you speak.

## Tips

1. **Hold for three to five seconds,** then look away naturally.
2. **In groups,** share eye contact around the room.
3. **On video,** look at the lens when making key points.
4. **If it is hard,** look at the forehead or nose bridge.
5. **Respect cultural norms.**

## The bottom line

Warm, steady, not staring.

q: How do I stop interrupting people? | Pause, jot down your thought and wait until the speaker finishes before replying.
q: What do I say when I am interrupted? | "I would like to finish my point" delivered calmly.
q: Is interrupting always rude? | Not always, such as to clarify or in lively discussions, but pay attention to cultural and individual norms.
---
Both sides of the habit matter.

## Direct answer

If you tend to interrupt, count a beat after the speaker finishes and note your thought instead of speaking. If you are interrupted, calmly say, "Let me finish this point," and continue. Notice patterns and speak to the person privately if it repeats.

## If you interrupt

1. Write your thought down.
2. Wait a full second.
3. Ask a question before offering your view.

## If you are interrupted

- "Hold on, I would like to finish."
- Raise a hand and keep going.
- Follow up after the meeting if it keeps happening.

## The bottom line

Listening first makes you easier to listen to.

=== mirroring-in-conversation
title: Mirroring in Conversation: Build Rapport Without Being Obvious
desc: Mirroring means subtly matching another person's pace, tone or posture to build rapport. Learn how it works, how to do it naturally and when to avoid it.
tags: mirroring, mirroring body language, mirroring in conversation, build rapport, active listening techniques
rel: rapport-building, active-listening-skills
q: What is mirroring? | Subtly matching aspects of someone's communication, such as pace, tone or posture, to create comfort.
q: Is mirroring manipulative? | Not when done naturally and with real interest in the other person.
q: How do I avoid being obvious? | Match the general energy and pace, not exact gestures, and focus on listening.
---
People like people who feel like them.

## Direct answer

Mirroring is matching the other person's pace, tone and general posture to build rapport. Do it lightly and naturally, focus on genuine listening and do not copy gestures exactly. Pay attention to their energy and adjust.

## How to do it

1. **Notice their pace** and match it.
2. **Use similar language** and key words.
3. **Match energy levels:** calm with calm, upbeat with upbeat.
4. **Lean slightly** if they lean.
5. **Stay genuine.**

## Caution

If it feels fake, stop. Interest in the person is more important than technique.

## The bottom line

Be responsive, not robotic.
