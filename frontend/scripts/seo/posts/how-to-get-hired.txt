=== job-search-strategy
title: Job Search Strategy: A Weekly Plan That Gets Interviews
desc: A practical job search strategy: define your target, tailor applications, network, track progress and set weekly goals to keep momentum.
tags: job search strategy, how to search for a job, job search plan, job hunting tips, weekly job search routine
rel: how-to-find-a-job, how-to-get-hired-step-by-step
q: How do I start a job search? | Define the roles you want, update your resume and profiles, and build a list of target employers.
q: How many applications should I send? | Quality beats quantity. Tailored applications to well-matched roles perform better than mass applying.
q: How long does a job search take? | It varies widely. Keep a steady weekly routine.
---
A search without a plan becomes a mood.

## Direct answer

A good job search strategy defines target roles and employers, prepares a strong resume and profile, applies selectively with tailored materials, uses networking and tracks progress weekly. Set small targets and review what is working.

## A weekly plan

| Activity | Target |
| :--- | :--- |
| Tailored applications | 5 |
| Networking messages | 5 |
| Follow-ups | 3 |
| Skill practice | 2 sessions |
| Review and adjust | 30 minutes |

## Steps

1. **Define** target roles and skills.
2. **Update** your resume and profile.
3. **List employers** and watch their openings.
4. **Tailor each application.**
5. **Network** for referrals.
6. **Track** in a spreadsheet.

## The bottom line

Consistency and targeting beat volume.

=== informational-interview
title: Informational Interviews: How to Ask for One and What to Say
desc: An informational interview is a conversation to learn about a role or industry. Learn how to ask, what to ask and how to follow up.
tags: informational interview, informational interview questions, how to ask for an informational interview, career conversation, networking message
rel: how-to-network-for-a-job, how-to-get-noticed-by-employers
q: What is an informational interview? | A short conversation with someone in a role or field you are interested in, to learn and build a connection.
q: How do I ask for one? | Send a brief, specific message with a clear small request, such as 15 minutes.
q: Is it okay to ask for a job? | No. Ask for advice and insight. Opportunities may follow.
---
Ask for advice, not a job.

## Direct answer

An informational interview is a short chat to learn about someone's work. Message them briefly, ask for 15 to 20 minutes, prepare questions, listen and say thank you. Follow up with what you did with their advice.

## Message template

"Hi [name], I admire your work in [field]. I am exploring a move into [role]. Could I ask you three questions over a 15-minute call?"

## Questions

- How did you get into this?
- What does a typical week look like?
- What skills matter most?
- What do you wish you had known?
- Who else should I talk to?

## The bottom line

Be curious, concise and grateful.

=== job-references-guide
title: Job References: Who to Ask, How to Ask and What to Share
desc: Choose good references, ask politely, brief them on the role and say thanks. Includes a request template and what to do with no work references.
tags: job references, how to ask for a reference, who to use as a reference, reference letter, professional references
rel: how-to-get-hired-step-by-step, how-to-write-a-cv-with-no-experience
q: Who should I ask to be a reference? | Managers, colleagues, clients or teachers who know your work well and will speak positively.
q: Should I ask permission? | Always.
q: What if I have no work references? | Use teachers, volunteer leaders or people who supervised projects.
---
A good reference can tip the decision.

## Direct answer

Choose references who know your work, ask permission, brief them about the role and share your resume. Thank them and update them on the outcome. If you have little work history, ask teachers, mentors or volunteer leaders.

## Request template

"Hi [name], I am applying for [role] and would be grateful if you would act as a reference. I have attached my resume and the job description. Would you be comfortable speaking about [skills]?"

## Tips

1. Ask early.
2. Give them context.
3. Thank them.
4. Tell them the result.

## The bottom line

Prepare your references like you prepare your interview.

An offer is the start of a conversation.

## Direct answer

Review the whole offer: pay, benefits, flexibility, role and growth. Thank them, ask for time, research the market and negotiate politely, with reasons and options. Get the final terms in writing before accepting.

## Checklist

- [ ] Salary and bonus
- [ ] Leave and benefits
- [ ] Hours and flexibility
- [ ] Role and reporting line
- [ ] Training and growth
- [ ] Start date
- [ ] Probation terms

## The bottom line

Be grateful, prepared and clear.

=== hidden-job-market
title: The Hidden Job Market: How to Find Jobs That Are Not Advertised

tags: hidden job market, unadvertised jobs, how to find hidden jobs, referral hiring, cold outreach for jobs
rel: how-to-network-for-a-job, informational-interview
q: What is the hidden job market? | Jobs that are filled through referrals, networks or direct contact without public advertising.
q: How do I access it? | Build relationships, tell people what you are seeking and contact companies directly.
q: Is it a myth? | It is partly real, since referrals are a major hiring route, though many jobs are advertised.
---
Not every job opening begins with an advert.

## Direct answer

To tap the hidden job market, tell your network what you are looking for, ask for introductions, follow companies you like, contact hiring managers with a short, specific message and attend events. Combine this with applying to advertised roles.

## Steps

1. List 20 target companies.
2. Follow them and their teams.
3. Reach out to people with a specific, short ask.
4. Share what you can offer.
5. Follow up politely.

## Message example

"Hi [name], I admire your team's work on X. I am a [role] with experience in Y. Are you hiring or could you suggest who I should speak to?"

## The bottom line

People hire people they know. Get known.

=== job-application-tracker

q: What should I include? | Company, role, date, link, status, contact and next step.

---
Organisation turns effort into results.

## Direct answer

Keep a spreadsheet with a row per application: company, role, link, date applied, status, contact, follow-up date and notes. Review it weekly to follow up and adjust your approach.

## Suggested columns

| Column | Purpose |
| :--- | :--- |
| Company | who |
| Role | what |
| Link | find it again |
| Date applied | timeline |
| Status | applied, interviewing, offer |
| Contact | person |
| Follow-up date | reminder |
| Notes | learning |

## Habits

1. Add each application immediately.
2. Set follow-up reminders.
3. Note what version of your resume you used.
4. Review results monthly.

## The bottom line

A tracker keeps your search calm and organised.
