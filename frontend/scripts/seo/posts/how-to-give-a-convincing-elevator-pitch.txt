=== value-proposition-explained
title: Value Proposition Explained: How to Write One That Works
desc: A value proposition says why customers should choose you. Learn the formula, examples, how to test it and common mistakes.
tags: value proposition, what is a value proposition, value proposition examples, how to write a value proposition, unique value
rel: how-to-write-an-elevator-pitch
q: What is a value proposition? | A clear statement of the benefit you provide, for whom and why it is better than alternatives.
q: What is a simple formula? | For [audience] who [need], we provide [benefit], unlike [alternative], because [reason].
q: How do I test it? | Show it to potential customers and ask if they understand and care.
---
If customers cannot say why you matter, you do not have a value proposition yet.

## Direct answer

A value proposition is a short statement of who you help, what problem you solve, the benefit and why you are different. A simple formula is: "For [audience] who [need], we [do X] so that [benefit], unlike [alternative]." Test it with real customers.

## Examples

- "For busy parents who struggle to plan meals, our app creates a weekly plan in two minutes, so dinner is never a scramble."
- "For freelancers who dislike chasing payments, we send invoices and reminders automatically."

## How to write one

1. Describe the customer and their problem.
2. State the main benefit.
3. Say what makes you different.
4. Use plain words.
5. Test and refine.

## Mistakes

- Listing features instead of benefits.
- Vague claims such as "best quality".
- Too many ideas at once.

## The bottom line

One clear promise to one clear audience.

q: What should I include? | Who you are, who you help, the problem, your solution or strength and a call to action.
q: Should I memorise it? | Know the points well, but keep it conversational.
---
Half a minute is enough to be remembered.

## Direct answer

A 30-second pitch tells people who you are, what problem you solve, how you solve it and what you want next, in about 75 to 90 words. Practise until it sounds natural and adapt it to the listener.

## Template

1. **Hook:** a question or striking fact.
2. **Who you help.**
3. **The problem.**
4. **What you do** and the result.
5. **The ask.**

## Example for a job seeker

"I help customer teams reduce wait times. At my last job I built a simple triage process that cut average replies from two days to four hours. I'm looking for a support operations role where I can do that at scale."

## The bottom line

Short, specific, human.

=== call-to-action-writing
title: Call to Action: How to Close a Pitch or Message With a Clear Ask
desc: A call to action tells people what to do next. Learn how to write clear asks for pitches, emails and networking, with examples and mistakes to avoid.
tags: call to action, call to action examples, how to write a call to action, clear ask, pitch closing
rel: thirty-second-pitch
q: What is a call to action? | A clear instruction or request that tells someone what to do next.
q: How specific should it be? | Specific enough that the person knows exactly what to do and by when.
q: Where do I put it? | At the end of your message, and sometimes at the start for busy readers.
---
People rarely act on vague hints.

## Direct answer

A call to action is a clear, specific request that follows your pitch or message, such as "Could we book 20 minutes on Thursday?". Make it easy, specific and time-bound.

## Examples

| Weak | Strong |
| :--- | :--- |
| Let me know. | Could you reply by Friday with your preferred date? |
| Maybe we can talk. | Could we have a 15-minute call on Tuesday at 10? |
| Check it out. | Try the demo at the link and tell me what you think. |

## Tips

1. One ask per message.
2. Make it easy: link, time, one click.
3. Say why it benefits them.

## The bottom line

Always finish with a clear next step.

=== problem-solution-fit
title: Problem-Solution Fit: How to Know Your Idea Solves a Real Problem
desc: Problem-solution fit means your solution actually solves a problem people have. Learn how to test it with interviews and small experiments before building.
tags: problem solution fit, problem solution fit meaning, validate startup idea, customer interviews, product market fit
rel: how-to-validate-a-business-idea, pain-point-in-pitch
q: What is problem-solution fit? | Evidence that a real problem exists and your solution addresses it for a specific group.
q: How is it different from product-market fit? | Problem-solution fit comes first. Product-market fit means enough customers want the product at scale.
q: How do I test it? | Interview target customers and try a simple prototype or landing page.
---
Before you build, check the problem is real.

## Direct answer

Problem-solution fit means you have evidence that a specific group has a real problem and that your solution helps. Test it with customer interviews, a simple prototype or a landing page test, and look for clear signs: people describe the problem unprompted and ask for the solution.

## Steps

1. **Define the customer and the problem.**
2. **Interview** at least ten people.
3. **Listen** for strong, specific stories.
4. **Test a solution** with a prototype.
5. **Look for commitment:** time, data or money.

## Warning signs

- People are polite but not interested.
- The problem is rare or mild.
- They already have an easy fix.

## The bottom line

Fall in love with the problem first.

q: How many times should I rehearse? | Until you are comfortable with the flow, not word for word.

---
Fluent pitches are practised pitches.

## Direct answer

Practise your pitch aloud, record it, time it and rehearse in front of friends. Focus on the structure and key lines rather than memorising every word. Prepare for questions and practise in the real setting if you can.

## 8 drills

1. Say it to a mirror.
2. Record audio.
3. Time it.
4. Pitch to a friend.
5. Shorten it by a third.
6. Change the order of points.
7. Practise questions.
8. Run it standing as you will on the day.

## The bottom line

Practice makes it sound like you.
