=== linkedin-headline-examples
title: LinkedIn Headline Examples: 25 Ideas and a Formula That Works
desc: Write a LinkedIn headline that attracts recruiters: the formula, 25 examples for different roles and mistakes to avoid.
tags: linkedin headline, linkedin headline examples, best linkedin headlines, how to write a linkedin headline, linkedin profile tips
rel: linkedin-profile-tips-for-job-seekers, personal-brand-statement
q: What makes a good LinkedIn headline? | It states your role or goal, key skills and value in a few words, with keywords recruiters search for.
q: How long can it be? | There is a character limit, so keep the most important words first.
q: Should I write "open to work"? | You can use the platform's feature to signal availability, and make your headline clear about what you do.
---
Your headline is the first line recruiters read.

## Direct answer

A strong LinkedIn headline combines your role, key skills and the value you offer, using words recruiters search for. Formula: "[Role] | [Key skills] | [Value or result]".

## Examples

- Data Analyst | SQL, Python, Tableau | Turning data into decisions
- Customer Support Specialist | Empathy, Zendesk | Raising satisfaction scores
- Junior Web Developer | HTML, CSS, JavaScript | Building fast, accessible sites
- Marketing Graduate | Content, SEO | Looking for a growth role

## Mistakes

- Only your job title.
- Buzzwords with no proof.
- Too long to read.

## The bottom line

Be specific and keyword-rich.

q: How do I show projects without clients? | Use personal projects, volunteer work or case studies you create.
---
Show, do not just tell.

## Direct answer

A portfolio website should have a clear intro, three to five of your best projects with the problem, your role, the process and the result, and an easy way to contact you. Keep design simple, fast and mobile friendly.

## Structure

1. **Home:** who you are and what you do.
2. **Projects:** each with a short case study.
3. **About:** your story and skills.
4. **Contact.**

- What you did.
- The result.
- What you learned.

## Tips

- Quality over quantity.
- Link to code or live demos.
- Check it on a phone.

## The bottom line

A small, well-presented portfolio beats a large, messy one.

To get a referral, find people at the company, build a genuine connection, share the role and your resume, explain why you fit and make it easy for them to say yes. Always be polite and follow up.

## Message template

tags: resume summary, resume summary examples, resume profile, how to write a resume summary, cv personal statement
rel: how-to-write-a-cv-with-no-experience, impact-statements
q: What is a resume summary? | A short statement at the top of your resume that highlights your skills, experience and goals.
q: How long should it be? | Two to four lines.
q: Do I need one? | It is optional but helpful for career changers and to focus attention.
---
The first lines decide whether they keep reading.

## Direct answer

A resume summary is two to four lines at the top of your resume that state who you are, your top skills and what you offer. Use keywords from the job description and include a result if you can.

## Examples

**Entry level:** "Recent business graduate with internship experience in customer research and strong Excel skills, seeking an analyst role."

**Career change:** "Teacher turned junior web developer with HTML, CSS and JavaScript projects and strong communication skills."

**Experienced:** "Operations manager with eight years' experience cutting costs and improving delivery times for retail teams."

## Tips

1. Tailor it to the role.
2. Avoid clichés.
3. Show proof.

## The bottom line

Short, targeted and specific.
