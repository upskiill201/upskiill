## The storyline: simple, one person, one goal

Founder brief: no frame device (no radio show, no anthology). The story should be easy to follow.

**One person. One goal. Five weeks.**

**Ada** (24, Lagos, junior product designer at a fintech startup) built a small app on the side to run her office **ajo** (rotating savings group). Without telling anyone, her colleague entered it into **"60 Seconds"**, a pitch night for young builders at the Lagos Tech Fest. It got shortlisted. In five weeks, Ada gets **one minute on stage** in front of three judges and 300 people. The winner gets ₦2 million and a mentor.

On day one she gets stuck in a lift with the head judge, **Mrs Funke Adeyemi**. Mrs Adeyemi says, "So, what's your app?" Ada talks about her database for 30 floors. The doors open. Mrs Adeyemi says, "Hmm," and walks out.

That's the whole hook. The course follows Ada week by week until pitch night, and ends back in that same lift.

**What keeps it easy to follow:**
- **The map counts down.** Chapter banners read "5 weeks to go", "4 weeks to go"… "Pitch night".
- **Ada's pitch gets a new draft every chapter.** Pitch v1 is the lift disaster. Pitch v5 is pitch night. Lesson 9 of each chapter ("Pitch check") shows the current draft and lets you tap every line to see what changed and why. Learners watch one pitch get better.
- **A stopwatch** appears whenever Ada pitches, so timing is always visible.
- **Two supporting people, both familiar.** **Arjun**, her mentor from Python for Beginners, coaches her on video calls and is brutally honest. **Tey** teaches. One extra, Mrs Adeyemi (the judge), appears at the start and at the end.
- **The learner's job is simple:** spot what's wrong with Ada's pitch, fix it, and then write your own.

**Why Ada:** she's the cast's builder, the cast sheet says she "says yes to everything and then panics a little", and the ajo app links to Python for Beginners without needing that course. The skills aren't only for founders. Chapter 2 is entirely about pitching *yourself* (networking, recruiters, "so what do you do?"), and every lesson's Reflect asks learners to write their own pitch, whether that's for a job, an idea, a business or a scholarship.

**Cast note (lives move forward):** this happens after Python for Beginners. The ajo tracker exists, and Ada has turned it into a small app. Arjun and Ada's mentor relationship is already fixed in CAST.md.

## What the research says (the course's backbone)

| Finding | Source | Used in |
|---|---|---|
| **Being prepared beat looking passionate.** When investors rated business-plan pitches, how prepared the founder seemed predicted funding; displayed enthusiasm didn't. | Chen, Yao & Kotha (2009), *Academy of Management Journal* 52(1) | Ch4 L2 |
| **Delivery mattered more than investors admitted.** Business angels' interest tracked presentation quality, while their stated reasons were all about content. | Clark (2008), *Venture Capital* 10(3) (small sample: 24 angels) | Ch4 L2 |
| **"I'm excited" beat "I'm calm."** Speakers who reframed nerves as excitement were rated more persuasive and confident. | Brooks (2014), *Journal of Experimental Psychology: General* (hedged: replication evidence is still limited) | Ch4 L4 |
| **Six alternatives to the classic pitch:** one-word, question, rhyming, subject-line, Twitter and Pixar pitches. | Daniel Pink, *To Sell Is Human* (2012) | Ch3 L2–L4 |
| **Rhymes feel truer.** People rated rhyming sayings as more accurate than identical non-rhyming ones. | McGlone & Tofighbakhsh (2000), *Psychological Science* 11(5) | Ch3 L4 |
| **Curiosity gaps hook attention.** A small gap in what someone knows makes them want to close it. | Loewenstein (1994), *Psychological Bulletin*, information-gap theory | Ch3 L6 |
| **People decide fast.** Strangers form lasting impressions from very thin slices of behaviour. | Ambady & Rosenthal (1993), *JPSP*; Willis & Todorov (2006) | Ch1 L8 |
| **People enjoy talking about themselves**, and follow-up questions increase liking. A personal pitch should end with a question back. | Tamir & Mitchell (2012), *PNAS*; Huang et al. (2017), *JPSP* | Ch2 L2, L7 |
| **Stories and concrete details stick; abstractions slip.** | Heath & Heath, *Made to Stick* (2007); concreteness effect (Paivio) | Ch3 L7–L8 |
| **The name has no single origin.** Credited variously to 1990s magazine editors, Hollywood folklore and Philip Crosby's "elevator speech". | Mental Floss; Wikipedia, "Elevator pitch" | Ch1 L2 |
| **Speaking pace:** about 130–150 words a minute, so a 60-second pitch is roughly 140 words, and a 30-second one about 70. | Standard speech-rate guidance | Ch1 L7 |

## Chapter plan

### Chapter 1 · 5 weeks to go · "30 floors, zero words"
*Ada blows her lift moment with the head judge. Tey and Arjun start from zero: what a pitch is, the simple formula, and the one sentence that does most of the work.* **Challenge:** Ada gets a second lift ride and 30 seconds.

1. The lift (Story) · 2. What a pitch is, and isn't (Myth or Fact) · 3. The five-part pitch: who, problem, what you do, why you, the ask (Step-by-step) · 4. Your one sentence: "We help ___ do ___ so ___" (Chat with Tey) · **5 Checkpoint** · 6. Features vs benefits (Before/After) · 7. 60 seconds is about 140 words (Card deck) · 8. The first ten seconds decide (Reading) · 9. Pitch check: v1, the lift disaster (Tap to explore) · **10 Challenge: the second lift ride**

### Chapter 2 · 4 weeks to go · "So, what do you do?"
*At a Tech Fest networking mixer, three people ask Ada what she does. She says "I'm just a junior designer" three times. This chapter is the personal pitch: introducing yourself so people remember you.* **Challenge:** a recruiter and a stranger at the mixer.

1. "I'm just a junior designer" (Story) · 2. Drop the "just" (Myth or Fact) · 3. Present, past, future: a 3-line intro (Step-by-step) · 4. Job title vs the problem you solve (Before/After) · **5 Checkpoint** · 6. Tailor it to who's listening (Chat with Tey) · 7. Turn your pitch into a conversation (Card deck) · 8. Your pitch online: bio, headline, first message (Reading) · 9. Pitch check: Ada's LinkedIn "About" (Tap to explore) · **10 Challenge: the mixer**

### Chapter 3 · 3 weeks to go · "Make it stick"
*Ada's pitch is correct now, but on a video call Arjun admits he zoned out. Tey shows her how to make a pitch memorable: stories, hooks, specifics, and Daniel Pink's six kinds of pitch.* **Challenge:** the 60-second video the organisers ask every finalist to send.

1. Arjun zones out (Story) · 2. Six ways to pitch (Reading) · 3. The Pixar pitch: "Once upon a time…" (Chat with Tey) · 4. Questions and rhymes (Myth or Fact) · **5 Checkpoint** · 6. A hook that makes them curious (Step-by-step) · 7. Vague vs vivid (Before/After) · 8. Numbers that prove it (Card deck) · 9. Pitch check: v3 (Tap to explore) · **10 Challenge: the video submission**

### Chapter 4 · 2 weeks to go · "Say it like you mean it"
*The words are great. Then Ada rehearses for her colleagues: she reads from her phone, speaks to the floor, and races to finish in 41 seconds. Now it's about delivery.* **Challenge:** the dress rehearsal in front of the whole office.

1. The first rehearsal (Story) · 2. Prepared beats passionate (Reading) · 3. How to rehearse properly (Step-by-step) · 4. "Calm down" vs "I'm excited" (Myth or Fact) · **5 Checkpoint** · 6. Memorised script vs knowing your beats (Before/After) · 7. Voice, pace, eyes and hands (Card deck) · 8. When your mind goes blank (Chat with Tey) · 9. Pitch check: Ada's notes card (Tap to explore) · **10 Challenge: the dress rehearsal**

### Chapter 5 · Pitch night · "The big minute"
*Pitch night. Ada is speaker seven of ten. The last pieces: a clear ask, handling the judges' questions, adjusting on the spot, and the follow-up.* **Final Challenge:** Ada's minute on stage, the judges' questions, and one more lift ride with Mrs Adeyemi.

1. Backstage (Story) · 2. End with a clear ask (Step-by-step) · 3. "I don't know" is allowed (Myth or Fact) · 4. Tough questions (Chat with Tey) · **5 Checkpoint** · 6. Fading out vs a strong close (Before/After) · 7. 10 seconds, 30 seconds, 2 minutes: one pitch, three lengths (Reading) · 8. The follow-up message (Card deck) · 9. Pitch check: v5, the final script (Tap to explore) · **10 Final Challenge: the big minute**

Every chapter uses all 8 Learn formats, and no two lessons in a row share a main format. The story stays light and funny: Ada's panic, Arjun's honesty, and the stopwatch. But each lesson teaches one clear tool, and the Reflect step always asks learners to build their *own* pitch.
