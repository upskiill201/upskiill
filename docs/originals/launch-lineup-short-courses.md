## 1. Acing Job Interviews: *Remi's Interview Season* (Career)

Remi (21, Nairobi, final-year computer science, calm, too relaxed about deadlines, brilliant at understanding things and poor at explaining them) has three internship interviews in five weeks:
- a phone screen with Savanna Bank (fictional), where he rambles for four minutes and gets turned down;
- a technical interview at Mtaa Labs (a fictional startup), where he comes second;
- the Synergix Strategy & Operations final round: a group exercise with Zanele, then a panel with Brian Otieno.

Ada coaches him through WhatsApp voice notes. Lesson 9 is his **interview card**, v1 to v5. The course ends with the Synergix offer, which leads into *Corporate Jargon*.

| Ch | Title | Covers |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "So, tell me about yourself" | how interviews work, what interviewers look for, the 60–90 second answer, structured interviews |
| 2 | Do your homework | researching in an hour, decoding a job ad, "why us?", video set-up, questions to ask |
| 3 | Your story bank | STAR, six stories for most questions, numbers, honesty, "I" vs "we" |
| 4 | The hard questions | thinking out loud, weakness and failure, salary, inappropriate questions, tricky-question sheet |
| 5 | The final round | nerves, group exercises, "any questions?", follow-up, rejection and feedback; finale: the offer |

Research:
- Structured interviews: Schmidt & Hunter (1998); Sackett, Zhang, Berry & Lievens (2022), where structured interviews top the list at .42.
- Behaviour-description interviews: Janz (1982).
- Interview anxiety: McCarthy & Goffin (2004).
- Reframing anxiety: Brooks (2014).
- Power posing: Carney et al. (2010), Ranehill et al. (2015) and Carney's 2016 statement.
- The "7% words" myth: Mehrabian.

Practice: AI as a mock interviewer is mentioned only as something learners can do with their own tools. There is no in-app AI feature (Phase One guard).

## 2. Clear Writing at Work: *Zanele Writes It Plain* (Communication)

Zanele (Synergix junior analyst; English is her third language) solved the late-deliveries puzzle in *Data Science for Beginners*. Now the Saturday dispatch pilot is running, and she has to write about it:
- weekly updates nobody reads;
- a 12-page report;
- an email asking Emeka for data;
- a bad-news message;
- finally, a one-page memo asking Mr Kamau to roll the pilot out.

Mrs Achieng: "He reads two lines." Brian speaks jargon. Lesson 9 is Zanele's **plain checklist**, v1 to v5. Pilot (fictional): late deliveries fell from about 1 in 3 to 21%. The what-if analysis had predicted 19%.

| Ch | Title | Covers |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Nobody reads it | busy readers, less is more, one message one purpose, why we over-write |
| 2 | Plain words | short common words, active voice, sentence length, jargon swaps, writing in a second language |
| 3 | Point first | BLUF, subject lines, one clear ask, make responding easy |
| 4 | Docs people skim | how people read on screens, headings that say something, enough formatting, one-pagers |
| 5 | Hard messages | bad news, saying no in writing, specific feedback, editing with AI, final edit; finale: the memo |

Research:
- Rogers & Lasky-Fink, *Writing for Busy Readers* (2023), six principles.
- Oppenheimer (2006): needless complexity lowers how intelligent readers judge the writer.
- US Plain Writing Act (2010); Plain English Campaign.
- Nielsen (2006): F-shaped reading.
- Noy & Zhang (2023), *Science*: ChatGPT and writing tasks.
- Kruger & Epley (2005): tone in email.

## 3. Speak Like a CEO: *Lucía Takes the Room* (Communication)

After her first 90 days leading the team at Nopal (*Leadership for Beginners*), Lucía has two things ahead:
- a weekly two-minute update to Carmen and the leadership team;
- in five weeks, presenting the summer launch results at Nopal's all-hands, the Encuentro, to 300 people.

Lucía is clear in writing and rambles out loud. Carmen wants "the headline first". Diego is now an ally who gives blunt notes. Lesson 9 is her **talk card**, v1 to v5. This course deliberately avoids the content of *How to Make People Listen to You* (holding the floor) and *Elevator Pitch* (the one-minute pitch). It is about everyday speaking clarity, meetings with senior people, longer talks with slides, voice and presence, and Q&A.

| Ch | Title | Covers |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Headline first | the point in 30 seconds, rule of three, filler words, speaking in answers |
| 2 | In the room with leaders | updates to executives, what leaders listen for, recommending not reporting, disagreeing upward |
| 3 | Build the talk | one message, structure, story and data, slides that help (Mayer), signposts |
| 4 | Voice and presence | pace and pauses, eye contact, nerves, presence myths, recording yourself and self-checking |
| 5 | Questions and the big day | handling Q&A, "I don't know", hostile questions, rehearsal plan; finale: the Encuentro |

Research:
- Mayer's multimedia principles (coherence, signalling, redundancy).
- Fillers: Clark & Fox Tree (2002).
- The Mehrabian myth.
- Brooks (2014).
- Power-posing replication: Ranehill et al. (2015).
- Rehearsal and the "spotlight effect": Gilovich, Medvec & Savitsky (2000).
- The "illusion of transparency" about nerves: Savitsky & Gilovich (2003).

Speaking practice follows the playbook: technique, plus recording yourself and checking against a checklist. No AI feedback.
