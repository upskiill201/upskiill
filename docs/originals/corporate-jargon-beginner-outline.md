## The story

**Remi** (21, Nairobi, from the shared cast) has just finished his Computer Science degree and starts a 12-week internship in the Strategy & Operations team at **Synergix**, a large company whose head office is in Nairobi, with offices in Lagos, Johannesburg and London. Nobody, including most of its staff, can explain in one sentence what Synergix actually does. (Running gag; the answer arrives in the final chapter.)

Remi is brilliant at understanding systems. Corporate English is the first system that beats him. In his very first meeting his new manager says:

> "Let's circle back on the Q3 deck, take the vendor thing offline, grab the low-hanging fruit, and deep-dive the numbers EOD."

Remi writes every word down and understands none of it. The course is his 12 weeks, from that meeting to the day he's offered a job.

The countdown on the map is the internship itself: **Day 1 → Week 12 → Offer day.**

The story has no villain (playbook §8a). The tension comes from confusion, embarrassment, deadlines and Remi's habits. Nobody who uses jargon is mocked as stupid: Tey teases the situation, never the person (CAST.md rule 3).

### What keeps learners coming back

1. **"Translate Brian."** A recurring segment in every chapter: Brian sends a message or voice note, and the learner turns it into plain English before Remi replies.
2. **Literal Remi.** Many lessons open on what Remi *pictures* when he hears a phrase (taking a meeting "offline" by unplugging the router). It's a hook, and it makes the real meaning stick.
3. **Remi's Jargon Notebook.** Every lesson adds phrases to Remi's notebook; the chapter's Deepen offers it as a printable cheat sheet. About 250 phrases by the end of Level 1.
4. **Real or made up?** Quick-fire rounds where some phrases are real office jargon and some are invented ("let's mango the pipeline"). Hard to stop playing.
5. **Checkpoints are office moments:** Buzzword Bingo at the Monday stand-up, Brian's 7 a.m. voice note, the fish in the microwave.
6. **A cliffhanger after every Unit Challenge** that opens the next chapter.

## Facts to handle carefully (playbook §9)

- **Phrase origins:** many jargon "origin stories" online are disputed or invented. A lesson only states an origin when a reliable dictionary or etymology source supports it, with the source in the author notes. Otherwise Tey says what the phrase means today, not where it came from.
- **Acronym meanings vary by company and industry** (PM, PR, CS). Lessons say "usually" and teach how to check.
- **World Englishes are valid English.** "Kindly revert" and "do the needful" are standard in some countries' business English. Lessons teach *know your audience*, never "this is wrong English".
- **Office politics:** honest strategies only. Nothing on manipulating, flattering or undermining people.
- **Drinking:** after-work events never assume alcohol; not drinking is always presented as normal.
- **Offer letters and pay:** general meanings only, with a note that employment law and norms differ by country. No legal or financial advice.
- **Workplace norms differ** (punctuality, titles, dress, cameras). Lessons say where norms vary instead of declaring one right way.
