# How to Set Clear Expectations — Outline

> Leadership & Management track, course 11 in the plan (built third, 2026-10-16). Short Original: 5 chapters × 10 lessons.
> **Storyline: "Arjun's 30-Day Build".** Lead: Arjun. Countdown: Day 1 → Day 30, the Diwali sale.

## The story

Arjun (freelance developer, Bengaluru) wins his biggest job yet: Mr Rao's online shop in Pune, live before the Diwali sale, in 30 days. Too big for one person, so he takes on his first two subcontractors:

- **Kavya** (Pune, 23): fast, ambitious, funny; says "done" when things are 80% done.
- **Joel** (Kochi, 26): careful and thorough; waits to be told everything and goes quiet when unsure.
- **Mr Rao** (narrated): the client who reads only the first line of every email.

Arjun is brilliant and precise, and assumes everyone can see what's in his head. His arc: from "it's obvious" to expectations that are clear, written, checked and kept.

## Chapters

**Ch1 · Day 1 · "I thought it was obvious"**: the curse of knowledge, the Gallup item, the illusion of transparency, what "done" means, the first brief. Challenge: Day 3 and both juniors have built the wrong thing.

**Ch2 · Day 6 · "Who does what?"**: role clarity, RACI, commander's intent, decision rights, working agreements. Challenge: Kavya and Joel both rebuilt the checkout.

**Ch3 · Day 12 · "What good looks like"**: examples and quality bars, the early draft, priorities and trade-offs, teach-back, a review checklist. Challenge: Kavya's "done" page.

**Ch4 · Day 20 · "The client changes everything"**: expectations upwards, scope creep, unwritten expectations, "yes, and that means...", resetting the plan with the team. Challenge: Mr Rao wants a loyalty programme by Diwali.

**Ch5 · Day 30 · "Launch, and what we learned"**: missed expectations (can't vs won't), the expectations conversation, recognising expectations met, the retrospective, the next agreement. Final challenge: launch night.
