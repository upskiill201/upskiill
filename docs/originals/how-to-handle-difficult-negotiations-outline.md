## The storyline: the biggest client he's ever had

Arjun (Bengaluru, freelance developer) and his two subcontractors, Kavya and Joel, are invited to quote for a loyalty app for **Harlow & Finch**, a fictional London homeware chain with 60 shops. It's the biggest job they've ever seen.

Arjun quotes **£48,000** for 16 weeks. Mr Pryce, the procurement manager, replies with **£28,000**, a two-day deadline, and "our last supplier did it for less". It's a hard negotiation by design. Over five chapters it brings every classic tactic and an endless list of "just one more thing" requests.

**What keeps it easy to follow:**
- **The tactic log.** Lesson 9 of every chapter is Arjun's deal sheet: price, scope, payment terms, his walk-away and his alternatives, plus a growing list of tactics spotted and how he answered each one.
- **The same people.** Kavya is fast and suspicious. Joel is friendly and wants to say yes. Grace (Manchester; Arjun freelances for her company, Kinwell) is his calm sounding board, the ex-nurse who has seen people at their worst. Tey teaches. Mr Pryce and Ms Okafor-Lane (the digital director who wants the app) are narrated.
- **Money:** pounds, round numbers, fictional but realistic for a small agency.

## What the research says

| Finding | Source | Used in |
|---|---|---|
| "Go to the balcony": step back before you react, and don't attack, defend or give in. | Ury, *Getting Past No* (1991) | Ch1 |
| Separate the people from the problem; principled negotiation. | Fisher, Ury & Patton, *Getting to Yes* | Ch1, Ch5 |
| Extreme first offers anchor the outcome. Counter-anchor quickly, and don't negotiate from their number. | Galinsky & Mussweiler (2001) | Ch2 |
| Door-in-the-face (reciprocal concessions): a big request followed by a smaller one gets more yeses. | Cialdini et al. (1975), *JPSP*; replicated 2021 | Ch2, Ch3 |
| Good cop / bad cop works mainly when you don't notice it. | Brodt & Tuchinsky (2000), *OBHDP* | Ch2 |
| Time pressure and exploding offers push people into worse deals. | Negotiation research summaries (Harvard PON); Malhotra, *Negotiating the Impossible* (2016) | Ch2, Ch4 |
| Your power comes mostly from your alternatives (BATNA); improve it before the talk. | Fisher & Ury; Galinsky, Magee and others on power and first offers | Ch4 |
| Name emotions to calm them (affect labelling). | Lieberman et al. (2007), *Psychological Science* | Ch1 |
| Scope creep and change control are well-known causes of project failure. | Project-management practice (PMI) | Ch3 |
