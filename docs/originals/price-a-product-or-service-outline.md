## The storyline: one price card, five versions

**Decision day is over.** WashDay works: students pay, two-thirds come back, and 45% would be very disappointed without it. But Clinton guessed his price, **1,500 FCFA a bag**, and the numbers he's been avoiding are ugly:
- Mama Rose takes 1,000 FCFA a bag, leaving Clinton **500**.
- On rainy days the far hostels at the top of the hill need a moto-taxi, and that costs more than he keeps. **Some orders lose money.**
- He works about 12 hours a weekend for about 10,000 FCFA: **less than 1,000 FCFA an hour**.
- Mama Rose stops at 25 bags. Her sister, **Auntie Bih**, could wash more, but she wants 1,100 FCFA a bag.

Clinton is busy and nearly broke. The course follows him from a guessed price to a price card that pays him fairly and that customers accept.

**What keeps it easy to follow:**
- **The map shows the price card.** Chapter banners: "1,500, guessed" → "What it's worth" → "Three options" → "The price rise" → "Price card v5".
- **Clinton's price card.** Lesson 9 of every chapter shows WashDay's current price list (the course 1 evidence-board device, now for prices). Tap a line to see why it changed. Learners watch one price list grow up.
- **The same people.** **David** (the numbers friend) builds the cost sheet with him. **Miracle** mentors by voice note. **Tey** teaches. Narrated: **Mama Rose** (washes, 1,000 FCFA a bag), **Auntie Bih** (her sister, a second washer), and the **regulars** from course 1 (Brenda, Ama, Esther), whose reactions test each price.
- **The learner's job:** spot Clinton's pricing mistakes, do the sums, and price something of their own. Every Reflect asks about the learner's own product or service, including freelance work: "What do you charge per hour, and why?"

**Money:** FCFA, round numbers, always worked out in the lesson. The numbers are fictional but realistic.

## How "practical" works

There is no code. Pricing is the most numerical topic in the set, so most lessons include short calculations: unit cost, margin in francs and as a percentage, break-even bags, hourly earnings, what a 10% discount really costs, how many customers you can lose after a price rise and still earn more. The answers are typed or chosen, and the numbers are kept phone-calculator friendly.

## What the research says (the course's backbone)

| Finding | Source | Used in |
|---|---|---|
| **Small price changes have a big effect on profit.** In a classic analysis of large companies, a 1% price improvement raised operating profit by about 11% on average, more than the same 1% gain in volume or costs. | Marn & Rosiello (1992), "Managing Price, Gaining Profit", *Harvard Business Review* | Ch1 L1, Ch4 L1 |
| **Three ways to set a price:** cost-plus, competitor-based and value-based. Value-based usually captures more. | Nagle & Müller, *The Strategy and Tactics of Pricing* (standard pricing text) | Ch2 L2 |
| **Anchoring: the first number people see shapes what seems fair.** | Tversky & Kahneman (1974), *Science* | Ch2 L6 |
| **A "decoy" option can change which option people choose.** In Ariely's subscription example, adding an unattractive option shifted choices towards the expensive bundle. | Ariely, *Predictably Irrational* (2008); asymmetric dominance (Huber, Payne & Puto, 1982) | Ch3 L6 |
| **Good-better-best tiers** let different customers pay for different value. | Pricing practice (e.g. Nagle & Müller) | Ch3 L3 |
| **Raising prices when demand spikes can feel unfair** (the snow-shovel study), which affects "rainy-day surcharges". | Kahneman, Knetsch & Thaler (1986), *American Economic Review* | Ch4 L7 |
| **The "pain of paying":** how and when people pay changes how much it hurts (prepaid plans, bundles). | Prelec & Loewenstein (1998), *Marketing Science* | Ch3 L8 |
| **Prices ending in 9 can lift sales, but the effect varies.** | Anderson & Simester (2003), *Quantitative Marketing and Economics*; taught as "mixed evidence" | Ch4 L8 |
| **Asking "what would you pay?" overstates willingness to pay.** Use behaviour: real offers at real prices. | Hypothetical bias (as in course 1); Van Westendorp's price-sensitivity questions (1976) shown as a rough guide only | Ch2 L4 |
| **What a discount really costs:** with a thin margin, a small discount needs a large rise in sales just to break even. | Standard margin arithmetic | Ch4 L2 |

Every figure is checked and dated in the lesson's `notes` when it's written.

## Chapter plan

### Chapter 1 · 1,500, guessed · "Busy and broke"
*Clinton adds up what a bag really costs him. Some far-hill orders lose money, and his weekends earn under 1,000 FCFA an hour.* **Challenge:** cost a rainy-day pickup at the top of the hill.

1. The 500-franc problem (Story) · 2. What a price has to cover (Step-by-step) · 3. The cost of one bag (Card deck) · 4. Margin in francs and in percent (Before/After) · **5 Checkpoint** · 6. Your time is a cost too (Chat with Tey) · 7. Pricing myths: "cheapest wins", "cost plus a little" (Myth or Fact) · 8. Break-even: how many bags before you earn anything (Reading) · 9. Price card v1: one guessed price (Tap to explore) · **10 Challenge: the rainy-day pickup**

### Chapter 2 · What it's worth · "Price the Saturday, not the soap"
*Brenda says she'd happily pay more "to never carry that basket again". Clinton learns to price from value, using what customers already pay and give up.* **Challenge:** set a value-based price range for a standard bag.

1. "I'd pay more" (Story) · 2. Three ways to set a price: cost, competitors, value (Before/After) · 3. Finding the value: time, money, hassle saved (Step-by-step) · 4. Don't ask what they'd pay; watch what they do (Chat with Tey) · **5 Checkpoint** · 6. Anchors: Mama Rose's 2,400 and the free bucket (Reading) · 7. Value myths: "students can't pay more" (Myth or Fact) · 8. Price floor, price ceiling and the space between (Card deck) · 9. Price card v2: a range, not a guess (Tap to explore) · **10 Challenge: what is a standard bag worth?**

### Chapter 3 · Three options · "One price doesn't fit everyone"
*Esther wants it cheap and slow, the third-years want it back the same day, and the hill hostels cost more to reach. One price can't serve them all.* **Challenge:** design price card v3 with options that each make money.

1. Esther, the third-years and the hill (Story) · 2. Per item, per bag or per month? (Before/After) · 3. Good, better, best (Step-by-step) · 4. Zones and extras: pricing the hard orders (Card deck) · **5 Checkpoint** · 6. The decoy option (Reading) · 7. The Saturday Plan: a monthly subscription (Chat with Tey) · 8. Options myths: "more choice is always better" (Myth or Fact) · 9. Price card v3: three options and a zone fee (Tap to explore) · **10 Challenge: every option must earn**

### Chapter 4 · The price rise · "Discounts, deals and raising prices"
*Auntie Bih wants 1,100 FCFA a bag. Clinton has to raise prices, but his friends want discounts and the rainy season tempts him to charge more.* **Challenge:** plan and test the rise from 1,500 to 1,800 FCFA.

1. Auntie Bih's price (Story) · 2. What a discount really costs (Step-by-step) · 3. Free trials, referral rewards and slow-week deals (Card deck) · 4. Raising prices without losing regulars (Chat with Tey) · **5 Checkpoint** · 6. Testing a new price on one hostel first (Before/After) · 7. Rainy-day surcharge: fair or not? (Reading) · 8. Price psychology: 1,800 or 1,750? (Myth or Fact) · 9. Price card v4: the rise (Tap to explore) · **10 Challenge: the 1,800 test**

### Chapter 5 · Price card v5 · "Say it, defend it, review it"
*The new prices are live. Now Clinton has to explain them clearly, handle "that's too expensive", agree a fair rate with his washers, and check the results.* **Final challenge:** publish price card v5 and show it pays.

1. "That's too expensive" (Story) · 2. A price list anyone understands, with no hidden fees (Step-by-step) · 3. Answering price objections with value, not discounts (Chat with Tey) · 4. Pricing what you pay: agreeing rates with suppliers (Before/After) · **5 Checkpoint** · 6. Did it work? Margin, customers kept, hours earned (Reading) · 7. Review your prices regularly (Myth or Fact) · 8. Pricing your own time: freelancers and side-hustles (Card deck) · 9. Price card v5 (Tap to explore) · **10 Final challenge: the price card that pays**

Each chapter uses all eight Learn formats exactly once in lessons 1–4 and 6–9, and lesson 9 is always the price card.

## Hooks

- **From course 1:** it opens on the 500 FCFA a bag that decision day flagged as "doubtful".
- **Into course 3:** at the end, WashDay earns a fair margin, but David's notebook shows the money arrives on Sundays, while Auntie Bih wants paying on Saturdays and the bicycle needs a new chain. "We're profitable. So why is there never any money?" That opens Profit, Revenue and Cash Flow.
