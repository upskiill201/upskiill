## The storyline: Lucía's First 90 Days

**Lucía** (Mexico City, mid-career marketer) has just been promoted to lead the marketing team at **Nopal**, a Mexico City snack brand. That's the team she sat in until yesterday. The map counts down her first 90 days: **Day 1 → Week 3 → Day 45 → Day 70 → Day 90**. At the end is the summer campaign launch, and her 90-day review with her boss.

**The team (course-only cast):**
- **Marta:** her desk-mate and best work friend for six years. She applied for the same job. Dependable, says yes to everything, quietly overloaded.
- **Diego:** 12 years at Nopal, funny, well liked, used to being the unofficial boss. Not a villain.
- **Sofía:** the newest hire. Brilliant and quiet.
- **Tomás:** junior. Eager, fast, and makes mistakes fast too.
- **Rafa:** designer in Guadalajara, remote (narrated).
- **Carmen:** marketing director, Lucía's boss. Direct and busy.

Each chapter ends with a real moment Lucía has to lead through. The learner sees her get it wrong, then right, and builds the same habits.

## The research backbone

| Idea | Source | Where |
|---|---|---|
| New managers' myths (authority, control, compliance vs commitment) | Linda Hill, "Becoming the Boss", HBR (2007); *Becoming a Manager* | Ch1 L2–L3 |
| Managers account for at least 70% of the variance in team engagement; about one in two people has left a job to get away from a manager | Gallup, *State of the American Manager* (Harter & Adkins) | Ch1 L3 |
| What the best managers do (coach, empower without micromanaging, include, communicate, develop careers, set a vision…) | Google Project Oxygen (8 behaviours, 10 from 2018) | Ch1 L8 |
| One-on-ones: weekly 30 min works best; the employee should drive the agenda; a manager who talks too much is the clearest warning sign | Steven Rogelberg, *Glad We Met* | Ch2 L2–L3 |
| Psychological safety: the top team dynamic in Project Aristotle; defined by Edmondson (1999) | Edmondson (1999), *Administrative Science Quarterly*; Google re:Work | Ch2 L4, L7 |
| Specific, challenging goals beat "do your best" | Locke & Latham goal-setting research (1990, 2002) | Ch3 L2 |
| Structure and clarity as a team dynamic | Project Aristotle | Ch3 L2–L3 |
| Feedback: Situation–Behaviour–Impact (+ Intent) | Center for Creative Leadership | Ch4 L2 |
| Recognition: well-recognised employees were 45% less likely to have left two years later | Gallup & Workhuman (2022–2024) | Ch4 L3 |
| Moods spread through teams ("the ripple effect") | Barsade (2002), *Administrative Science Quarterly* | Ch5 L2 |
| Early wins; the "breakeven point" in a new role | Michael Watkins, *The First 90 Days* | Ch1 L6, Ch5 |
