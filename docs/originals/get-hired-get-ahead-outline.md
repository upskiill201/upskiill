## Why this track

Getting hired is the most urgent thing many of our learners want, and the second most urgent is getting ahead once they're in. Most career advice online is generic lists ("10 CV tips") written for one country. This track is different in three ways:

1. **One person's real job hunt, start to finish.** Learners follow Clinton from "I have no experience" in his final year to his first promotion conversation, so every skill shows up when it's needed.
2. **You build your own career kit as you go.** Each chapter's challenge adds a real piece to the learner's kit: a skills list, a portfolio piece, a CV, a cover letter, a LinkedIn profile, interview stories, a negotiation script. By the end of Level 1 they have everything needed to apply.
3. **The hiring side of the table.** Miracle now hires people at Afiya. Every chapter has a "Miracle's desk" moment: what a hiring manager actually notices, skips and remembers.

## The story

### Level 1: Clinton Gets Hired

**Clinton** *(shared cast)* is now 21, in the final year of Computer Science at the **University of Buea**, Cameroon. He is still loud, funny and brilliant at organising people: he runs the department WhatsApp group, the football team's fixtures and a campus tech meet-up of 40 people. He's also still tempted by shortcuts.

**The hook:** the university careers fair. Clinton walks up to the stand of **Njangi Pay**, a fast-growing fintech in Douala (a *njangi* is a Cameroonian rotating savings group). The recruiter asks, "So what can you do?" Clinton says, "Well, I don't have any experience yet..." and watches her eyes glaze over. Every one of his friends seems to have an internship lined up. He has eight months until graduation.

- **Miracle** *(shared cast)*, his old mentor, is now a team lead at **Afiya** in Lagos, and she hires people. She agrees to coach him on video calls on one condition: "No lies on anything you send. I can smell them." She is the voice of the hiring side.
- **David** *(shared cast)*, his best friend, is in medical school and has the opposite problem: perfect grades, freezes in interviews. He's Clinton's practice partner.
- **Kofi** *(shared cast)*, now at university in Accra, appears as the friend who built a website from nothing (HTML & CSS for Beginners): proof that a portfolio doesn't need a job.
- **Mrs Ewane** *(new, course-only)*, Head of Careers at the university. Precise, kind and tired of CVs written in Comic Sans. She runs the careers office quizzes (Checkpoints).
- **Brenda Fon** *(new, course-only)*, the Njangi Pay recruiter from the careers fair. Busy, fair, and the person Clinton keeps trying to impress. She reappears at the interview and the offer.

**Map markers count down to graduation:** "8 months to grad" in Chapter 1, down to "Graduation" at the offer.

**The arc:** Clinton discovers the skills he already has (Ch1), builds proof (Ch2), writes a CV and cover letter that get read (Ch3–4), makes himself findable (Ch5), finds the job that was never advertised (Ch6), lands a three-month internship at a Douala start-up (Ch7), interviews for Njangi Pay's graduate role (Ch8), gets rejected by his dream company and recovers (Ch9), and negotiates the Njangi Pay offer (Ch10).

### Level 2: Clinton's First Year

Clinton starts as a **junior developer at Njangi Pay**, Douala.

- **Ngozi Bate** *(new, course-only)*, his engineering manager. Direct, generous with praise when it's earned, allergic to surprises.
- **Paul Tabi** *(new, course-only)*, a senior developer who knows everything and explains nothing, until Clinton earns it.
- Miracle stays his mentor, now as a peer as much as a coach.

**Markers:** "Week 1", "Month 3", "Month 6", "Month 9", "One year in".

**The arc:** survives his first 90 days without breaking production (Ch1), learns what "valuable" means at work (Ch2), asks for a promotion and doesn't get it the first time (Ch3), builds a real career plan (Ch4), and discovers he loves the product side more than the code. He makes a strategic switch to associate product manager (Ch5). The level ends with him on the hiring side for the first time, interviewing an intern.

*Cast timeline check:* Clinton was 17 (Study Smarter) and 18 in first year (Tey FM). A Cameroonian bachelor's takes three years, so final year at 21 fits. Miracle leads a team at Afiya by then (How to Motivate a Team).

## What makes it engaging (designed in, not added later)

1. **Your career kit grows every chapter.** Each Chapter Challenge produces a real piece the learner keeps, in their own words: skills inventory, portfolio idea, CV bullet points, cover letter opening, LinkedIn headline, interview story bank, a negotiation script, a 90-day plan, a promotion case. A "Kit so far" moment ends every chapter.
2. **Miracle's desk.** A recurring lesson beat: Miracle shares her screen while she reviews real-looking (invented) CVs, cover letters and profiles. The learner sees in seconds what she skips and what makes her stop.
3. **Fix-it rounds.** Learners repair weak CV bullets, vague cover-letter openings and rambling interview answers line by line (Find the bug on prose, Put in order, Fill in the blanks). It's a lot more fun than reading tips.
4. **Hard choices that matter.** Each chapter has a Story choice, and the hardest ones are about honesty: padding a CV, a cover letter AI wrote in full, a fake competing offer, badmouthing an old boss.
5. **Practice out loud.** Interview chapters use Chat reply exercises as mock interviews: Brenda asks, the learner picks or builds an answer, and Tey explains what the interviewer heard.
6. **Global by design.** Every chapter has a "Where you live" note: CV vs résumé, photos on CVs, how direct salary talks are, notice periods. The rule is to show the range and tell the learner to check local norms (playbook §9).

### Chapter stories (one line each)

**Level 1**
1. **What you've already got.** The careers fair: "I don't have any experience." Miracle: "You run a 40-person meet-up. That's experience." *Climax:* Clinton's skills inventory, each skill with proof.
2. **Proof without experience.** Clinton builds a small savings-group tracker for his mum's njangi and writes it up. *Choice:* copy a tutorial project and call it his own, or build something real and smaller.
3. **The CV that gets read.** Miracle's desk: she gives his CV 7 seconds. *Climax:* a one-page CV with bullets that say what changed. *Choice:* "fluent in French" (he isn't).
4. **The cover letter.** His first letter starts "I am writing to apply..." and says nothing. *Choice:* let AI write the whole thing, or use it to sharpen his own draft.
5. **Be findable.** His LinkedIn photo is from a party. *Climax:* Brenda Fon views his profile.
6. **The hidden job market.** The best roles never get posted. Clinton messages people he's never met, and most ignore him. One doesn't. *Choice:* spam 200 people with the same message, or write 10 real ones.
7. **A foot in the door.** He wins a three-month internship at a Douala start-up, then has to stand out among five interns. *Choice:* ask Miracle to "just get him in" at Afiya.
8. **The interview.** Njangi Pay's graduate interview. David plays the interviewer; Clinton freezes on "Tell me about yourself". *Climax:* the real interview with Brenda.
9. **When it's a no.** His dream company says no, by email, on his birthday. *Choice:* reply angrily, ghost them, or ask for feedback.
10. **The offer.** Njangi Pay offers. The salary is lower than he hoped. *Choice:* invent a competing offer, accept on the spot, or negotiate honestly. *Climax:* "You're hired."

**Level 2**
1. **The first 90 days.** He pushes a bug to production in week 2. *Choice:* hide it or tell Ngozi.
2. **Worth more.** Busy isn't valuable. He starts a "wins log" and finds the problem nobody owns.
3. **The promotion conversation.** He asks too early and hears "not yet". Then he builds a case with Ngozi and comes back three months later.
4. **Your plan.** Paul asks, "Where do you want to be in three years?" Clinton has no idea, so he builds a one-page plan.
5. **Changing lanes.** He's drawn to product. *Choice:* quit to "go into product" with no plan, or test it inside Njangi Pay first. *Finale:* he interviews his first intern, from the other side of the table.

## Facts (checked 2026-10-20; sources go in each lesson's author notes)

- **Employers look for problem-solving and teamwork first.** In NACE's *Job Outlook 2025*, about 90% of US employers look for problem-solving evidence on graduate résumés and roughly 80% look for teamwork. More than three-quarters want communication; initiative, work ethic and technical skills each matter to over 70%. Nearly two-thirds use skills-based hiring. (NACE, 2025. A US survey, so say so.)
- **Analytical thinking is the top core skill worldwide.** In the WEF *Future of Jobs Report 2025* (over 1,000 employers, 55 economies), about 7 in 10 employers rate analytical thinking essential. Next come resilience, flexibility and agility, then leadership and social influence, creative thinking, and motivation and self-awareness. (WEF, January 2025.)
- **Recruiters' first look at a CV takes seconds.** In Ladders' 2018 eye-tracking study, the initial CV screen averaged **7.4 seconds** (about 6 seconds in 2012). Simple layouts with clear headings held attention; cluttered, multi-column layouts did worse. (Ladders, 2018. It's a small, industry study: say "one study found", and frame it as the first skim, not the whole decision.)
- **Weak ties lead to new jobs.** In *A Causal Test of the Strength of Weak Ties* (Rajkumar et al., *Science*, 2022), LinkedIn experiments covering over 20 million people over five years found that moderately weak ties (people you know a little) led to more job moves than close friends. The effect was strongest in digital industries. The idea goes back to sociologist Mark Granovetter, *The Strength of Weak Ties* (1973).
- **The first 90 days.** The idea is from Michael D. Watkins, *The First 90 Days* (Harvard Business School Press, 2003).
- **No invented statistics.** Common internet claims such as "70–80% of jobs are never advertised" have weak or untraceable sources, so we will not use them. Say "many roles are filled through referrals and networks before they're posted" unless a checked source is found.

## Global notes (playbook §9)

- **CV vs résumé:** CV in most of Africa, the UK, Europe and Asia; résumé in the US and Canada (and an academic "CV" there is a long document). The course uses "CV" and explains the difference once.
- **Photos, age, marital status:** common on CVs in parts of Africa, Europe and Asia, discouraged or avoided in the UK, US and Canada. The course explains the trade-off and tells learners to follow local norms.
- **Salary talks:** how direct you can be varies by country and company. The negotiation chapter teaches principles (research, a range, politeness, total package), not one script.
- **No legal or financial advice.** Contracts, tax and employment law: general awareness only, and "check locally".
