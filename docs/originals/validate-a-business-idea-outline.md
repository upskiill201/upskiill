## The storyline: one person, one idea, five weeks

**Clinton** (cast: Bamenda-born, now a first-year Computer Science student at the University of Buea) wants to start a business this semester. He's loud, funny, runs every group chat and is brilliant at organising people. He's also tempted by shortcuts, but he keeps his drafts, and that saves him again.

**His first idea: CampusEats**, a food-delivery app for students. He posts it in five group chats. Forty fire reactions. "Bro this is genius." He's ready to spend his savings on a developer.

**Miracle** (his old online mentor, now working at a startup in Lagos) sends one voice note: "Forty people said it's genius. How many of them would pay you tomorrow?" Clinton doesn't know. The course starts there.

**What happens:** Clinton learns to talk to customers properly, and his interviews uncover something nobody mentioned in the group chats. In Buea's rainy season clothes won't dry, hostel students lose every Saturday morning to hand-washing, and some already pay a woman at the campus gate to do it. Food delivery was his idea. Laundry is their problem. He tests a laundry pickup service, **WashDay**, by hand, with a WhatsApp group and a borrowed bicycle, before building anything.

**What keeps it easy to follow:**
- **The map counts the evidence.** Chapter banners: "0 customers asked" → "15 interviews" → "Market sized" → "12 paid pre-orders" → "Decision day".
- **Clinton's evidence board.** Lesson 9 of every chapter shows his board: each assumption marked *untested*, *supported* or *killed*, with the evidence. Tap a card to see why it changed. Learners watch an idea turn into evidence.
- **Two supporting people, both familiar.** **David** (his friend from Study Smarter, now a Medicine student in Buea, the best at maths in the group) is the sceptic who asks "how many?". **Miracle** mentors by voice note. **Tey** teaches. One narrated extra, **Mama Rose**, runs the wash-and-iron stand at the campus gate: Clinton's competitor, and later possibly his partner.
- **The learner's job:** spot what Clinton is getting wrong, fix it, and then apply each step to an idea of their own. Every Reflect asks about the learner's own idea.

**Cast note (lives move forward):** this is after How to Make People Listen to You, where Clinton and David are first-year students in Buea and Miracle has graduated. Miracle has chosen the Lagos startup (Afiya), so she mentors from Lagos. Nothing here contradicts How to Motivate a Team, which happens 18 months later.

**Money:** amounts are in FCFA (Central African CFA franc). The numbers are made up but realistic, and always worked out in the lesson.

## How "practical" works

This course has no code. Practice comes from exercises built on Clinton's real situation:
- spot the bad interview question and rewrite it;
- sort messages into compliments, opinions and commitments;
- size a market from simple counts (students × washes per month × price) and type the answer;
- set a test's success line before seeing the results, then judge the results;
- write the one-page validation summary.

The calculations are short and use round numbers, so learners can do them in their head or with a phone calculator.

## What the research says (the course's backbone)

| Finding | Source | Used in |
|---|---|---|
| **"No market need" is the most common reason startups say they failed.** | CB Insights startup post-mortem analyses (2014: 42% cited it; later updates still rank it at or near the top). Exact figures checked and dated in lesson notes. | Ch1 L2 |
| **Ask about past behaviour, not opinions about the future.** Compliments and hypotheticals mislead; specifics about what people already did and paid don't. | Rob Fitzpatrick, *The Mom Test* (2013) | Ch2 L2–L4 |
| **"Get out of the building."** Business ideas are untested hypotheses until customers confirm them. | Steve Blank, *The Four Steps to the Epiphany* (2005); customer development | Ch1 L3–L4 |
| **Minimum viable product, validated learning, build-measure-learn, pivot.** | Eric Ries, *The Lean Startup* (2011) | Ch4, Ch5 L4 |
| **People overstate what they'd pay when it's hypothetical.** | Hypothetical bias in willingness to pay; meta-analysis by Murphy et al. (2005), *Environmental and Resource Economics* | Ch1 L6, Ch4 L6 |
| **We look for evidence that agrees with us.** | Confirmation bias: Nickerson (1998), *Review of General Psychology* | Ch1 L8 |
| **Customers "hire" a product to get a job done; the real competitor is often doing nothing or a workaround.** | Christensen et al., *Competing Against Luck* (2016), jobs to be done | Ch3 L6 |
| **Bottom-up market sizing is more believable than top-down.** TAM / SAM / SOM. | Standard market-sizing practice | Ch3 L2–L4 |
| **Cheap tests before building:** fake door / smoke test, concierge MVP, pre-sales. | Lean startup practice; e.g. the Dropbox demo video and Zappos photographing shoes in local shops, as told in *The Lean Startup* and elsewhere | Ch4 L3, L7 |
| **Decide the pass mark before running a test.** | Standard experiment design (pre-registration logic) | Ch4 L4 |
| **Sunk costs pull us to keep going.** | Arkes & Blumer (1985), *Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes* | Ch5 L6 |
| **The "very disappointed" test:** if 40% or more of users would be very disappointed without the product, it has strong pull. | Sean Ellis, product-market fit survey | Ch5 L7 |
| **What a value proposition is:** the customer's job, pains and gains, matched to what you offer. | Osterwalder et al., *Value Proposition Design* (2014) | Ch3 L8 |

## Chapter plan

### Chapter 1 · 0 customers asked · "Everyone loves my idea"
*Forty fire emojis and zero evidence. Clinton learns that an idea is a pile of guesses, and that the guess that could kill it comes first.* **Challenge:** list CampusEats's assumptions and pick the riskiest one to test.

1. Forty fire emojis (Story) · 2. Why most new businesses fail (Myth or Fact) · 3. Problem first, solution second (Step-by-step) · 4. Your idea is a list of guesses (Card deck) · **5 Checkpoint** · 6. Compliments vs commitments (Before/After) · 7. The riskiest assumption (Chat with Tey) · 8. Why we only hear what we want (Reading) · 9. Evidence board v1: all untested (Tap to explore) · **10 Challenge: the riskiest guess**

### Chapter 2 · 15 interviews · "Ask about yesterday, not tomorrow"
*Clinton interviews students and learns not to pitch. Halfway through, the conversations keep drifting to wet laundry.* **Challenge:** read five interview notes and find the real problem.

1. Fifteen conversations (Story) · 2. Good questions vs bad questions (Before/After) · 3. The Mom Test rules (Step-by-step) · 4. Don't pitch, listen (Chat with Tey) · **5 Checkpoint** · 6. What they already spend: time, money, workarounds (Reading) · 7. Friends, family and the group chat (Myth or Fact) · 8. Notes that keep the truth (Card deck) · 9. Evidence board v2: the laundry surprise (Tap to explore) · **10 Challenge: find the real problem**

### Chapter 3 · Market sized · "How many, and who else?"
*David asks the question Clinton has avoided: how many students would actually pay, and how often? Then they visit Mama Rose at the gate.* **Challenge:** size the WashDay market bottom-up and map the competition.

1. David's question (Story) · 2. Top-down vs bottom-up sizing (Before/After) · 3. Sizing in four steps (Step-by-step) · 4. Total, reachable, winnable (TAM, SAM, SOM) (Card deck) · **5 Checkpoint** · 6. Your real competitors, including "doing nothing" (Reading) · 7. Visiting Mama Rose (Chat with Tey) · 8. Why would anyone switch? (Myth or Fact) · 9. Evidence board v3: the numbers (Tap to explore) · **10 Challenge: size it, map it**

### Chapter 4 · 12 paid pre-orders · "Test before you build"
*Clinton wants to hire a developer for a laundry app. Miracle says: "Do it by hand first." A WhatsApp group, a borrowed bicycle and one weekend.* **Challenge:** judge the weekend's results against the pass mark set beforehand.

1. "I need an app" (Story) · 2. What a minimum viable product is, and isn't (Myth or Fact) · 3. Four cheap tests: fake door, concierge, pre-sale, landing page (Card deck) · 4. Set the pass mark first (Step-by-step) · **5 Checkpoint** · 6. Sign-ups vs payments (Before/After) · 7. Doing it by hand (Chat with Tey) · 8. Reading results honestly (Reading) · 9. Evidence board v4: the weekend test (Tap to explore) · **10 Challenge: pass or fail?**

### Chapter 5 · Decision day · "Go, change or stop"
*Ten customers, two weeks of pickups, some coming back and some not. Clinton has to decide, with evidence, and without falling for what he's already spent.* **Final challenge:** write the one-page validation summary and make the call.

1. The first ten customers (Story) · 2. Finding customers 11 to 30 (Step-by-step) · 3. Do they come back? (Chat with Tey) · 4. Persevere, pivot or stop (Before/After) · **5 Checkpoint** · 6. Sunk costs (Myth or Fact) · 7. The "very disappointed" test (Card deck) · 8. The one-page validation summary (Reading) · 9. Evidence board v5: decision day (Tap to explore) · **10 Final challenge: Go?**

Each chapter uses all eight Learn formats exactly once in lessons 1–4 and 6–9, and lesson 9 is always the evidence board.
