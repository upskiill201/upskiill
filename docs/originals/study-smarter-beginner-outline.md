## The story

**Kofi Mensah, 17, Accra.** It's the start of his final year of secondary school. He wants to study Biomedical Engineering at university, and that needs good grades in Chemistry, Physics and Maths. Chemistry is his weak subject, and his final exams are coming.

Kofi has just got an AI study partner. Over the year he learns to make it explain anything, quiz him, plan his revision and check his writing. He also learns where the line is. Halfway through, he's tempted to let it write his essay, and the course is about the choice he makes.

**Miracle** (22, Buea, Cameroon) is a final-year Software Engineering student at the University of Buea. She's Kofi's mentor through an online scheme that pairs university students with final-year school students. She mentors a small group on a weekly video call and a group chat:

- **Kofi** in Accra.
- **Clinton** and **David**, two final-year students at the same school in Buea.

Miracle is a year past the stress the three of them are going through. Her own final-year project and job hunt run in the background, so university students see themselves in her.

The group gives the story two countries and four ways of studying. It also gives Kofi friends who aren't in his class: they push him, copy his tricks and catch his mistakes.

The story moves forward as a **countdown to exams**. Each unit is a chapter, and its banner on the map shows how long is left ("30 weeks to go"). The final chapter ends on results day and a university offer. The Intermediate level continues the story at university.

## What it teaches (based on research)

The course teaches study habits that the research strongly supports, with AI as the tool that makes them easy:

- **Retrieval practice:** testing yourself beats rereading.
- **Spacing:** little and often beats cramming.
- **Interleaving:** mixing topics during practice.
- **Explaining in your own words:** the explain-it-back check.

It also busts popular myths: learning styles, highlighting as studying, and rereading as revision.

Academic honesty runs through the whole course, not just one chapter: you think first, AI second. Lessons never claim that AI detectors are reliable (they aren't), and never teach anyone how to get around a school's rules.

## Chapter 1 — First day back (written first; includes the free lessons)

Miracle shows him that the problem is the question, not the tool. **Chapter climax (lesson 20):** Mrs Asante's surprise quiz arrives a week early. ## Facts to handle carefully (playbook §9)

- **School and exam details stay general.** Kofi takes his "final exams". There are no real exam dates, and no claims about any exam board's rules.
- **Learning research:** cite the named effects (retrieval practice, spacing). Never use made-up percentages.
- **AI detectors:** say they "can flag honest work and miss AI writing". There are no accuracy numbers.
- **Age rules:** "check the tool's age rules; many need you to be 13 or older, and some need a parent's OK". Never name exact ages for a specific product.
- **Wellbeing (Ch. 11):** AI is never presented as a counsellor. Point to a trusted adult or a local helpline.
